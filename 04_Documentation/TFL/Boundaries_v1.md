# Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) Boundaries

The Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) is a **local, mechanistic framework** describing short-timescale integration of sensory evidence, memory retrieval, affective modulation, and predictive simulation. The following boundaries define the limits of its claims and prevent overextension beyond its intended scope.

## 1. Scope of Applicability

TFL applies **only** to moment-level integration processes operating over brief temporal windows. It does **not** describe:

- Long-term memory consolidation
- Identity formation
- Narrative construction
- Developmental trajectories
- Group-level or social-level dynamics

These require separate frameworks.

## 2. Not a Neural Implementation

TFL does **not** specify:

- Exact neural circuits
- Anatomical substrates
- Neurotransmitter systems
- Laminar pathways
- Oscillatory signatures

Any neural references in the literature are **illustrative**, not commitments.

## 3. Not a Theory of Consciousness

TFL does **not** claim:

- What consciousness *is*
- How subjective experience arises
- Why awareness exists
- Metaphysical or ontological explanations

It models **functional integration**, not phenomenology.

## 4. Not a Complete Cognitive Architecture

TFL is **one module** within a larger cognitive ecosystem. It does not replace or subsume:

- Decision-making frameworks
- Attentional control systems
- Emotional regulation models
- Long-range predictive architectures
- Narrative or identity frameworks

It interacts with these systems but does not define them.

## 5. Not a Behavioral or Clinical Model

TFL does **not** provide:

- Diagnostic criteria
- Therapeutic mechanisms
- Behavioral predictions outside its defined window
- Explanations for psychopathology

Any resemblance to clinical phenomena is **conceptual**, not prescriptive.

## 6. Assumption-Bound Validity

TFL assumes:

- Discrete temporal windows
- Identifiable simulation content
- Measurable affective modulation
- Stable sensory and contextual inputs

If these assumptions fail, such as under chaotic input, absent simulation content, or extreme physiological states, TFL's predictions may not hold.

## 7. No Claims of Agency or Intentionality

TFL does **not** attribute:

- Agency
- Volition
- Goals
- Selfhood

To any internal process. Simulations are treated as **computational hypotheses**, not intentions.

## 8. No Inference Beyond Declared Variables

TFL's guarantees apply **only** to:

- Sensory evidence
- Retrieved memory content
- Affective state
- Predictive simulation
- Integrated conscious state

It does **not** support inference about unmodeled variables or external constructs.

## 9. No Claims of Exclusivity

TFL does **not** claim to be:

- The only valid model of temporal integration
- A universal explanation for spontaneous thought
- A replacement for predictive coding, active inference, or other frameworks

It is a **specific, falsifiable module** within a broader architecture.
