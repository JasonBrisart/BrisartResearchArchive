# Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) Overview

The Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) models conscious experience as a recursive, affectively modulated integration of sensory input, memory reactivation, and predictive simulation within brief temporal windows.

Rather than treating cognition as a linear stream, TFL formalizes a loop architecture in which internally generated simulations can persist, escalate, or resolve as a function of feedback, belief structure, and affective weighting.

> Central to TFL is the hypothesis that affectively salient thoughts encode predictive simulations that remain active within the loop until incoming feedback affirms or contradicts their simulated content.

In its Institutional Edition, TFL is positioned as an implementation-ready substrate for experimental design, computational modeling, and clinical hypothesis generation, with clearly specified variables, phases, and resolution conditions.
