# Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL)

## Definitions (v2 Draft)

### 1. `Input(t)`

**Definition:**

Current sensory or internal perceptual input available at time `t`.

**Notes:**

- Includes exteroceptive and interoceptive information.
- Serves as the present-state input to the loop.
- `Input(t)` is interpreted recursively rather than passively received.

### 2. `Minfo(t)`

**Definition:**

Memory information retrieved at time `t`.

**Notes:**

- Retrieved through hippocampal replay / sharp-wave ripple-associated processes.
- Context-sensitive rather than archival.
- Emotionally filtered and reconstructed in relation to current loop state.

### 3. `E(t)`

**Definition:**

Emotional-affective modulation at time `t`.

**Notes:**

- Regulates salience, memory access, perceptual weighting, and simulation persistence.
- Functions as the loop's primary modulation variable.
- High `E(t)` increases recurrence, narrowing, rigidity, and simulation reentry.

### 4. `F`

**Definition:**

Recursive integration function operating on `Input(t)`, `Minfo(t)`, and `E(t)`.

**Notes:**

- Produces predictive, evaluative, and behavioral output.
- Determines how current input is integrated with memory and emotional state.
- Output of `F` influences future loop state and subsequent input weighting.

### 5. `Output(t)`

**Definition:**

The behavioral, attentional, or interpretive result generated at time `t`.

**Notes:**

- Output may be internal or external.
- Influences subsequent `Input(t+1)`.
- Carries the loop forward across frames.

### 6. Elementary Temporal Frame

**Definition:**

A discrete but overlapping temporal processing window of approximately 100 to 300 milliseconds.

**Notes:**

- Serves as the unit of recursive loop operation.
- Conscious continuity is produced through stitching and reintegration of these frames.

### 7. Recursive Feedback Integration

**Definition:**

The reentry of loop output into subsequent processing cycles.

**Notes:**

- Later stages can alter the interpretation of earlier events.
- Supports postdiction, reinterpretation, and recursive correction.

### 8. Persistence

**Definition:**

The continued activation or reinfluence of a simulation across multiple loop frames.

**Notes:**

- Driven primarily by `E(t)`.
- May remain active without immediate behavioral expression.
- Can persist despite ambiguous or contradictory feedback.

### 9. Loop Drift

**Definition:**

A state in which feedback neither confirms nor contradicts an active simulation strongly enough to stabilize or extinguish it.

**Notes:**

- Produces weakening, diffusion, or instability in narrative structure.
- May lead to incoherent or erratic interpretation.

### 10. Loop Inversion

**Definition:**

A structural reversal of a persistent simulation under repeated disconfirmation.

**Notes:**

- A role or narrative may re-enter in inverse form.
- Functions as a recursive correction rather than simple suppression.

### 11. Residual Loop Echo

**Definition:**

A reduced but persistent simulation trace that can re-enter later under matching contextual or emotional conditions.

**Notes:**

- Explains spontaneous reappearance of previously weakened loops.
- Depends on latent loop persistence and contextual reactivation.

### 12. Role Encoding

**Definition:**

The implicit role structure embedded within simulations.

**Notes:**

- Simulations do not only encode events, but roles such as agent, observer, victim, leader, or outsider.
- Repeated activation of role-consistent simulations contributes to identity stabilization.

### 13. Role Saturation

**Definition:**

Condition in which one role pattern dominates the loop excessively.

**Notes:**

- Produces rigidity, overgeneralization, and reduced adaptive flexibility.
- Associated with elevated emotional persistence and narrowed attention.

### 14. Identity Stabilization

**Definition:**

The emergence of a recurrent, emotionally coherent role pattern across time.

**Notes:**

- Identity is not static but loop-stabilized.
- Forms through repeated simulation, confirmation, and emotional coherence.
