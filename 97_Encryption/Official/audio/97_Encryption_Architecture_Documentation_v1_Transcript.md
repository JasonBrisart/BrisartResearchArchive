## About This Record

This transcript accompanies the **97_Encryption_Architecture_Documentation_v1** audio record preserved within the Brisart Research Archive.

The recording provides a technical walkthrough of the architecture represented by the `97_Encryption` research project, with particular emphasis on the BSR2 alpha 4 cryptographic design and the security problems that influenced its development.

The discussion examines the project's entropy and deterministic random-bit generation mechanisms, entropy-source health checks, process-fork protections, and the rollback and keystream-reuse concerns associated with the earlier BSR2 alpha 3 architecture.

It further documents the BSR2 alpha 4 approach to synthetic initialization vectors and nonce-misuse resistance, the BSR2-P1024 permutation, BSR2-S64 sponge construction, Sealed Message V3, key commitment, Padmé padding, the BSR-Stream 1 protocol, BSR2-MHF1 password derivation, Unicode normalization, recovery records, interleaved parity, and secure archive extraction.

The recording also discusses the relationship between these mechanisms and the project's broader defensive design philosophy, including the use of overlapping integrity, authentication, recovery, and input-validation mechanisms.
