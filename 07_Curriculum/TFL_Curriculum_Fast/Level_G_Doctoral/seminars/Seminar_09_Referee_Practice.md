# Level G — Seminar 9: Referee Practice

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants produce referee-quality review of work on an unvalidated framework, and calibrate against one another.

---

## 1. Frame — The Referee's Specific Problem (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Reviewing work on an unvalidated framework has a failure mode in both directions. Reject because the framework isn't established — that's reviewing the framework's reputation, not the manuscript. Accept because the work is careful — that's letting rigor substitute for relevance. Your job is neither."

Write the standard:

    REVIEW THE INFERENCE, NOT THE FRAMEWORK.

    Ask: does this manuscript support what it claims?
    Not: is TFL true?

---

## 2. Build — The Review Structure (45 min)

    SUMMARY
      The manuscript's claim, in the reviewer's words.
      If you cannot write this, that is finding one.

    SIGNIFICANCE
      If the result holds, what question does it move?
      Is that question open?

    MEASUREMENT
      Construct validity of each variable.
      Prespecification evidence.
      Resolution matched to claim.

    ADJUDICATION
      Are rivals stated fairly?
      Is the discriminating condition entered?
      Is the rival's operationalization one its proponents would accept?

    INFERENCE
      Highest rung the design licenses.
      Highest rung the manuscript claims.
      Gap between them.

    DISCLOSURE
      Is the framework's status represented accurately?
      Overclaimed, underclaimed, or accurate?

    VERDICT AND REQUIRED REVISIONS

**Say:**
> "The disclosure section is specific to this literature and you will not find it in a standard review template. Given the framework's status, a manuscript that presents it as established has a problem no amount of methodological quality fixes."

---

## 3. Apply — Blind Review (70 min)

Each participant reviews two manuscripts — either anonymized proposals from the seminar or instructor-supplied specimens.

Written, to the structure above, in session. No discussion during.

---

## 4. Contest — Calibration (35 min)

Two reviews of the same manuscript read aloud anonymously, selected for divergence.

**Ask the room:**
- Where do they disagree on fact versus on standard?
- Which reviewer reviewed the framework rather than the manuscript?
- Which verdict would you defend to an editor?

**Say:**
> "Reviewer disagreement is normal. Reviewer disagreement about whether the framework's existence is the problem is not — that's a reviewer error and editors can see it."

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Final meeting: you defend. Your Seminar 1 standing objection comes back with you."

---

## Due

**Referee Report** submitted at end of session.

## Instructor Notes

- Instructor-supplied specimens work better than peer proposals for the first review because participants review strangers more honestly.
- The "reviewed the framework rather than the manuscript" diagnosis is the transferable skill here and generalizes well beyond TFL.
