# Band A — Day 3: Expecting

**Grades:** K-2
**Duration:** 20-25 minutes
**Materials:** Familiar picture book, chart paper

---

## Objective

Students will state predictions out loud before an outcome is revealed.

## Success Looks Like

Each student commits to a prediction before finding out, and can say why.

---

## 1. Hook — Check Yesterday's Prediction (3 min)

If a student made a prediction on Day 2, check it now.

**Say:**
> "Yesterday [student] expected [prediction]. Let's find out."

Reveal the outcome. Whatever happened:

**Say:**
> "Good prediction. Not because it was right — because we found out."

This models the norm: prediction quality is about committing, not about being correct.

If no prediction was made on Day 2, use the hook below instead.

**Alternative hook:** Hold up a closed book. "What do you think is on page one?"

---

## 2. Name — Expecting (5 min)

Write **EXPECT** on the chart, completing the trio.

**Say:**
> "Expecting is what you think happens next. Today we're going to say them out loud before we find out. That's the rule."

Point to the chart.

**Say:**
> "Notice. Remember. Expect. That's our three words."

---

## 3. Work — The Story Stop (12 min)

Read a familiar picture book aloud. Stop at four or five points.

At each stop:
1. Ask: **"What happens next?"**
2. Take three or four predictions.
3. Ask one student: **"What made you think that?"**
4. Continue reading.

**The critical move is step 3.** Every prediction traces back to remembering. Make that visible each time.

**Expected student reasoning:**
- "Because last time he was scared."
- "Because the picture shows a door."
- "Because that always happens in these books."

All three are memory-driven. Say so:
> "You remembered something, and that gave you the guess."

---

## 4. Compare — Different Guesses, Same Story (4 min)

**Say:**
> "Did everybody guess the same thing?"

No.

**Ask:**
> "We all heard the exact same words. Why were the guesses different?"

Let students work on it. Steer toward:
> "Because you each remembered different things."

---

## 5. Carry — One Sentence Forward (2 min)

**Say:**
> "You're getting good at guessing. Next time, I'm going to break one on purpose."

Leave it there. The anticipation is useful.

---

## Instructor Notes

- Choose a book students know well. Familiarity makes predictions confident, which makes Day 4's break land harder.
- If a student predicts something wildly off-story, don't correct it. Ask what made them think that. Sometimes the reasoning is excellent.
- Never let a correct prediction receive more praise than an incorrect one. Watch your own tone here — it is easy to slip.

## Variations

**Wordless books** work extremely well and remove reading-level barriers.

**Song version:** Stop a familiar song before the chorus. Students sing what comes next.

**Extension:** After the book, ask: "If we read it again tomorrow, would you guess the same things?" This previews carry-forward.
