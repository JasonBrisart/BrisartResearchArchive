# Level H — Module 6: Research Programs and Their Failure Modes

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Artifact:** Program memo

## Objective

Participants design a multi-year line of work whose value does not depend on the framework surviving.

---

## 1. Frame — Programs Fail Differently Than Studies (20 min)

**Say:**
> "A bad study wastes a year. A bad program wastes a lab's decade and several careers. The failure modes are structural and they are visible in advance if you look for them."

---

## 2. Build — Five Program Failure Modes (50 min)

**Failure 1 — Single-point dependency.** Every project assumes the framework is correct. One refutation ends the program and everyone in it.
*Structural fix:* adjudication designs throughout, which produce results either way.

**Failure 2 — Confirmation drift.** Early ambiguous support makes later designs progressively less discriminating, because discriminating designs risk the program.
*Structural fix:* a standing requirement that every study enter the discriminating condition, enforced by someone other than the PI.

**Failure 3 — Scope creep under success.** Apparent success invites application to identity, groups, clinical populations. The framework's boundaries erode from inside the program.
*Structural fix:* the Module 3 scope procedure applied at project initiation.

**Failure 4 — Specification drift.** The framework is quietly modified to accommodate each unexpected result until it is unfalsifiable.
*Structural fix:* a change log. Any modification documented with the result that prompted it and the falsifiability cost.

**Failure 5 — Reputational entanglement.** Program members' standing becomes tied to the framework being true, making honest reporting personally expensive.
*Structural fix:* portable skills, publishable-either-way designs, and public credit for disconfirming results.

**Say:**
> "Failure 4 is the one that has ended the most research programs historically and it never announces itself. The change log is cheap and it is the only reliable detection."

---

## 3. Contest — Is a Program Warranted Yet? (45 min)

- **Participant A:** Committing a lab to a program before any independent validation exists is premature. Run one adjudicating study, then decide.
- **Participant B:** One study cannot settle it. Programs are how frameworks get properly examined, and a hedged program is the responsible vehicle.

**Say:**
> "A's single-study-first position is the conservative one and it is defensible. A program memo that recommends against a program, with reasons, is a passing artifact at this level."

---

## 4. Apply — The Program Memo (55 min)

    PROGRAM MEMO

    RECOMMENDATION:        proceed / proceed with limits / single study first / decline

    IF PROCEEDING
      Sequence of studies, each with its own falsification condition:
      How each produces value under refutation:
      Discriminating-condition requirement, and who enforces it:
      Change log procedure:
      Scope checkpoints at project initiation:
      Portable competences built:
      Stop conditions — what would end the program:

    IF DECLINING
      Reasons:
      What would change the recommendation:

**The stop conditions are the graded item.** A program without them is Failure 1 with extra steps.

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: licensing. The practical constraint most of you have not yet checked."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The decline option must be genuinely available and visibly scored equally.
- Stop conditions are consistently the weakest section in first drafts. Push until they name observations rather than feelings.
