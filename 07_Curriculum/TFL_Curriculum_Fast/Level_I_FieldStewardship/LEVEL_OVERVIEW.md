# Level I Overview — Field Stewardship

**Convening title:** Governing the Examination of an Unvalidated Framework
**Meetings:** 6 convenings
**Length:** Full day each
**Format:** Quarterly across one year, or a two-week residential
**Participants:** 8-24, drawn from multiple institutions

---

## What This Level Is For

Level H produces a faculty member who can steward a lab. Level I produces a
steward who can govern the framework's examination **across institutions, across
versions, and across time** — including deciding that it should stop.

The shift is from institutional responsibility to field responsibility. A Level H
graduate ensures their own group behaves well. A Level I graduate ensures that
the accumulated record of examination remains interpretable when the original
participants are gone.

This is the terminal level of the package.

---

## The Level I Premise

Stated at Convening 1 and returned to throughout:

> A framework can be well specified, honestly taught, rigorously supervised, and
> still produce an uninterpretable literature — because nobody governed the
> version authority, the replication record, or the conditions of retirement.
> Those are field-level functions and they are nobody's job by default.

| 5 | Supersession, Retirement, and Graceful Failure | Retirement policy |
| 6 | Charter Review | Defended governance charter |

Every convening produces a document that a body other than its author could
adopt and operate.

---

## Prerequisites

Level H, or equivalent standing. Participants must arrive with at least one of:

- Responsibility for a research group, program, or department
- Editorial, review-board, or standards-body responsibility
- Consortium, funder, or multi-institution coordination responsibility

Level I is not useful to someone who has never had to make a decision that
binds people other than themselves.

---

## Assessment

| Artifact | Weight |
|---|---|
| Field position statement | 10% |
| Version authority policy | 20% |
| Consortium protocol | 20% |
| Registry specification | 15% |
| Retirement policy | 20% |
| Charter defense | 15% |

**Gate requirement:** The charter does not pass without a version authority
policy and a retirement policy, both executable by a body that did not write
them.

A charter that recommends against field-level coordination, with reasons and
stated conditions for revisiting, is a complete and passing artifact.

---

## Standing Rules

1. Every artifact must be adoptable by a body other than its author.
2. The governing document is the Institutional Edition, and the version authority
   policy must say so explicitly.
3. The operational layer is never distributed through governance instruments.
   Registries, consortium protocols, and charters specify what laboratories must
   determine for themselves.
4. Clinical, diagnostic, and individual-assessment applications are excluded, and
   participants must be able to enforce that exclusion on institutions rather
   than individuals.
5. Participants are not asked to endorse the framework. A steward who considers
   it unpromising can complete every artifact honestly.

---

## What This Level Does Not Do

Level I does not confer authority over the framework. It confers the capability
to govern examination where such authority has been granted by the relevant
institutions, funders, or publishers.

Participants should leave clear that governance is a service function, not
ownership, and that the framework's licensing terms are separate from and
unaffected by any governance instrument produced here.
