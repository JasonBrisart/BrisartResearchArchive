# Level I — Convening 6: Charter Review

**Duration:** Full day
**Artifact:** Defended governance charter

## Objective

Participants defend a complete, adoptable governance charter.

---

## 1. Frame — The Adoption Test (45 min)

Restate the gate:

> "Two requirements. A version authority policy and a retirement policy, both
> executable by a body that did not write them. Without both, the charter does
> not pass."

**Say:**

> "That standard applies to all five instruments. You are building something
> intended to outlast your involvement. A charter that requires its authors is a
> committee, not a charter."

---

## 2. Blind Adoption Test (150 min)

Charters are exchanged between participants from different institutions.

Using the received charter only, with no verbal support, each participant:

1. Resolves a manufactured citation dispute under the version authority policy
2. Admits or refuses a hypothetical site under the consortium protocol
3. Accepts or rejects a registration under the registry specification
4. Renders a dormancy determination under the retirement policy
5. Identifies what the charter explicitly does not claim authority over

Each reports where the document failed them.

**Say:**

> "Those failures are your adoption score, and they are more useful than anything
> a reviewer could tell you."

---

## 3. Defenses (180 min)

Fifteen minutes presenting, fifteen defending.

**Presentation covers, in order:**

    1. The field position and what this body does not claim
    2. Which document governs and how supersession is declared
    3. How an unaffiliated institution joins and contributes
    4. How prespecification becomes verifiable
    5. How this ends, in each of the three ways
    6. What is preserved regardless of ending
    7. How the charter excludes operational content

**Challenge roles, assigned:**

- The **author's advocate** — asks by what right this body acts
- The **skeptical institution** — asks why adoption is worth the burden
- The **funder** — asks what this costs and who sustains it
- The **archivist** — asks what survives if this body dissolves in five years

---

## 4. Close (75 min)

Collect charters.

**Final discussion:**

> "Where does this group stand on whether coordinated field-level examination of
> this framework should proceed?"

Record the distribution, including declines to proceed.

**Say:**

> "Several of you have written charters recommending minimal coordination. Those
> are complete and correct artifacts. The level asked you to become capable of
> governing this examination responsibly, and concluding that it should not be
> governed at scale is inside that capability."

**Say:**

> "The framework remains unvalidated. What has changed across nine stages is that
> there is now a path from a child noticing that they were already guessing, to a
> body that could determine honestly whether the guess was right — and could
> conclude, on the record, that it was not."

---

## End of Level I and of the Package

Participants completing Level I should be able to:

- State what field-level functions an examination requires and which lack owners
- Establish version authority without claiming authority over the specification
- Build a replication protocol that independent institutions will actually adopt
- Specify a registry that makes prespecification verifiable and nulls recoverable
- Specify in advance how the programme concludes, in each of three ways
- Preserve a record that remains interpretable after their own involvement ends
