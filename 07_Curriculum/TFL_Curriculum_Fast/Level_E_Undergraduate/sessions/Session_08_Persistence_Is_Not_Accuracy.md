# Level E — Session 8: Persistence Is Not Accuracy

**Duration:** 75 min
**Materials:** Student prediction logs from Session 2

## Objective

Students test the independence claim against their own prospectively collected data and interpret the result honestly.

---

## 1. Frame — Your Own Data (8 min)

**Say:**
> "Six weeks ago you started recording predictions before outcomes. Today that log does work. This is the only session where you're testing the framework on data you generated under controlled conditions rather than reconstructing from memory."

---

## 2. Build — Two Independent Properties (18 min)

Write:

    ACCURACY     — did Sim(t) match what occurred?
    PERSISTENCE  — did Sim(t) carry into later windows?

    These are structurally independent.
    E(t) governs persistence.
    E(t) is silent on accuracy.

**State the provenance of this claim before teaching it:**

> "One honest note. The framework specifies that `E(t)` functions exclusively as a persistence gate and does not contribute to integration. It does not say anything about accuracy, because accuracy isn't one of its declared variables. The independence claim we're about to work with is an inference from exclusive gating, not something the specification states. That's a legitimate inference, and it's also the kind of thing you should be checking me on."

**Then the important qualification, which K-12 does not state:**

> "Independence in the architecture is not a prediction of zero correlation in data. They may correlate in the world for other reasons. The claim is that the mechanism doesn't make them correlate. Keep those two things separate or you'll misread your own results."

---

## 3. Apply — The Four-Box Study (25 min)

Moved before Contest deliberately; the data must exist before it is argued about.

**Part A (12 min):** Each student sorts their logged predictions into the matrix. Minimum eight entries.

                    PERSISTED        FADED
    ACCURATE    │              │              │
                ├──────────────┼──────────────┤
    INACCURATE  │              │              │

**Part B (13 min):** Written analysis.

1. Did all four cells receive entries?
2. If accuracy determined persistence, which two cells would be empty?
3. What does your own data show?
4. What are three reasons your data might mislead you here?

Question 4 is the adult addition and the most important item. Target answers: small n, self-selection of logged events, intensity rated by the same person who rated persistence, no control over ambiguity or repetition.

---

## 4. Contest — Interpreting the Class Tally (19 min)

Pool class totals across all four cells on the board.

The inaccurate-persisted cell is the framework's strongest observation and usually fills.

**Then assign the challenge:**

- **Student A:** This pattern is consistent with TFL.
- **Student B:** This pattern is equally consistent with uncertainty-only — unresolved items persist, and unresolved items are more often inaccurate.
- **Student C:** This pattern is equally consistent with habit — frequently-revisited items persist regardless of intensity.

**Say:**
> "B and C are correct, and this is the most useful thing that will happen to you this term. Your data is consistent with TFL. It is also consistent with two rival accounts. Consistency is not evidence for. Distinguishing requires a design, and that's Sessions 11 and 13."

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: what contradiction actually does, which is also not what most people expect."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The move from "my data fits" to "fitting is not evidence" is the single most valuable transfer in Level E. Let students feel the initial confirmation before dismantling it.
- If a class shows a pattern inconsistent with the framework, report it plainly. That is a legitimate result and modeling honest reporting matters more than the outcome.
