# Level E — Session 11: Three Alternative Accounts

**Duration:** 75 min

## Objective

Students state three competing accounts precisely and identify conditions that distinguish them from TFL.

---

## 1. Frame — Explain It Without TFL (10 min)

Present a result:

> A person continues expecting a cancelled event to occur. The expectation persists across three days despite contradicting information.

**Say:**
> "Explain that without using the framework."

Students produce uncertainty, stress or arousal, and habit.

**Say:**
> "You have just independently generated the three accounts TFL must be adjudicated against. That they're obvious is the problem — obvious alternatives are the ones that quietly explain your data."

---

## 2. Build — The Three Accounts (25 min)

Write each with its distinct prediction.

    UNCERTAINTY-ONLY
    Persistence reflects unresolved ambiguity.
    Predicts: resolve ambiguity → persistence ends,
    regardless of intensity.

    AROUSAL-ONLY
    Persistence reflects general activation.
    Predicts: any high-arousal state produces persistence,
    regardless of specific content.

    HABIT-BASED
    Persistence reflects repetition.
    Predicts: frequently repeated content persists,
    regardless of intensity or ambiguity.

Then TFL:

    TFL
    Persistence reflects E(t) relative to θ_persistence,
    with identifiable simulation content.
    Predicts: content-identifiable simulations persist when
    intensity clears threshold — not reducible to ambiguity,
    arousal, or repetition alone.

**Say:**
> "A valid implementation must be expressible in a form permitting adjudication against these. That is a requirement the framework places on itself, not a courtesy."

---

## 3. Contest — Alternative Attack (20 min)

Divide the room into thirds. Each third attacks a result that appears to support TFL:

> "Participants who rated an expectation as high-intensity were more likely to still hold it an hour later."

- **Uncertainty third:** Were high-intensity items also the more ambiguous ones?
- **Arousal third:** Was intensity just general activation? Content may be doing no work.
- **Habit third:** Were persistent items simply revisited more often?

Each presents. Then the defense round:

> "What must the study add to survive all three?"

Target: ambiguity measured and varied independently of intensity; general arousal measured separately; repetition counted or controlled; persistence criterion prespecified.

---

## 4. Apply — Differential Prediction Table (15 min)

| Account | Predicts persistence when... | Predicts NO persistence when... |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertainty-only | | |
| Arousal-only | | |
| Habit-based | | |
| TFL | | |

Then: name one condition where TFL predicts something different from all three.

Strong answers involve low ambiguity, controlled repetition, novel content, with `E(t)` measured and varied.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: where the framework doesn't apply at all. That's assessed, and it's where capable students lose the most points."

---

## Assessment

Assign the **Critique Memo**, due Session 13.

## Instructor Notes

- The rotation matters. A student assigned to attack one week and defend the next develops judgment; repeated assignment to one role produces a partisan.
- Keep the alternatives fairly stated. Strawmanning them makes the adjudication exercise worthless.
