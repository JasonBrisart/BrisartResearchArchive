# Band C — Day 1: Naming the System

**Duration:** 45 min
**Materials:** Chart paper, notebooks

---

## Objective

Students map plain-language descriptions onto the five formal variables.

---

## 1. Hook — Describe It Without the Words (7 min)

**Say:**
> "Something happened to you in the last ten seconds. Describe exactly what went into it. You can't use the word 'think.'"

Students write for two minutes.

Collect three. They will produce things like: *I heard you talking, I knew this was class, I figured you'd ask something.*

**Say:**
> "You just described a system with parts. Today we name the parts."

---

## 2. Name — Five Variables (15 min)

Build the table on chart paper. Give the plain name first, then the formal one.

| What you said | Formal name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| What's coming in | `Input(t)` | Sensory evidence in this window |
| What came back | `Minfo(t)` | Retrieved memory content |
| What I figured would happen | `Sim(t)` | The predictive simulation |
| How much it hit me | `E(t)` | Affective intensity |
| The whole moment | `F(t)` | The integrated state |

**Explain the notation once:**
> "The `(t)` just means *at this time step*. Each of these has a value right now, and a different value a moment from now."

**State the constraint immediately** — do not save it:

> "One rule that surprises people. `E(t)` — the intensity — does **not** help build the prediction. It only decides whether the prediction survives into the next step. Write that down."

---

## 3. Work — Translate Three Moments (15 min)

Students take three real moments and label the components.

**Template:**

```
Moment: _______________________________

Input(t):   ___________________________
Minfo(t):   ___________________________
Sim(t):     ___________________________
E(t):       high / medium / low
F(t):       ___________________________
```

**Requirement:** `Sim(t)` must be a **specific statement** of what was expected. "I felt weird about it" is not a simulation.

**Circulate.** The `Sim(t)` line is where errors appear. Push for content.

---

## 4. Compare — The Vague Sim Problem (6 min)

Put two student examples on the board — one with a specific `Sim(t)`, one vague.

**Ask:**
> "Which one could we actually test?"

**Say:**
> "Simulation content has to be identifiable. If you can't say what you expected, this model doesn't apply to that moment. That's a real rule, not a technicality."

---

## 5. Carry (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Tomorrow: how fast this runs. It's faster than you'd guess."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Introduce notation once and then use it constantly. Students adapt quickly at this age.
- The `E(t)` constraint stated on Day 1 prevents most Band C misconceptions. Do not delay it.
