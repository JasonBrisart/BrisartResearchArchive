# Band C — Day 5: Persistence Is Not Accuracy

**Duration:** 45 min
**Materials:** Chart paper, notebooks

---

## Objective

Students demonstrate from their own data that persistence and correctness are independent.

**This is the pivot session of Band C.**

---

## 1. Hook — The Confidence Poll (6 min)

**Say:**
> "Think of a prediction you were really confident about. Hands up if you were right."

Some hands.

**Say:**
> "Hands down. Now: think of a prediction you were really confident about and completely wrong about."

Most hands go up.

**Say:**
> "So confidence didn't tell you. Today we find out what it *does* tell you."

---

## 2. Name — Two Separate Things (10 min)

Write both clearly:

```
ACCURACY     — did the prediction match what happened?
PERSISTENCE  — did the prediction carry into later windows?

These are independent.
E(t) governs persistence.
E(t) says nothing about accuracy.
```

**Say:**
> "The model is explicit about this. Affective intensity functions exclusively as a persistence gate. It doesn't appear in how the state gets built, and it doesn't evaluate whether the prediction was right."

---

## 3. Work — The Four-Box Study (20 min)

Students gather their own data.

**Part A (10 min)** — Fill the matrix with at least six real predictions.

```
                  PERSISTED        FADED
    ACCURATE    [           ]   [           ]
    INACCURATE  [           ]   [           ]
```

Each entry needs one line of description.

**Part B (10 min)** — Written analysis:

1. Did all four boxes fill?
2. If accuracy determined persistence, which boxes would be empty?
3. What does your own data show?

---

## 4. Compare — Class Totals (7 min)

Tally the class across all four boxes on the board.

All four will have substantial entries.

**Say:**
> "If accuracy drove persistence, the accurate-faded and inaccurate-persisted boxes would be near empty. Look at them."

**Say the line to remember:**
> "Being right doesn't make it stick. Mattering makes it stick."

---

## 5. Carry (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: what being wrong actually does to a simulation. Also not what you'd guess."

## Instructor Notes

- This is the highest-priority session in Band C. If a class is behind, cut Day 8 before cutting this.
- The inaccurate-persisted box is the most powerful. Draw attention to it explicitly.
- Keep examples low-stakes — games, schedules, weather, tests. Not personal distress.
