# Level F Overview — Advanced Undergraduate and Master's

**Course title:** Operationalizing and Testing a Formal Cognitive Framework
**Meetings:** 12
**Length:** 100 minutes (seminar plus design lab)
**Typical placement:** 400/500-level

---

## What Students Learn

Level E produced a student who could critique the framework. Level F produces one who could **test** it.

The level is built around a single deliverable: a complete, executable study design capable of adjudicating TFL against its three rival accounts, with a prespecified falsification condition. Everything else serves that artifact.

The organizing constraint:

> TFL applies only where all core variables can be operationalized and measured. Use without observable mapping of `Input(t)`, `Minfo(t)`, `E(t)`, `Sim(t)`, and `F(t)` does not constitute a valid application or test.

Students discover early that this constraint invalidates most of what they initially want to do.

## Session Arc

| # | Title | Core move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Testability Constraint | Why most proposed uses are not tests |
| 2 | Measuring Sim(t) | Prespecification, timing, and contamination |
| 3 | Measuring E(t) | Separating intensity from general arousal |
| 4 | Measuring Persistence | Sampling rate, criteria, and the recurrence problem |
| 5 | Windows and Sampling | Choosing a unit of observation you can defend |
| 6 | Estimating the Weights | What `w1`-`w3` require from data |
| 7 | Designing to Adjudicate | Independent manipulation of ambiguity and intensity |
| 8 | Confounds and Controls | The audit that kills most designs |
| 9 | Prespecification and Analysis Plans | Locking decisions before data |
| 10 | Scope Enforcement in Design | Rejecting your own best idea |
| 11 | Peer Design Review | Formal audit of one another's work |
| 12 | Final Design Defense | Present, defend, revise |

Sessions 2-4 are the measurement core. A student weak there cannot pass Session 12.

## Prerequisites

Level E or placement. One research methods course. No advanced statistics required, though Session 6 assumes familiarity with regression as a concept.

## Assessment Summary

| Instrument | Weight | When |
|---|---|---|
| Problem sets (4) | 20% | After Sessions 3, 5, 8, 10 |
| Operationalization examination | 20% | After Session 6 |
| Peer review report | 15% | Session 11 |
| Final study design | 45% | Session 12 |

**Gate requirement:** The final design does not pass without a measurement map another person could execute without asking clarifying questions.

## Human Subjects

From Session 7 onward students design studies involving people. All institutional research ethics requirements apply in full and are the instructor's responsibility to enforce. Course designs that remain hypothetical must be labeled as such. No data may be collected under course auspices without appropriate approval.

## Standing Rules

1. Prediction accuracy is never graded.
2. The student's conclusion about TFL is never graded.
3. A design built on an unmeasurable `Sim(t)` is not a valid TFL study and cannot pass.
4. Over-application is a scored error.
5. Clinical, diagnostic, or individual-assessment designs are rejected outright.

## What Carries Into Level G

Students finishing Level F should be able to:

- Operationalize all five variables with defensible instruments
- Prespecify a persistence criterion and a sampling interval
- Design a study that distinguishes TFL from all three rival accounts
- Identify and control the confounds that invalidate naive designs
- Produce a written analysis plan before data exist
- Conduct a formal design audit of someone else's work

Level G begins from a design that survives audit and adds original critique, adjudication at literature scale, and referee-quality review.
