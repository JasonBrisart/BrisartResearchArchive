# Level F — Session 10: Scope Enforcement in Design

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students apply scope boundaries to research designs, including their own, and reject invalid proposals formally.

---

## 1. Frame — The Attractive Invalid Study (12 min)

**Say:**
> "At Level E scope was about examples. Here it's about studies, which is harder, because an invalid study can be methodologically excellent. Rigor does not rescue a design that falls outside what the framework models."

---

## 2. Build — Design-Level Rejection Criteria (28 min)

Review the exclusion set, then apply it to research proposals specifically.

**Invalid by timescale:** Any design whose outcome is measured in months or years — attitude change, skill acquisition, identity development.

**Invalid by level of analysis:** Any design whose unit is a group, an institution, or a culture.

**Invalid by implementation claim:** Any design proposing to locate a variable in neural tissue. The framework specifies a process, not an implementation.

**Invalid by clinical inference:** Any design that classifies individuals by condition, screens for a disorder, or evaluates an intervention. The framework provides no diagnostic criteria and no therapeutic mechanism.

**Invalid by unmeasurable Sim(t):** Any design where simulation content cannot be prespecified and checked — regardless of how good everything else is.

**Say:**
> "The clinical category is the one this room will violate. It is the most intuitive application and it is always wrong, and at this level proposing it is a scored error rather than a conversation."

---

## 3. Contest — The Hardest Case (28 min)

Present a genuinely borderline design and let the room work it:

> A study tracking whether expectations about an ongoing negotiation persist across a two-week period, with daily prompted elicitation and intensity ratings.

Arguments both ways: two weeks exceeds short timescale; but if identifiable simulation content is tracked with adequate sampling, propagation claims may hold.

**Resolution to steer toward:**
> "It depends on whether the claim is about content tracked across windows or about the negotiation as a unit. The first may be inside at declared resolution. The second is outside. The design must say which, and most don't."

---

## 4. Apply — Reject Your Own Idea (27 min)

Each student writes a formal rejection of an invalid variant of *their own* design.

    MY INVALID VARIANT:
    REJECTED BECAUSE:
    BOUNDARY VIOLATED:
    APPROPRIATE FRAMEWORK OR LEVEL INSTEAD:
    WHAT MADE THIS VERSION TEMPTING:

The last line is the graded item. Students who cannot say why the invalid version appealed to them will build it again.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next session you review each other formally. Bring a complete design; you're reviewing, not drafting."

---

## Assessment

Administer **Problem Set 4**.
