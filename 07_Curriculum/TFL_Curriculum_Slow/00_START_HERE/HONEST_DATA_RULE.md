# The Honest Data Rule

This is the rule most likely to be broken, and the one that matters most.

## The rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern:

1. **Preserve the result.** Write it down as it happened.
2. **Do not re-run it** to obtain a tidier outcome.
3. **Do not change any student's classification** to fill a cell.
4. **Do not suggest** the student misunderstood merely because their result differs.
5. **Ask whether the method could have detected the pattern at all.**
6. **Ask whether the sample was large enough.**
7. **Record the result as contradictory or inconclusive.**
8. **State what additional observation would be needed.**
9. **Continue with the limited claim the data actually supports.**

## Why this matters more than the result

A failed activity still teaches:

- sampling limits;
- measurement limits;
- what sparse data looks like;
- ambiguity;
- the difference between illustrating an idea and testing it.

A tidied activity teaches students that data should be adjusted until it agrees
with the teacher. That is the opposite of what this curriculum exists to build.

## What students are never asked to do

- Alter a recorded result
- Reclassify an example so the class pattern looks stronger
- Produce an example in a category where they genuinely have none
- Treat "cannot determine" as a failure

"Cannot determine" is a complete and correct answer at every grade.

## Illustration is not validation

Every activity in this curriculum **illustrates** an idea. None of them **tests**
whether the idea is true. Say so aloud at least once per lesson. Students who
leave believing a classroom demonstration validated the framework have learned
something false.

## You are never graded on whether a prediction turned out correct. You are graded on recording it and reasoning about it.
