## The pivot session of this quarter

**Session 4 — Persistence Criterion And Sampling.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Session 1:** every draft returns marked and the student can say why
- **Session 2:** every protocol names who scores and how reliability is reported
- **Session 3:** every student produces a genuine alternative explanation
- **Session 4:** every student names a post-hoc vulnerability and what prevents it
- **Session 5:** the final line names real excluded conclusions
- **Session 6:** every student states honestly whether estimation is feasible

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Session | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | defend an unmarked line by explaining what they meant | Point at the evidence: they could not run it. That is the test. |
| 2 | omit inter-rater reliability | Require the number: two coders, what agreement, reported how? |
| 3 | assert the instrument is clean without auditing | Run the three requirements line by line. |
| 4 | omit the partial-match rule | Supply a borderline response and ask how their rule scores it. |
| 5 | write generic limitations in the final line | Require specifics: name the conclusion, not the word limitations. |
| 6 | propose estimating from a collinear design | Walk the collinearity: how will you separate memory from simulation? |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
