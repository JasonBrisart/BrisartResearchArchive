# Grade 3

**Course:** Right Now · **Stage:** Primary
**32 lessons of 35 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

That experience arrives already combined, and can be decomposed afterwards.

> Noticing, remembering, and expecting combine into one moment.

## Entry check — before session one

**Grade 3 assumes Grade 2.** The student builds and repairs a four-column comparison table.

- [ ] Set two expectations side by side and state the difference
- [ ] Name the differing memory that caused a difference in expectation
- [ ] Support a claim with evidence that can be pointed at
- [ ] Frame a changed expectation as an update, not a mistake

If a box will not tick, return to `../03_Grade_02/` and reteach that outcome before starting. Advancement follows demonstrated readiness, never the calendar.

## Exit outcomes

- State that the three parts combine into one experienced moment
- Build a five-line reconstruction ending in a single combined statement
- Decompose a described moment into three parts that check forwards
- Support each line with evidence and mark unsupported lines honestly

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **right now** | everything combined into the moment you are actually having |
| **combine** | three things arriving together as one |
| **decompose** | taking a moment apart to see what built it |

**Not used at this grade:** Carry forward, integrated state, all notation.

## How this grade is taught

Short structured paragraphs. A claim, the evidence, and the cause, in complete sentences. Drawing supports but no longer substitutes.

## How students show they have learned it

Written reconstructions, quarter quizzes and tests, four projects.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Right now is just what I am seeing"

**Why it happens.** Perception feels direct and unconstructed.

**How to fix it.** Show an ambiguous stimulus and collect differing reports.

**What to say.** "Right now is what you notice, plus what you remember, plus what you expect."

### "The combination sentence is a list"

**Why it happens.** Students rewrite the three parts with commas and call it combined.

**How to fix it.** Refuse the list and ask what the moment added up to.

**What to say.** "That is three things with commas. Give me the one thing."

### "Everybody here has the same right now"

**Why it happens.** The shared setting implies shared experience.

**How to fix it.** Put two reconstructions of the same event side by side.

**What to say.** "The event was shared. The moment was not."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Provide the five-line template with the final line pre-started.

**Extension.** Ask for a reconstruction from somebody else's point of view. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
