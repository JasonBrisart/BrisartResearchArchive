## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — The Snack Guess.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every child has said at least one guess out loud
- **Lesson 2:** at least half the group answers a do-not-look question
- **Lesson 3:** the group can say because I heard it before in any words
- **Lesson 4:** every child commits before looking
- **Lesson 5:** every child has drawn a guess about something later
- **Lesson 6:** every child guesses from touch before seeing
- **Lesson 7:** the group notices that answers differ
- **Lesson 8:** the group can say guessing happened every time

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | refuse to guess, saying they do not know | Offer two choices instead: do you think it is soft or hard? |
| 2 | turn around to look before answering | Make it a freeze game. Statues cannot turn. |
| 3 | offer a phrase nobody else knows | Accept it warmly, then ask who has heard it. Absence of a shared guess is the lesson. |
| 4 | look first, then announce | Cover the window or turn chairs away. Remove the option. |
| 5 | worry about being wrong | Model it. Guess aloud, be wrong, be delighted. |
| 6 | peek | Use a bag with a sleeve rather than an open box. |
| 7 | argue about who is right | Remove correctness. Say we are finding out that they are different, not who wins. |
| 8 | forget earlier sessions | Hold up the kept drawings. The evidence is physical. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
