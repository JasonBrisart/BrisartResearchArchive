## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Invisible.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student describes a poorly fitting case
- **Lesson 26:** every student has written and sealed a list
- **Lesson 27:** every student states limits on three results
- **Lesson 28:** every student receives an audit marking at least one overreach
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** every student compares an early and a late investigation
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | invent a reason it does not count | Remove the option: describe it, you may not dismiss it. |
| 26 | list questions the year did answer | Check each: did we answer that? Then it does not go on. |
| 27 | write generic limits | Require specifics: what could your trial not have seen? |
| 28 | find no overreach | Require one per set. There always is one. |
| 29 | report no question | Brief the listener to ask one. |
| 30 | cannot find early work | One folder from Lesson 1. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | run out of time on the alternative | The alternative is the Grade 6 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
