## Exit outcomes

- Define contradiction and name revision and suppression as its effects
- Distinguish persistence from recurrence and state the measurement needed
- Report a result within the limits of its sampling rate
- List genuine assumptions under a claim and identify the weakest

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **revision** | a simulation being modified after contradiction |
| **suppression** | a simulation being pushed aside rather than deleted |
| **recurrence** | previously identified content reappearing after absence |
| **Belief(t)** | an affectively stabilised prior constraining retrieval under ambiguity |
| **assumption** | something a claim depends on being true |

**Not used at this grade:** The integration function, w1/w2/w3, the three rival accounts by name.

## How this grade is taught

An assumption audit is required on every major task. A student states what their claim depends on, not only what it asserts.

## How students show they have learned it

Trials, written audits, quarter quizzes and tests, four projects.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Contradiction deletes the simulation"

**Why it happens.** Students assume mismatch acts like an eraser.

**How to fix it.** The multi-break trial with recorded percentages.

**What to say.** "It drives revision or suppression, not instant deletion."

### "Recurrence is just persistence I did not notice"

**Why it happens.** A legitimate objection, not an error.

**How to fix it.** Credit it, then show that distinguishing them is a measurement problem.

**What to say.** "You are right that you cannot tell from here."

### "Belief(t) replaces the input"

**Why it happens.** Students overextend the idea of a prior.

**How to fix it.** Show a case with unambiguous input where belief does not override it.

**What to say.** "It biases which memories come back. It does not replace what you see."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Provide the sampling log as a five-checkpoint sheet with fixed times.

**Extension.** Ask them to design a schedule that could detect a two-minute absence. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
