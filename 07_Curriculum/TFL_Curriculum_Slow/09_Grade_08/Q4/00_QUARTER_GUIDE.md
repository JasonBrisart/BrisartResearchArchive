## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Limits.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every rejection names a specific boundary
- **Lesson 26:** every student has a written verbatim refusal
- **Lesson 27:** every student resolves at least two by identifiability
- **Lesson 28:** every student answers both challenges specifically
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** every student lists at least four real limitations with examples
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | reject without naming which boundary | Require the boundary word: timescale, level, implementation, or clinical. |
| 26 | propose a clinical application as a good idea | Reject it outright and explain the absence of diagnostic content. |
| 27 | decide by duration alone | Supply a counter-case with a long duration and stateable content. |
| 28 | defend by restating the rejection | Require engagement with the specific challenge. |
| 29 | present the limits as apologies | Reframe: these are part of the framework, not caveats on it. |
| 30 | list generic limitations | Require the example and the entry number. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | list restatements as assumptions under pressure | The assumption audit is the Grade 9 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
