## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — Different Memories.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** children distinguish a notice answer from a remember answer
- **Lesson 10:** most pairs sort at least six of eight correctly
- **Lesson 11:** the group recalls at least three items before checking
- **Lesson 12:** the group completes at least three fragments
- **Lesson 13:** every pair finds one difference
- **Lesson 14:** each child completes the frame with a connected pair
- **Lesson 15:** the class can say different memories give different guesses
- **Lesson 16:** the group sorts at least nine of twelve correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | describe remembering as hard work | Contrast it: did you have to try to remember GO? |
| 10 | sort by preference rather than category | Ask the deciding question: is that happening right now, or before? |
| 11 | look at the chart | Cover it. The cold recall is the point. |
| 12 | think they are being tested | Frame it as a game, never as a quiz. |
| 13 | insist one of them is wrong | Remove correctness: both were there, both remember different bits. |
| 14 | fill the frame with a wish | Ask whether that is a guess or a hope. |
| 15 | insist one answer was right | Remove correctness explicitly and repeat it. |
| 16 | slip back into using them interchangeably | Re-run the timing test on every disputed card. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
