## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Silence Is Not Denial.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every student compares their sealed prediction with current understanding
- **Lesson 18:** every student names a candidate observable for at least three
- **Lesson 19:** every student rejects at least three unobtainable proposals
- **Lesson 20:** every student routes five questions correctly
- **Lesson 21:** every student sorts at least six correctly
- **Lesson 22:** every student compares three outside parameters
- **Lesson 23:** every student ranks all four with justification
- **Lesson 24:** the class judges at least four correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | dismiss a wrong prediction as naive | Defend it: you had no way to know. You checked it. That is the job. |
| 18 | propose an observable that could not actually be measured | Ask the practical question: who measures that, with what, this week? |
| 19 | accept observables that require impossible access | Ask who, with what equipment, in what timeframe. |
| 20 | attribute persistence effects to the integration function | Route it: persistence is the other relation. Which one has E(t)? |
| 21 | treat every silence as a denial | Use the three-way sort on every disputed item. |
| 22 | assume other models never have free parameters | Show one that does and how it is defended. |
| 23 | rank by importance rather than support | Ask what evidence exists for each, specifically. |
| 24 | accept a parameter with no measurement pathway | Re-run the practical question every time. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
