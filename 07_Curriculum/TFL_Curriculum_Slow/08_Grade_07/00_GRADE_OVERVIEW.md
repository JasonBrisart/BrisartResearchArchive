# Grade 7

**Course:** Persistence Is Not Accuracy · **Stage:** Middle
**32 lessons of 45 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

The central distinction of the entire curriculum, tested on the student's own data.

> How strongly it mattered decides whether it lasted. It says nothing about whether it was right.

## Exit outcomes

- Write the persistence condition and define every term in it
- State that raising the threshold decreases persistence, with reasoning
- Demonstrate from their own prospective data that persistence and accuracy are independent
- State in advance what result would have counted against a claim

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **theta_persistence** | the persistence threshold required for carry-over |
| **accuracy** | whether Sim(t) matched what actually happened |
| **decay** | a simulation weakening and not carrying forward |
| **prospective** | recorded before the outcome is known |

**Not used at this grade:** The integration function, w1/w2/w3, the three rival accounts.

## How this grade is taught

Comparative analysis with collected data. A student states what would change their mind before presenting a conclusion.

## How students show they have learned it

A prospective log running all year, quarter quizzes and tests, four projects.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "If I felt strongly about it, I was probably right"

**Why it happens.** Confidence and accuracy feel identical from the inside.

**How to fix it.** The four-box study on the student's own prospective data.

**What to say.** "Intensity decides how long it stuck, not whether it was right."

### "Persistence means I cannot stop thinking about it"

**Why it happens.** The word carries everyday intrusive connotations.

**How to fix it.** Keep persistence mechanical and low-stakes.

**What to say.** "Persistence just means it carried into the next cycle."

### "Raising the threshold means more persistence"

**Why it happens.** The intuitive reading of higher as more.

**How to fix it.** The passing-grade analogy, then the direction table.

**What to say.** "Raising the pass mark does not mean more people pass."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Provide the log as a daily sheet with intensity as a three-point circle.

**Extension.** Ask for one case in their own data the framework does not predict well. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
