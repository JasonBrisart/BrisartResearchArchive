## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Loop.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student redraws as a loop with a justification
- **Lesson 26:** every student states that their trace sampled a longer chain
- **Lesson 27:** every student defeats at least two apparent fresh starts
- **Lesson 28:** every student produces a one-sentence statement that is not a list
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question asked
- **Lesson 30:** every student compares an early and a late trace
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | draw a loop but describe a start | Ask where: point at the start. What did that row inherit? |
| 26 | claim the trace is complete | Ask what happened between rows three and four. |
| 27 | accept a fresh start at face value | Ask what they were expecting on the way there. |
| 28 | produce a list with conjunctions | Count the ideas. More than one means rewrite. |
| 29 | leave out the return | Brief the listener to ask: and then what? |
| 30 | cannot find early work | One folder from Lesson 1. Say so on day one. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | produce eight independent moments | The inheritance is the Grade 5 prerequisite. Score it separately and say so. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
