## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — Expecting At Home.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every student writes EXPECT in at least two sentences
- **Lesson 2:** most students have two rows connecting across all three columns
- **Lesson 3:** every student lists five routine expectations
- **Lesson 4:** every student has three dated expectations
- **Lesson 5:** every student writes five home expectations
- **Lesson 6:** every student applies the break test
- **Lesson 7:** each pair identifies one such row
- **Lesson 8:** the class sorts at least nine of twelve correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | use expect and guess interchangeably | Accept the meaning and restate: yes, and in here we say expect. |
| 2 | write three unrelated things in a row | Point at the middle column and ask what it makes them expect. |
| 3 | claim they had no expectation about routine things | Run the break test aloud: imagine the light did nothing. |
| 4 | write events rather than expectations | Ask for the sentence: I expect ___ to happen. |
| 5 | reuse school examples | Ask for the room: in the kitchen, what do you expect there? |
| 6 | claim there was no expectation | Run the break test with them, aloud, item by item. |
| 7 | conclude one of them is wrong | Remove correctness from the question. |
| 8 | confuse noticing and remembering under time pressure | Re-run the timing test on every disputed item. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
