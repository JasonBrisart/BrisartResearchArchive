# Level E — Session 2: Prediction as Continuous Process

**Duration:** 75 min
**Materials:** Audio source; index cards with high-contrast shapes

## Objective

Students observe that prediction runs continuously and becomes reportable only when broken.

---

## 1. Frame — The Invisible Process Problem (10 min)

**Say:**
> "The framework claims you are predicting constantly, in every window, whether or not you notice. That's a strong claim. How would you catch it happening?"

Take answers. Steer toward: break it.

**Say:**
> "Correct prediction is invisible precisely because it matched. The only observable is failure. That's a methodological constraint we'll pay for repeatedly this term."

---

## 2. Build — Engineered Mismatch (20 min)

Run three demonstrations on the room. Do not warn beforehand; do warn individual students privately in advance if they have requested it.

**Demonstration 1 — Sound stop.** Play a familiar recording. Let it run fifteen seconds. Stop abruptly mid-phrase.

**Demonstration 2 — Pattern break.** Hold up shape cards: four identical, then one different, then return to the original.

**Demonstration 3 — Sequence skip.** Count aloud: "One, two, three, five." Stop.

After each, the same question:

> "You reacted. Reacting means you expected something. What, specifically, did you expect?"

Collect specific completions. Reject "it was weird" and push once:

> "Weird compared to what? Name the content."

**Say:**
> "That conversion — from a reaction to a stateable expectation — is the entire measurement problem of this framework in miniature."

---

## 3. Contest — Does This Show What It Claims? (20 min)

Assign three students, announced in advance, to argue alternative explanations of what the room just experienced:

- **Student A:** Surprise reflects violated statistical regularity, not a discrete simulation with content.
- **Student B:** The reports are post-hoc confabulation — people construct the expectation after the break, not before.
- **Student C:** This is habit, not prediction. The pattern was trained in twenty seconds.

Each presents. Then open.

**Say:**
> "B is the serious one and it will follow us all term. If expectation is only ever reported after the outcome, we have no measurement. Session 5 is about the fix, and the fix is prespecification."

Do not resolve this now. Leave it visibly unresolved.

---

## 4. Apply — Prospective Prediction Log (20 min)

Students begin a log they will carry to Session 8.

Rules, stated explicitly:
- The prediction must be written *before* the outcome is known
- It must be specific enough that another person could check it
- Intensity is rated at the time of writing, not later
- The outcome is recorded when it arrives, without revising the prediction

    Prediction (written in advance):
    Time written:
    Intensity at writing:      high / medium / low
    Outcome:
    Time outcome known:
    Still thinking about it 24h later?   Y / N

Minimum eight entries by Session 8. Start two now, in class, about the next twenty-four hours.

**Say:**
> "Your prediction being wrong costs you nothing. Writing it after the fact costs you the entire data point."

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next session we name the parts. You've now felt four of the five."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The confabulation objection is the most important thing raised in Level E's first third. Credit the student who raises it unprompted and return to their name at Session 5.
- Adults suppress visible surprise. Watch hands and posture rather than faces, and use volunteers for demonstrations where a visible reaction matters.
- The prospective log is the backbone of Session 8. If students do not start it now, Session 8 collapses into anecdote.
