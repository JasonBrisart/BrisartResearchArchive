# Level E — Session 10: Variable Closure and Its Cost

**Duration:** 75 min

## Objective

Students state the closure constraint and evaluate the trade of explanatory reach for falsifiability.

---

## 1. Frame — Return to Session 3 (8 min)

**Say:**
> "In Session 3 you proposed sixth variables. I deferred the cost question. Today it gets answered."

Put the original student proposals back on the board.

---

## 2. Build — Closure (22 min)

    VARIABLE CLOSURE

    All valid inferences are restricted to:
      Input(t), Minfo(t), Sim(t), E(t), F(t)

    No additional constructs, latent processes, or
    external variables are assumed or inferred
    beyond this set.

**Say:**
> "This is a commitment and it costs something. The framework gives up explanatory reach to gain testability."

**The core argument:**
> "If I can add a variable whenever the model fails, the model can never fail. Closure is what lets someone else prove this wrong."

Work through the student proposals. Attention is partially absorbable — it affects what gets sampled into `Input(t)`. Motivation is not cleanly absorbable, and saying so is more honest than forcing it.

Then the guarantee set, in the framework's own order:

    1. State continuity (selective, not exhaustive)
    2. Threshold-gated simulation persistence
    3. Conditional determinism of integration
    4. Temporal window constraint
    5. Convergence under defined conditions
    6. Bounded affective persistence
    7. Standardized variable interface
    8. Measurable state evolution
    9. Adjudication compatibility

Note that guarantee 5 is stated as a *conditional* guarantee, which is why it doubles as the framework's most important limit:

    CONVERGENCE IS NOT GLOBALLY GUARANTEED.

    Resolution occurs only under:
      - sufficient congruence between Sim(t) and Input(t)
      - contradiction-driven updating or suppression
      - decay of E(t) below threshold

---

## 3. Contest — Is Closure Principled or Convenient? (22 min)

- **Student A:** Closure is methodologically principled — the standard defense of parsimony.
- **Student B:** Closure is convenient. It excludes exactly the variables that would complicate the framework's central claim, and the boundary is drawn by the author.

**Say:**
> "B's challenge has a test. If closure were convenient, the excluded variables would be the threatening ones. Are they? Attention and motivation are excluded. Do either specifically threaten the gating claim?"

Generally not — which weakens B, though students should notice that arousal is a closer call and is handled as a rival account rather than as a variable.

---

## 4. Apply — The Absorption Exercise (18 min)

Four proposed variables, each analyzed:

    Proposed variable:
    Expressible through the declared five?   Yes / No / Partly
    If yes, how:
    If no, what would adding it cost the model?

The final line is the analytical work. Target answers: reduced falsifiability, unbounded post-hoc fitting, loss of adjudication capability.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: the three accounts that would explain your Session 8 data just as well. Time to name them properly."

---

## Assessment

Administer **Problem Set 3**.

## Instructor Notes

- Do not present closure as strictness for its own sake. The trade framing is what makes it defensible.
