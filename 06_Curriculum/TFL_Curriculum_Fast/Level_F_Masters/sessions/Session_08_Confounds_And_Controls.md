# Level F — Session 8: Confounds and Controls

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students identify the confounds that invalidate naive TFL designs and specify controls for each.

---

## 1. Frame — Your Design Is Probably Broken (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Today is adversarial. Your design is the defendant. Most will not survive intact and that is the intended outcome — better here than after data collection."

---

## 2. Build — The Standard Confound Set (30 min)

**Confound 1 — Intensity confounded with ambiguity.**
Control: measure and vary independently; report their observed correlation.

**Confound 2 — Intensity confounded with general arousal.**
Control: separate activation index; include as covariate.

**Confound 3 — Intensity confounded with repetition.**
Control: count exposures; hold constant or vary deliberately.

**Confound 4 — Outcome knowledge contaminating the intensity rating.**
Control: timestamp; rate before outcome; blind raters.

**Confound 5 — Demand characteristics.**
Control: participants unaware of the persistence hypothesis; cover task; check suspicion at debrief.

**Confound 6 — Experimenter as scorer.**
Control: blind coding; report inter-rater reliability.

**Confound 7 — Selection of which predictions get logged.**
Control: prompted logging at fixed times rather than participant-initiated.

**Say:**
> "Seven is the one that ruined your Level E prediction logs, and nobody noticed at the time. You recorded the predictions you found interesting enough to record."

---

## 3. Contest — The Audit (30 min)

Structured peer attack, three rounds, rotating.

Each reviewer is assigned one rival account and must argue that the design's anticipated result is better explained by it.

    Reviewer role:                 uncertainty / arousal / habit
    The design's likely result:
    How my account explains it equally well:
    What the design must add to exclude me:

Designers may not defend during the attack. They take notes and respond at the end of the round.

---

## 4. Apply — Confound Table (25 min)

    | Confound | Present in my design? | Control specified | Residual risk |

All seven addressed explicitly. "Not applicable" requires a reason.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: locking your decisions before data exist, so none of these controls can quietly loosen later."

---

## Assessment

Administer **Problem Set 3**.

## Instructor Notes

- Enforce the no-defending rule during the attack rounds. Designers who argue in real time stop listening.
- Confound 7 lands hardest because students experienced it personally. Use that.
