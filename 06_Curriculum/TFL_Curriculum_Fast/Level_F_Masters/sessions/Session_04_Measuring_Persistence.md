# Level F — Session 4: Measuring Persistence

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students define a persistence criterion in advance and specify sampling adequate to distinguish persistence from recurrence.

---

## 1. Frame — Two Decisions Made Before Data (12 min)

**Say:**
> "Persistence looks like the easy measurement — did it stick or not. It is not. Two decisions determine everything, and both must be made before you collect anything: what counts as persisting, and how often you look."

---

## 2. Build — Criterion and Sampling (32 min)

**The sampling problem.**

Recall from Level E: persistence is carry-forward across windows under threshold; recurrence is the reappearance of previously identified simulation content, as a function of persistence and reentry conditions. Distinguishing the two empirically requires that you be able to detect absence.

> "To claim recurrence you must observe absence. To observe absence you must sample faster than the gap. If you sample once an hour, everything looks persistent and recurrence is invisible by construction."

> "Your sampling interval sets a floor on the phenomena you can see. State it, justify it, and report what it excludes."

---

## 3. Contest — Is Recurrence Distinguishable in Principle? (25 min)

- **Student A:** Any absence could be an unsampled presence. Recurrence and persistence are not distinguishable by sampling; the distinction is untestable.
- **Student B:** The distinction is empirical at a defined resolution. You cannot prove absence absolutely, but you can state the resolution at which your claim holds — which is how every measurement works.

**Say:**
> "B is right about method and A has identified why the framework should state this as a resolution-bounded claim rather than an absolute one. That is a legitimate specification critique and worth writing up."

---

## 4. Apply — Persistence Protocol (26 min)

Students complete criterion and sampling specification, then trade for audit.

Auditor questions, applied strictly:

1. Could the criterion be adjusted after data collection to change the result?
2. Is the sampling interval fast enough to detect the gaps the design claims to see?
3. Is there a prespecified rule for partial matches?

Return written notes.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "You've now specified the three hard variables. Next: the window itself, and whether you can defend your unit of observation."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Students consistently under-specify partial matches. It is where post-hoc flexibility hides.
- The resolution-bounded framing from Contest is worth recording; several Level G critique papers develop from it.
