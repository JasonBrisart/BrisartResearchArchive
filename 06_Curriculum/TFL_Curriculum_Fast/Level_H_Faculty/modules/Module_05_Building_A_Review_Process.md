# Level H — Module 5: Building a Review Process

**Duration:** 3.5 hours
**Artifact:** Review protocol and reviewer brief

## Objective

Participants construct a review process for work on the framework that reviews the inference rather than the framework.

---

## 1. Frame — Two Reviewer Failures (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Reviewers fail in two directions here, and both are common. Reject because the framework is unvalidated — that reviews reputation. Accept because the methods are careful — that lets rigor substitute for relevance. Your protocol has to prevent both, in reviewers you did not train."

---

## 2. Build — Protocol Architecture (55 min)

    REVIEWER BRIEF — what every reviewer is told

      The framework's status, accurately stated
      The governing rule: review the inference, not the framework
      The two failure directions, named explicitly
      What is in scope for this review
      What is not

    REVIEW STRUCTURE — what every reviewer completes

      Summary — the claim in the reviewer's words
      Significance — which open question this moves
      Measurement — construct validity, prespecification, resolution
      Adjudication — rival fairness, discriminating condition entered
      Inference — licensed versus claimed
      Disclosure — status representation
      Verdict and ranked revisions

    CALIBRATION

      How divergence between reviewers is handled
      Which divergences are legitimate
      Which indicate reviewer error

    DECISION RULE

      Who decides when reviewers split
      What an editor does with a framework-based rejection

**Say:**
> "The brief matters more than the structure. Most reviewers will never have encountered a pre-validation framework in review and will default to one of the two failures without instruction."

---

## 3. Contest — Can You Review Fairly on Work You Have a Stake In? (45 min)

- **Participant A:** Anyone sufficiently expert to review this work is inside the small community around it and cannot be disinterested.
- **Participant B:** This is the standard small-field problem, addressed by declared interests, structured protocols, and reviewers from adjacent areas.

**Say:**
> "A's concern is sharper here than in most fields because the community is small and the framework has a single author. Your protocol should state how conflicts are declared and what disqualifies. If you can't answer that, the protocol isn't finished."

---

## 4. Apply — Draft Both Documents (50 min)

Participants write the reviewer brief and the review protocol, then test them:

Exchange with a partner. Partner reviews a specimen manuscript **using only the received documents**, with no verbal explanation.

The test: did the documents produce a usable review from someone who did not write them?

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: programs. What a multi-year line of work on this should and shouldn't look like."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The blind test in Apply is the module's real assessment. Protocols that need their author present have failed.
- Conflict declaration deserves real time given the single-author situation.
