# Level H — Module 7: Licensing, Publication, and Derivative Work

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Artifact:** Compliance checklist

## Objective

Participants determine what rights their intended work requires and build a checklist ensuring their group operates within them.

---

## 1. Frame — The Constraint Nobody Checked (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Most of you have been reasoning about this framework for weeks without examining what you are permitted to do with it. That is understandable and it is also exactly how groups end up out of compliance in good faith."

---

## 2. Build — What the Terms Actually Govern (50 min)

Work from the licensing documents directly. Participants read; the instructor does not paraphrase.

**The distinction that matters:**

    FREE GRANT
      Download and run official releases
      Read published source
      Read and study published frameworks
      Use official releases as published

    REQUIRES SUBSCRIPTION
      Fork, modify, adapt, implement, extend
      Create Brisart-Derived Work
      Publish, distribute, host, maintain such work
      Commercialize such work

**The provision most relevant to this room:**

> Academic publications, research papers, and validation studies are treated as derivative work subject to the same subscription requirements as commercial work. Rights exist only while a subscription is active, and there is no grandfathering on lapse.

**Say:**
> "Read that again in the context of a five-year program. Rights that end on lapse are a continuity risk for any multi-year line of work, and it belongs in your program memo's stop conditions."

**Practical consequences to work through:**

- A validation study requires rights before it is published
- Multi-institution collaboration requires all participants covered
- A lapse affects previously published derivative work, not only new work
- Terms may change at renewal; a stated future revenue-sharing provision exists

---

## 3. Contest — Does This Affect the Scientific Question? (45 min)

- **Participant A:** Independent validation gated behind a subscription is a structural problem for the framework's credibility. Validation that must be paid for is not fully independent.
- **Participant B:** The terms govern use, not findings. A subscribed lab can publish a refutation, and the framework's author has no control over the result.

**Say:**
> "Both are right about different things. B is correct that findings aren't controlled. A is correct that the arrangement is unusual and reviewers may treat it as relevant to independence. Your disclosure protocol should probably name the licensing relationship, and that is worth deciding now rather than at submission."

This is a legitimate open issue and the seminar should not resolve it artificially.

---

## 4. Apply — The Compliance Checklist (55 min)

    COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST

    INTENDED ACTIVITIES
      Reading and studying only:                yes / no
      Testing or validation:                    yes / no
      Extension or modification:                yes / no
      Publication of derivative work:           yes / no
      Teaching from published materials as published: yes / no
      Adapted or localized teaching materials:  yes / no

    RIGHTS REQUIRED:
    CURRENT STATUS:
    WHO HOLDS THE SUBSCRIPTION:
    COLLABORATORS AND THEIR STATUS:
    RENEWAL RESPONSIBILITY (role, not name):
    WHAT HAPPENS ON LAPSE — to published work:
    DISCLOSURE OF LICENSING RELATIONSHIP:  where stated

    CHECKPOINT
      Verified before: project initiation / submission / both
      By whom:

**Note for the teaching case:** using official curriculum materials as published falls within the free grant; adapting, localizing, translating, or extending them does not.

**Say:**
> "Final module: your whole portfolio, defended. Bring all six artifacts."
