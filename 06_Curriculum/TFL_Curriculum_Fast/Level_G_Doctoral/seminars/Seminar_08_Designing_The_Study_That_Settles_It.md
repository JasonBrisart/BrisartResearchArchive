# Level G — Seminar 8: Designing the Study That Settles It

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants construct an adjudication proposal capable of moving an open question.

---

## 1. Frame — Moving a Question (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Not 'testing TFL.' Frameworks aren't tested as wholes. Pick one claim, identify the rival it's least separable from, and design the thing that separates them. That is what moving a question looks like."

---

## 2. Build — Proposal Architecture (55 min)

    1. THE OPEN QUESTION
       Stated as a question, not a hypothesis.
       Why it is currently open.

    2. THE CLAIM AND ITS LEAST-SEPARABLE RIVAL
       Both stated in strongest form.
       Where they agree; where they must diverge.

    3. THE DISCRIMINATING CONDITION
       The cell where predictions differ.
       Evidence that the cell is reachable.

    4. MEASUREMENT
       Full map, publication standard.
       Fair operationalization of the rival — stated as its
       proponents would state it.

    5. PREDICTIONS
       For the claim, the rival, and any remaining accounts.
       All must differ.

    6. FALSIFICATION
       Prespecified, controlled, possible, inconsistent.
       Demonstrated firing pathway under the analysis plan.

    7. INFERENCE CEILING
       The highest rung this design licenses.
       What it explicitly does not license.

    8. SCOPE
       One invalid application, formally rejected, boundary named.

**Say:**
> "Item 4's fairness requirement is where proposals are won and lost at review. Have someone who prefers the rival check your operationalization of it."

---

## 3. Contest — Proposal Triage (55 min)

Participants present in five minutes. The room assigns each proposal to one of three verdicts:

    WOULD MOVE THE QUESTION
    WOULD PRODUCE A RESULT BUT NOT MOVE THE QUESTION
    WOULD NOT PRODUCE AN INTERPRETABLE RESULT

Verdicts must be justified against the architecture above, naming the item that fails.

**Say:**
> "The middle verdict is the most common and the most useful. A study can be perfectly executed and still not change anyone's mind, because it never entered the discriminating condition."

---

## 4. Apply — Revision Against Verdict (40 min)

Participants revise the failing item identified by the room, in writing, in session.

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: you referee. Real standard, real verdicts."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Triage verdicts should be recorded. The distribution shifts markedly between Seminar 8 and the Seminar 10 defense and showing that is motivating.
- Enforce the fair-operationalization check. Assign a participant who genuinely prefers the rival wherever possible.
