# Level G — Seminar 10: Defense

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants defend an adjudication proposal against structured challenge and account for their own position across the term.

---

## 1. Frame — Return the Standing Objections (25 min)

Hand back the Seminar 1 objections.

**Say:**
> "Read what you wrote before the audit, before the gating seminar, before you tried to design the study. Mark it: answered, sharpened, abandoned, or standing."

Each participant reports their mark aloud with one sentence.

**Say:**
> "Sharpened and abandoned are both good outcomes. Unchanged after ten weeks usually means the objection was about status rather than substance."

Restate the gate:

> "Two requirements. An executable adjudicating design. A correctly identified invalid application with its boundary named. Both, or the proposal doesn't pass."

---

## 2. Defenses (100 min)

Each participant: 8 minutes presenting, 8 minutes defending.

**Presentation covers, in order:**

    1. The open question and why it is open
    2. The claim and its least-separable rival, both in strongest form
    3. The discriminating condition and evidence it is reachable
    4. Measurement, including fair operationalization of the rival
    5. The falsification condition and its firing pathway
    6. Inference ceiling
    7. One invalid application, rejected

**Challenge roles, assigned and rotating:**

- The **rival's advocate** — argues the design does not separate the accounts
- The **measurement referee** — attacks construct validity and prespecification
- The **inference referee** — attacks the ceiling claim
- The **scope referee** — attacks boundary handling

---

## 3. Close (35 min)

Collect proposals.

**Final discussion, instructor-led:**

> "Where does this room now stand on whether the framework is worth the field's attention? Not whether it's true — whether it's worth testing."

Take a reading around the table. Record the distribution.

**Say:**
> "You arrived asked to evaluate something with no defending literature and no reputational protection. You've produced an audit document, a set of critiques, and proposals that would move pieces of it. None of you know whether TFL is true and that is still the accurate state of the question."

**Say:**
> "What you have is the ability to work at the stage before that's settled. Most of your careers will be spent there."

---

## End of Level G

Participants completing Level G should be able to:

- Conduct and document a specification audit with textual evidence
- Adjudicate against the actual rivals in their own field
- Subject a load-bearing claim to structured attack and determine what resolves it
- Evaluate measurement at publication standard, including fair rival operationalization
- Distinguish licensed inference from claimed inference in both directions
- Produce referee-quality review that examines the manuscript rather than the framework
- Build a proposal capable of moving an open question

Level H begins from these and adds supervision, curriculum design, review-process construction, and institutional responsibility.
