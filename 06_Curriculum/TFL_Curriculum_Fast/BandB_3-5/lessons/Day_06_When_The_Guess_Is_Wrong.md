# Band B — Day 6: When the Guess Is Wrong

**Duration:** 35-40 min
**Materials:** Index cards with a repeating pattern

---

## Objective

Students observe that a broken prediction is not instantly deleted, and often returns.

---

## 1. Hook — The Pattern Break (8 min)

Hold up index cards one at a time in a clear pattern.

```
Cards 1-4:  circle, circle, circle, circle
Card 5:     square
Cards 6-8:  circle, circle, circle
```

Before card 5, ask: **"What's next?"** Everyone says circle.

Show the square. React.

Before card 6, ask again: **"What's next?"**

**Most students will say circle again.**

**Say:**
> "Interesting. It was just wrong. And you went right back to it."

---

## 2. Name — What Breaking Actually Does (8 min)

**Say:**
> "Most people think being wrong erases the prediction. It doesn't work like that."

Write on the chart:

```
A broken prediction can:
  - change a little
  - get replaced
  - come back anyway
```

**Say:**
> "It gets revised or pushed aside. It doesn't get deleted instantly. You just proved it — the square happened and you still said circle."

---

## 3. Work — The Contradiction Log (15 min)

Students run a partner version.

**Setup:** Partner A describes a scenario and stops before the outcome. Partner B states a prediction aloud. Partner A gives an outcome that breaks it. Then Partner A repeats a similar scenario.

**Partner B records:**

```
My first prediction:        ______________
What actually happened:     ______________
My prediction the 2nd time: ______________
Did the old one come back?  Yes / No / Partly
```

Run three rounds, then swap roles.

**Circulate.** The "partly" answers are the most interesting — flag those for the compare step.

---

## 4. Compare — The Return Rate (6 min)

Tally the class: how many old predictions came back at least partly?

Typically a substantial number.

**Ask:**
> "Why would it come back after being proven wrong?"

Accept any reasoning. Steer toward: one break isn't always enough to replace something built from a lot of remembering.

**Say:**
> "Being wrong once starts the change. It usually doesn't finish it."

---

## 5. Carry — Forward (3 min)

**Say:**
> "Last idea: some of these stick around for days and some are gone in a second. Next time we find out what decides that."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The card pattern is reliable but must be unambiguous. Use high-contrast shapes.
- If a class does *not* revert to "circle" after the square, run it again with a longer establishing pattern (six cards before the break).
