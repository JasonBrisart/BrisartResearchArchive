# Band B — Day 7: What Sticks

**Duration:** 30-35 min
**Materials:** Notebooks, chart paper

---

## Objective

Students distinguish predictions that persist from predictions that fade, and separate persistence from correctness.

---

## 1. Quiz 2 (8 min)

Administer Quiz 2.

---

## 2. Hook — Cold Recall (5 min)

Without reviewing anything:

**Say:**
> "What do you still remember from the pattern cards yesterday?"

Then:
> "What do you remember from Day 1 of this unit?"

Tally what survives and what doesn't.

**Say:**
> "Some of this stuck. Some is gone. Today: what decides that."

---

## 3. Name — Sticking (7 min)

Write on the chart:

```
STICKING = it carries into the next moment, and the next

What decides it:  how much it mattered
What does NOT decide it:  whether it was right
```

**Say:**
> "This is the part people get wrong. A prediction that mattered a lot to you will hang around even if it was completely wrong. A prediction that was perfectly correct can vanish in a second."

**Give both examples:**
> "Correct and gone: you predicted the chair would hold you. It did. You forgot immediately."
> "Wrong and stuck: you were sure something was happening today, it didn't, and you're still thinking about it."

---

## 4. Work — The Two-Column Sort (12 min)

Students sort their own recent predictions.

| Stuck with me | Faded fast |
|---|---|
| | |

**Requirement:** at least four entries total, and each one must be marked **R** (was right) or **W** (was wrong).

Then students answer one question in writing:

> Look at your two columns. Does being right or wrong predict which column something landed in?

The answer is no. Students discover it from their own data.

---

## 5. Compare — Class Tally (5 min)

Count the class totals:

```
Stuck + Right:   ____
Stuck + Wrong:   ____
Faded + Right:   ____
Faded + Wrong:   ____
```

All four boxes will have entries.

**Say:**
> "If being right decided sticking, two of these boxes would be empty. They're not. Sticking and being right are two different things."

---

## 6. Carry — Forward (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Last session: we put the whole loop together."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Keep "mattered" as the term. Avoid "emotion," "feelings," "important."
- If a student offers a personally distressing example, acknowledge briefly and redirect to a school or game example. Do not analyze. See `SCOPE_AND_BOUNDARIES.md`.
- The four-box tally is the strongest single demonstration in Band B. Do not skip it.
