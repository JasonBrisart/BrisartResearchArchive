# Micro-Lesson D3: Alternative Attack

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Band:** D (9-12)

---

## Purpose

Practice arguing against TFL using the three alternatives.

## Script

Present a result that appears to support TFL:

> "Students who rated an expectation as high-intensity were more likely to still hold it an hour later."

**Say:**
> "Your job is to destroy this. You get three weapons."

Assign thirds of the class:

**Group 1 — Uncertainty-only**
> "Were the high-intensity items also the more ambiguous ones? If so, unresolved ambiguity explains the persistence."

**Group 2 — Arousal-only**
> "Was intensity just general activation? If any aroused state produces persistence, specific content isn't doing the work."

**Group 3 — Habit-based**
> "Were the persistent items simply more frequently repeated?"

Each group presents its attack.

## The Defense Round

**Ask:**
> "What would the study need to add to survive all three?"

Target answers:
- Ambiguity measured and varied independently of intensity
- Arousal measured separately from content-specific intensity
- Repetition counted and controlled

## Close

> "A valid implementation must permit adjudication against these accounts. If a design can't survive this room, it can't survive review."

## Use

Before Day 9 design work and before the final.
