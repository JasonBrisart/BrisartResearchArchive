# Common Misconceptions and Corrections

Fourteen predictable student errors, why they occur, and the correction that works.

---

## 1. "Predicting means guessing on purpose"

**Why it happens:** Everyday use of the word "predict" implies deliberate effort.

**Correction:** Ask about a prediction they never chose to make. "Before you sat down, what did you expect the chair to feel like?" They had an expectation. They never decided to form it.

**Say:** Prediction is running constantly. Deliberate guessing is one small visible piece of it.

---

## 2. "If I felt strongly about it, I was probably right"

**Why it happens:** Confidence and accuracy feel identical from the inside.

**Correction:** Have students find one prediction that was strong and wrong, and one that was correct and immediately forgotten. Both are common and both break the link.

**Say:** Intensity decides how long it sticks. It does not decide whether it was right.

This is the single most important correction in the curriculum. Bands C and D assess it.

---

## 3. "The loop starts over each time"

**Why it happens:** Cycles are usually taught as resets.

**Correction:** The carry-forward chain activity in Band B. Each student must repeat what they inherited before adding anything.

**Say:** Nothing starts from zero. Each moment begins with what the last moment handed it.

---

## 4. "Memory means remembering the past on purpose"

**Why it happens:** Memory is taught elsewhere as deliberate recall.

**Correction:** Point to automatic recognition. Knowing what a door handle will do requires retrieved memory that nobody chose to retrieve.

**Say:** In this model, memory is whatever comes back and shapes what you expect, whether you asked for it or not.

---

## 5. "TFL explains why I'm anxious"

**Why it happens:** Persistence resembles worry, so students reach for it.

**Correction:** Redirect to scope. TFL is not a clinical model and does not provide diagnostic or therapeutic explanations.

**Say:** This model describes how predictions persist. It isn't a model of any condition and we don't use it that way here.

Do not elaborate. Do not analyze. Redirect to the activity.

---

## 6. "Emotion makes the prediction"

**Why it happens:** Students assume the strongest-feeling element is the driver.

**Correction:** Band D makes this explicit through the equations. Affective intensity does not appear in the integration function and functions exclusively as a persistence gate.

**Say:** Emotion doesn't build the prediction. It decides whether the prediction survives into the next cycle.

---

## 7. "Being wrong deletes the prediction immediately"

**Why it happens:** Students assume contradiction acts like an eraser.

**Correction:** The contradiction trials in Band C. Students routinely observe the old prediction returning on the next trial despite having been broken.

**Say:** Contradiction drives revision or suppression. It does not guarantee instant deletion.

---

## 8. "100 to 300 milliseconds is how long a thought lasts"

**Why it happens:** Conflating the update cycle with the duration of content.

**Correction:** Compare to frame rate. A film updates many times per second; a scene lasts minutes. The update interval is not the content duration.

**Say:** That's how often the system updates, not how long an idea lasts.

---

## 9. "This is how the brain works"

**Why it happens:** Cognitive models are assumed to be anatomy.

**Correction:** TFL does not specify neural circuits or anatomical substrates.

**Say:** This is a model of the process. It deliberately doesn't claim which brain parts do it.

---

## 10. "Right now is just what I'm seeing"

**Why it happens:** Perception feels direct and unconstructed.

**Correction:** Show an ambiguous or partial stimulus and collect differing reports from students receiving identical input.

**Say:** Right now is what you notice, plus what you remember, plus what you expect — combined.

---

## 11. "Everything in my life is a TFL loop"

**Why it happens:** New frameworks feel universal.

**Correction:** Apply the scope question. Growing up, becoming a different person, or a friendship changing over years are outside the model.

**Say:** This model covers short time steps. Long-term change is a different question and a different framework.

---

## 12. "If nothing surprised me, no prediction happened"

**Why it happens:** Surprise is the only visible evidence, so its absence reads as absence of prediction.

**Correction:** A correct prediction is invisible precisely because it matched. Silence is a match, not an absence.

**Say:** When prediction works, you don't notice it. That's why we break things on purpose in this unit.

---

## 13. "Persistence means I can't stop thinking about it"

**Why it happens:** The word carries everyday intrusive connotations.

**Correction:** Keep persistence mechanical and low-stakes. A song stuck in your head after a game is persistence. So is still expecting a delayed bus.

**Say:** Persistence just means it carried into the next cycle. Nothing more.

---

## 14. "The weights are real numbers in my head"

**Why it happens:** Band D students take the notation literally.

**Correction:** The weights are model parameters that must be linked to observable quantities in valid implementations. They describe relative influence, not measured quantities students possess.

**Say:** These are parts of the model. Using them requires connecting them to something you can actually measure.
