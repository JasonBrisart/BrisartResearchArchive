## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — The Question Mark Audit.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every student marks at least one line with a question mark
- **Lesson 18:** every student ranks all five and justifies the weakest
- **Lesson 19:** every student strengthens at least one line with external evidence
- **Lesson 20:** every student names a resolving observation
- **Lesson 21:** every student receives an audit naming at least one unmarked gap
- **Lesson 22:** every student repairs every marked line
- **Lesson 23:** every student has two checked reconstructions with evidence throughout
- **Lesson 24:** the class audits at least four correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | invent evidence to avoid question marks | Praise question marks explicitly: I am looking for them. |
| 18 | rank by how sure they feel | Ask what can be pointed at for each line. |
| 19 | assume their partner's account confirms theirs | Require the partner's actual words, written down. |
| 20 | discard the inconvenient account | Require both in the write-up. |
| 21 | return an audit finding nothing | Require one per reconstruction. There always is one. |
| 22 | defend rather than repair | Allow one defence if they can point at evidence now. |
| 23 | ask which moment to use | Give a category, not an example: something from break. |
| 24 | skip the question-mark convention | Re-run the pointing test on every unsupported line. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
