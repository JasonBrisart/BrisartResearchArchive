## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — The Whole Structure.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student's testability section names what the change would absorb
- **Lesson 26:** every student produces a genuine argument for the other side
- **Lesson 27:** every student ranks six arguments with criteria cited
- **Lesson 28:** every student answers three challenges specifically
- **Lesson 29:** every student writes the structure unaided and defends a claim
- **Lesson 30:** every student compares an early and a late argument
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | argue from intuition about emotion | Redirect to structure: not whether it feels right. What it costs. |
| 26 | write a deliberately weak opposing case | Have a partner judge whether it is genuinely the best case. |
| 27 | judge by which conclusion they agree with | Cover the conclusions and judge the reasoning only. |
| 28 | defend by restating the claim | Require engagement: they asked what it costs. Answer that. |
| 29 | defend a claim by restating it | Require engagement with the specific question asked. |
| 30 | cannot find early work | One folder from Lesson 1. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | state closure without its cost | The structural argument is the Grade 10 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
