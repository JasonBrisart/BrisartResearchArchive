## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Comparing Myself To Myself.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every student has a completed nine-card tally
- **Lesson 18:** the class has a pooled number greater than one
- **Lesson 19:** every student writes both explanations
- **Lesson 20:** every student frames one change as an update
- **Lesson 21:** every student names a cause
- **Lesson 22:** every student runs a trial and records the number
- **Lesson 23:** every student names a cause for a failed trial
- **Lesson 24:** the class cites the record rather than an impression

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | change answers after seeing the card | Collect written expectations face-down before each reveal. |
| 18 | report a number without a record | Ask for the table. A number with no table is a guess. |
| 19 | pick one explanation confidently | Ask what they would have to measure to tell them apart. |
| 20 | describe their earlier self as stupid | Correct it firmly: you were right with what you had. |
| 21 | claim nothing changed | Narrow it: pick something we ran twice. |
| 22 | establish the pattern only once | Three repetitions minimum. Send it back otherwise. |
| 23 | hide the failed trial | Ask for them publicly and treat them as the interesting cases. |
| 24 | quote a number without the record | Require the tally sheet every time a number is quoted. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
