## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Answering The Audit.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every student addresses all seven rows with reasons
- **Lesson 18:** every student produces an independent manipulation
- **Lesson 19:** every student names a direction for the bias in their own logs
- **Lesson 20:** every student receives three attacks, one per rival
- **Lesson 21:** every student revises against at least two attacks
- **Lesson 22:** every student names a resolving observation
- **Lesson 23:** the four predictions differ from each other
- **Lesson 24:** the class finds uncontrolled confounds in at least four designs

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | mark most confounds not applicable | Require a written reason for each dismissal. |
| 18 | vary them together and not notice | Trace the manipulation step by step with them. |
| 19 | claim they logged everything | Ask what they did during a boring hour. Nothing is logged there. |
| 20 | defend in real time | Enforce the rule: designers write during the attack and speak at the end. |
| 21 | dismiss all three attacks | Require at least one substantive revision. |
| 22 | discard the inconvenient result | Require both in the write-up. |
| 23 | present four functionally identical predictions | Read them back aloud: those three say the same thing. |
| 24 | dismiss confounds without reasons | Require a reason for every dismissal. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
