## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining Measurement.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student lists four with requirements
- **Lesson 26:** every student states honestly whether estimation is feasible
- **Lesson 27:** every student names real excluded conclusions
- **Lesson 28:** every write-up contains all four elements
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** every student compares the first and latest map
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | claim everything is measurable in principle | Ask for the equipment, the access, and the timescale. |
| 26 | propose estimating from a collinear design | Walk the collinearity: your prediction comes from the memory. How do you separate them? |
| 27 | write generic limitations | Require specifics: name the conclusion, not the word limitations. |
| 28 | omit the alternatives | Return any write-up without them, unmarked. |
| 29 | present measurement as straightforward | Require the failure modes in the explanation. |
| 30 | cannot find early work | One folder from Lesson 1. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | state rivals in weakened form | Fair operationalisation is the Grade 12 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
