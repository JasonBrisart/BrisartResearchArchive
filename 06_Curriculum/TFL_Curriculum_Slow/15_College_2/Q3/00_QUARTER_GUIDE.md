## The pivot session of this quarter

**Session 18 — Critiquing Your Own Protocol.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Session 15:** the second run produces fewer questions
- **Session 16:** every critique names at least six specific failures
- **Session 17:** every student states a claim the design actually licenses
- **Session 18:** every student names at least four failures in their own work
- **Session 19:** every declaration names real excluded conclusions
- **Session 20:** every proposal states its assumptions
- **Session 21:** every package contains all six components

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Session | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | defend the original wording | Point at the evidence: somebody asked. |
| 16 | critique the framework rather than the study | Redirect: does this study support what it claims? |
| 17 | conclude the study says nothing | Press: it says something. What, exactly? |
| 18 | find nothing wrong with their own | Require four. There always are. |
| 19 | write generic limitations | Require specifics: name the conclusion. |
| 20 | propose a measure without naming assumptions | Require them explicitly for each. |
| 21 | omit the resolution declaration | Check for it explicitly in every audit. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
