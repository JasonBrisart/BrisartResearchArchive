## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — Why Invisible.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every student lists at least eight unnoticed expectations
- **Lesson 2:** every student applies the test to at least eight items
- **Lesson 3:** every student ranks ten and justifies three
- **Lesson 4:** every student produces ten outside-school items
- **Lesson 5:** every student's explanation survives three cases
- **Lesson 6:** every student produces the three-step argument
- **Lesson 7:** every student produces five from other subjects
- **Lesson 8:** the class applies the test correctly to at least four

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | say nothing happened so there is nothing to list | Offer categories: doors, chairs, floors, switches, your own voice. |
| 2 | apply the test to something that would not surprise | Calibrate: would that really surprise you? |
| 3 | rank by importance rather than surprise | Ask which would make them jump, not which matters most. |
| 4 | reuse school examples | Require a location for each: kitchen, bus, street. |
| 5 | say correct expectations are unimportant | Distinguish: not unimportant, unnoticeable. Different thing. |
| 6 | assert it without the three steps | Require all three steps, numbered. |
| 7 | think it only applies here | Do the first one aloud from another subject. |
| 8 | skip the break test | Require it on every claimed expectation. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
