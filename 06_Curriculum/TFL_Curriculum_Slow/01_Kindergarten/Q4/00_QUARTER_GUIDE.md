## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — The Guess Jar Opens.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every child completes the surprise frame
- **Lesson 26:** each child names a stuck and a gone thing
- **Lesson 27:** each child has three connected boxes, not three unrelated ones
- **Lesson 28:** every group walks the second lap and can say why
- **Lesson 29:** every child compares their own recorded guess
- **Lesson 30:** every child attempts the full explanation
- **Lesson 31:** every child places at least six items
- **Lesson 32:** each child demonstrates all four outcomes

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | say only it was weird | Push once: weird compared to what? |
| 26 | say it stuck because it was correct | Find a counter-example together. Someone always has one. |
| 27 | draw three unrelated pictures | Point at the middle box and ask what it makes them guess. |
| 28 | stop after one lap | Send them again explicitly. |
| 29 | have forgotten they made it | Show them their own recorded drawing. The record does the work. |
| 30 | recite without meaning | Ask the listener for one question. The question is the check. |
| 31 | cannot recall early terms | The kept work does the remembering. |
| 32 | skip the harder parts | Use a four-picture checklist. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
