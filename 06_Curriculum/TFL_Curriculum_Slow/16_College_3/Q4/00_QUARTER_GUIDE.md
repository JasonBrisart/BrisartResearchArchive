## The pivot session of this quarter

**Session 25 — The Year In Comparisons.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Session 22:** every student names the failing requirement for three cases
- **Session 23:** a reader cannot infer the author's preference
- **Session 24:** every student ranks three requirements
- **Session 25:** every student compares first and latest
- **Session 26:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Session 27:** every student has worked on two identified weaknesses
- **Session 28:** all five outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Session | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | diagnose them as badly executed | Point at execution quality: it ran perfectly. That is not the problem. |
| 23 | signal their preference | Run the reader test with an actual reader. |
| 24 | answer with a verdict | Redirect to the settling conditions. |
| 25 | cannot find early work | One folder from Session 1. |
| 26 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 27 | review what they already know | Require the two weakest from the practice paper. |
| 28 | state rivals in weakened form | Fair operationalisation is the Year 4 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
