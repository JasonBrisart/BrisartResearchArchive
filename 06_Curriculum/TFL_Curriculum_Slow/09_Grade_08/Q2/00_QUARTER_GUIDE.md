## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — Dynamics In Data.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** every student names what somebody else would have expected
- **Lesson 10:** every student traces three beliefs to sources
- **Lesson 11:** every student sorts at least nine correctly and names the criterion
- **Lesson 12:** every student classifies at least six with conditions named
- **Lesson 13:** every student classifies three real datasets
- **Lesson 14:** every description is of system behaviour, not a person
- **Lesson 15:** every student produces three non-personal examples
- **Lesson 16:** the class classifies at least seven correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | describe Belief(t) as overriding the input entirely | Test it: if I show you something unambiguous, does belief change what you see? |
| 10 | treat beliefs as given | Ask what would have to happen for the belief to change. |
| 11 | treat them as the same thing at different sizes | Use the timescale test explicitly. |
| 12 | read escalation as describing a person | Restate the boundary firmly and reject any personal example. |
| 13 | force a dynamic onto ambiguous data | Allow cannot tell at this rate as a full-credit answer. |
| 14 | slip into describing a classmate | Reject the example outright and supply a mechanical one. |
| 15 | return to personal examples | Require weather, traffic, games, or queues. |
| 16 | name the dynamic without the condition | Require the condition every time. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
