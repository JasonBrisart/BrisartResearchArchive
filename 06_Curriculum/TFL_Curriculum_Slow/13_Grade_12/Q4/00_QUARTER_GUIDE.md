## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining Falsification.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student passes a peer gate check on both items
- **Lesson 26:** every student records their unanswered questions
- **Lesson 27:** every unanswered question produces a revision
- **Lesson 28:** every student produces a genuine argument against
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** both gates are satisfied and all four roles answered
- **Lesson 31:** every student knows whether they would currently pass the gates
- **Lesson 32:** both gates are satisfied

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | assemble seven parts and leave a gate | Run the gate check first. |
| 26 | defend by restating the design | Redirect: they asked what it cannot see. Answer that. |
| 27 | dismiss the questions as unfair | Require a revision for each one. |
| 28 | produce a weak case because they believe the opposite | Judge on reasoning with conclusions covered. |
| 29 | present falsification as pessimism | Reframe: it is what makes the claim mean something. |
| 30 | present advocacy before the condition | Stop the presentation: condition first. Start again. |
| 31 | ignore the gate items in practice | Mark the gates first, as the real one will be marked. |
| 32 | run out of time before the gate items | Instruct them to answer the gate items first. Say so before they start. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
