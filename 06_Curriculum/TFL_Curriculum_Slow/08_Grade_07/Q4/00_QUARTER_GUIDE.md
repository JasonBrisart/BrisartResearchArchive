## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Distinction.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student produces all five parts with genuine content
- **Lesson 26:** every student answers three challenges specifically
- **Lesson 27:** every student revises at least one part and says why
- **Lesson 28:** every student states what would have to be measured
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** every student names one thing in their own data that surprised them
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | omit the poorly fitting case to make it cleaner | Require it: the clean version is the weaker one. |
| 26 | defend by restating the claim | Require engagement: they asked about the awkward case. Answer that. |
| 27 | refuse to change anything | Require one revision. At least one challenge always lands. |
| 28 | invent a confident answer | Name it as genuinely open and say so plainly. |
| 29 | explain it as emotions make things stick | Require the accuracy half: and what does it say about being right? |
| 30 | summarise without examining | Require a specific surprise with the entry number. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | cannot produce the four-box demonstration without the log | The log is required equipment. Say so from Lesson 1. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
