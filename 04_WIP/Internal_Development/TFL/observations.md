## Persistence & threshold behavior

- Simulation persistence does not appear linear with affective intensity. There may be threshold behavior where persistence sharply increases after a certain point rather than scaling gradually.
- Decay of affect does not necessarily correspond directly to decay of simulation persistence, suggesting that persistence may have inertia independent of moment-to-moment E(t).
- Some persistence effects may occur even in the absence of strong affective signals, suggesting either measurement limitations or additional variables influencing persistence.
- The transition from persistence to resolution may not be smooth; it may occur as a discrete shift rather than gradual decay.

## Contradiction, belief & confirmation

- Some simulations seem to remain active even after partial contradiction, suggesting that contradiction strength interacts with belief structure rather than directly suppressing the simulation.
- Contradiction does not always produce immediate suppression; in some cases it appears to trigger reinterpretation, suggesting a bifurcation between revision and suppression processes.
- Contradiction effects appear asymmetric; strong confirmation strengthens persistence more reliably than weak contradiction weakens it.
- The interaction between belief manipulation and affective intensity may produce nonlinear effects that are not captured by simple additive models.

## Reentry & cue overlap

- Recurrence of simulations appears dependent not only on prior affect but also on contextual similarity, implying that cue overlap may function as a secondary gating mechanism.
- Delayed reentry of simulations may depend on both initial affective intensity and the presence of partial contextual cues, rather than simple reactivation.

## Memory & retrieval

- Affective intensity may not only sustain simulations but also bias which memory content is retrieved, indicating a coupling between E(t) and Minfo(t) selection rather than purely persistence.
- Memory retrieval may not be neutral; it appears to preferentially select content that is consistent with the current simulation, reinforcing persistence loops.

## Simulation dynamics

- There may be cases where simulations persist even when they are not currently dominant in conscious report, suggesting latent or subthreshold activity within the loop.
- Multiple competing simulations may be active across adjacent windows, even if only one is reported, indicating a potential need to model competitive dynamics rather than single-state dominance.
- Simulations with conflicting content may alternate across windows rather than resolving immediately, suggesting oscillatory or competitive loop behavior.
- There may be a critical difference between externally driven and internally generated simulations in how they persist and resolve.
- Repeated exposure to the same ambiguity condition may either reinforce or weaken simulation persistence depending on prior outcomes, suggesting learning effects within the loop.

## Ambiguity & uncertainty

- Under high ambiguity, simulations appear to influence perception more strongly, suggesting that Input(t) uncertainty increases the weighting of Sim(t) in integration.

## Simulation vs. belief distinction

- The distinction between simulation and belief may be operational rather than structural, with belief emerging from stabilized, high-persistence simulations.

## Measuring simulation strength

- Simulation strength may be better indexed by behavioral bias than by self-report, suggesting that introspective measures alone are insufficient.
