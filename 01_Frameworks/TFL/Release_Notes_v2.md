# Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) Release Notes

The Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) is a recursive, affect-modulated predictive simulation framework formalized as a discrete-time loop over sensory input, memory retrieval, affective state, and internal simulation.

This release sequence documents the progression from early conceptual formulations to a structurally defined, experimentally tractable system with a fully specified baseline implementation.

## Major Framework Characteristics

- Conscious processing is modeled as a recursive loop over `Input(t) → Minfo(t) → E(t) → Sim(t) → F(t)`.
- Affective intensity (`E(t)`) governs simulation persistence and recurrence.
- Predictive simulations bias behavioral response under conditions of ambiguity.
- Contradiction induces measurable revision or suppression of active simulations.
- The framework produces testable predictions grounded in observable variables.

## Release Progression

### Versions 1.0 to 3.1

**Status:** Early conceptual and formalization stages

**Changes:**

- Initial formulation of recursive temporal integration.
- Development of loop-based descriptions of simulation persistence and reentry.
- Early articulation of predictions related to rumination, perceptual bias, and affective modulation.

### Version 4.0: June 13, 2025

**Status:** First fully formalized version

**Changes:**

- Introduced complete symbolic model: `Input(t)`, `Minfo(t)`, `E(t)`, `Sim(t)`, and `F(t)`.
- Defined four-phase loop structure: Encoding, Integration, Propagation, and Resolution.
- Established the temporal window of approximately 100 to 300 ms as an operational constraint.
- Expanded neurobiological grounding.

### Version 4.1: June 16, 2025

**Status:** Structural refinement and licensing transition

**Changes:**

- Introduced formal licensing framework (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0).
- Clarified loop architecture and symbolic variable definitions.
- Expanded treatment of simulation persistence, resolution, and affective modulation.
- Added structured case examples and testable predictions.

### Version 4.2: June 23 to 24, 2025

**Status:** Latest public archival release; integrated into Brisart Research Archive

**Changes:**

- Rewrote abstract to conform to Brisart Format structure.
- Introduced system-level integration with PFT, SST, IRE, TCRF, VRIF, and RIET.
- Positioned TFL as a short-timescale simulation engine within the broader architecture.
- Clarified its role in perceptual stabilization and interpretive resolution.
- Formalized Archive-based access and distribution.

### Institutional Edition: April 2026

**Status:** Implementation-ready structural release

**Changes:**

- Added a complete baseline reference implementation with a fixed trial structure, predefined variables and measurement roles, and explicit input/output mappings.
- Introduced parameter identification constraints linking persistence, decay, and update to observable data.
- Standardized experimental requirements, including manipulation structure, measurement channels, and adjudication criteria.
- Consolidated the framework as an experimentally testable system rather than a purely conceptual model.

## Current Status

TFL is positioned as:

- A formally defined recursive simulation framework.
- A behaviorally testable model grounded in observable variables.
- A structured system supporting experimental implementation and model comparison.

The framework remains extensible in:

- Neural mapping and measurement resolution.
- Experimental validation across paradigms.
- Integration with broader cognitive architectures.

## Summary

Across this release sequence, TFL evolved from:

```text
conceptual formulation
→ loop-based structure
→ formal symbolic system
→ experimentally specified framework
```

The central claim remains:

> Predictive simulations are recursively maintained, modulated by affect, and resolved through feedback-driven updating within discrete temporal windows.
