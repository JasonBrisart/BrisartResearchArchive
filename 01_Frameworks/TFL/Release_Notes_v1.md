# Temporal Feedback Loop (TFL) Release Notes

This section summarizes the development history of the Temporal Feedback Loop framework, combining the archival Zenodo record with the Institutional Edition.

## Institutional Edition: April 2026

**Status:** Implementation-ready institutional release

**Changes:**

- Added a complete plug-and-play reference implementation with default parameters, clearly specified inputs and outputs, and example update rules.
- Reframed TFL explicitly as a substrate for experimental operationalization, clinical hypothesis generation, and computational modeling.
- Clarified measurement targets such as persistence thresholds on `E(t)`, congruence between `Sim(t)` and `Input(t)`, and memory-alignment constraints on `Minfo(t)`.

## Version 4.2: June 23 to 24, 2025

**Status:** Latest public archival release; integrated into the Brisart Research Archive

**Changes:**

- Rewrote the abstract to align with Brisart Format structural requirements.
- Added a full system-level integration description linking TFL to PFT, SST, IRE, TCRF, VRIF, and RIET.
- Expanded the conceptual framing of TFL as the "memory-feedback engine" of the cognitive architecture.
- Clarified TFL's role in stabilizing perception, meaning, and interpretation.
- Updated licensing to CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 and formalized Archive-based access.

## Version 4.1: June 16, 2025

**Status:** Licensing transition and structural refinement

**Changes:**

- Introduced the formal licensing shift to CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 for all versions.
- Clarified the recursive loop architecture and symbolic variables.
- Expanded the explanation of simulation persistence, observational resolution, and affective modulation.
- Added structured case studies and testable predictions.

## Version 4.0: June 13, 2025

**Status:** First fully formalized version

**Changes:**

- Introduced the complete symbolic model: `Input(t)`, `Minfo(t)`, `E(t)`, `Sim(t)`, and `F(t)`.
- Introduced the four-phase loop structure: Encoding, Integration, Propagation, and Resolution.
- Added the approximately 100 to 300 ms temporal window as a core architectural constraint.
- Expanded neurobiological grounding.

## Versions 3.1, 3.0, 2.0, and 1.0

**Status:** Early conceptual and formalization stages

**Changes:**

- Progressive development from initial articulation of recursive temporal integration, through pseudo-formal loop descriptions, to early predictions about identity, rumination, and perceptual bias.
