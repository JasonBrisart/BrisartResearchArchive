# Band B — Day 4: The Chain

**Duration:** 30-35 min
**Materials:** One small object to pass

---

## Objective

Students physically demonstrate inheritance and experience the effort it requires.

---

## 1. Quiz 1 (8 min)

Administer Quiz 1 if not given on Day 3.

---

## 2. Hook — Set Up the Chain (3 min)

Students form a circle. Hold up the object.

**Say:**
> "Rule: when you get this, you repeat everything you heard, then add one thing. No shortcuts."

---

## 3. Work — The Carry-Forward Chain (15 min)

**Round 1 — Simple chain**

Student 1 states one observation.
Student 2 repeats it, then adds an expectation based on it.
Student 3 repeats both, adds one more.

Continue around. It gets hard fast. That is the point.

When it breaks down — and it will — stop.

**Say:**
> "Notice what just happened. Carrying forward takes work. And you're doing it out loud with help. Your brain does it constantly without either."

**Round 2 — Broken inheritance**

Restart. This time, secretly instruct one student to add something completely unrelated.

When the chain reaches them, the disruption is obvious.

**Ask:**
> "What happened there?"

**Say:**
> "That one started from nothing. The chain broke. That's the thing that doesn't happen in real life — even when your expectation is wrong, it still came from somewhere."

---

## 4. Compare — Why It Got Harder (6 min)

**Ask:**
> "Why did the chain get harder as it went?"

Steer toward: more to carry.

**Ask the better question:**
> "But your brain carries stuff forward all day and doesn't get tired like that. What's different?"

Let them speculate. Accept any reasonable answer. The productive one:

> "You were carrying it *on purpose*. Normally it happens without you doing anything."

---

## 5. Carry — Forward (3 min)

**Say:**
> "Next time: what happens when your expectation turns out to be exactly right. Here's a hint — you usually don't notice at all."

---

## Homework

Assign **Homework Set 2**.

## Instructor Notes

- The chain breaking is a feature. Do not rescue it too early.
- For large classes, run two chains of 8-10 students in parallel.
- If a student is anxious about performing in a circle, let them hold a written record and read it rather than recall it.
