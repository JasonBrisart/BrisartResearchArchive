# Band B — Day 5: When the Guess Is Right

**Duration:** 30-35 min
**Materials:** Notebooks

---

## Objective

Students recognize that correct predictions are invisible, and that silence means a match.

---

## 1. Hook — The Nothing Moment (5 min)

Do something completely routine and expected. Walk to the board. Pick up a marker. Write the date.

**Say:**
> "Was anybody surprised just now?"

No.

**Say:**
> "So nothing happened, right?"

Let them agree.

**Say:**
> "Wrong. You made about ten predictions in the last twenty seconds and every single one was correct. You didn't notice a single one of them."

---

## 2. Name — Matching (8 min)

Write on the chart:

```
Prediction matches  →  you notice nothing
Prediction breaks   →  you notice everything
```

**Say:**
> "This is a strange feature of the whole system. When it's working perfectly, it's completely invisible."

**Say:**
> "That's why we break things on purpose in this unit. It's the only way to see the machine running."

---

## 3. Work — The Invisible Prediction Hunt (15 min)

Students reconstruct predictions they made without noticing.

**Task:** Pick three routine moments from today. For each, write the prediction you must have made.

**Template:**

```
Routine moment:              ______________________
The prediction I didn't notice making: ______________
How I know I made it:        ______________________
```

The third line is the reasoning. Model it:

> "Moment: I sat in my chair. Prediction I didn't notice: the chair would hold me. How I know: because if it hadn't, I'd have been extremely surprised."

**That's the test.** If breaking it would surprise you, the prediction was there.

---

## 4. Compare — The Surprise Test (7 min)

Collect three student examples. For each, ask the class:

> "If that had gone differently, would they have been surprised?"

Yes → the prediction was real.

**Say:**
> "That's how you prove an invisible prediction existed. Imagine breaking it."

---

## 5. Carry — Forward (3 min)

**Say:**
> "Tomorrow: what actually happens when a prediction gets broken. It's not what most people think."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Students often struggle to find routine predictions because routine is by definition unremarkable. Offer categories: doors, chairs, stairs, light switches, the sound of your own footsteps.
- This lesson directly addresses Misconception 12 ("if nothing surprised me, no prediction happened"). Reference `COMMON_MISCONCEPTIONS.md`.
