# Band D — Day 11: Scope and Invalid Application

**Duration:** 50 min

---

## Objective

Students identify invalid applications and explain the basis for rejection.

---

## 1. Hook — Four Proposals (8 min)

Present four study proposals. Students judge validity.

```
1. Measuring whether stated predictions persist across
   a 20-minute task with rated intensity.

2. Using TFL to explain why a student's personality
   changed between 9th and 12th grade.

3. Testing whether contradicting feedback revises a
   stated expectation within a session.

4. Using TFL to identify which students have
   attention difficulties.
```

Valid: 1 and 3. Invalid: 2 and 4.

**Say:**
> "Proposal 4 isn't just outside scope. It's a misuse. The framework provides no diagnostic content and doesn't support that inference."

---

## 2. Name — The Rejection Criteria (13 min)

Write the full exclusion set:

```
NOT DEFINED TO MODEL
  long-term memory consolidation
  identity formation
  narrative construction
  developmental trajectories
  group-level or social-level dynamics

DOES NOT SPECIFY
  neural circuits, anatomy, neurotransmitters,
  oscillatory signatures

DOES NOT CLAIM
  what consciousness is
  how subjective experience arises

DOES NOT PROVIDE
  diagnostic criteria
  therapeutic mechanisms
  explanations of psychopathology

DOES NOT ATTRIBUTE
  agency, volition, goals, or selfhood
```

**Say:**
> "Simulations are computational hypotheses. Not intentions. Not wishes. Not choices. That distinction matters when people start describing predictions as things the system 'wants'."

---

## 3. Work — Rejection Writing (21 min)

Students write formal rejections for three invalid proposals.

```
PROPOSAL: __________________________________

REJECTED BECAUSE: __________________________

WHICH BOUNDARY: ____________________________

WHAT FRAMEWORK WOULD BE APPROPRIATE INSTEAD:
____________________________________________
```

The last line requires recognizing that exclusion isn't dismissal — it points to a different level of analysis.

---

## 4. Compare — The Hardest Case (6 min)

Discuss a genuinely borderline proposal — for example, a study of expectations over a single school day.

**Ask:**
> "Is one day short-timescale?"

Let them argue. The productive resolution: it depends on whether identifiable simulation content can be tracked across windows, or whether the claim is really about the day as a whole.

---

## 5. Carry (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Final session. You'll design a study, state what would kill it, and correctly reject something. All three are required."
