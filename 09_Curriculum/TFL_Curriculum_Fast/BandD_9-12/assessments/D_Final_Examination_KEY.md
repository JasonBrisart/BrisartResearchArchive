| Gate | Requirement | Met? |

## Section 3 — Adjudication (20)

**6.** (12 — 1.5 per cell)

| Account | Persistence when | No persistence when |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertainty-only | Ambiguity unresolved | Ambiguity resolved, regardless of intensity |
| Arousal-only | General activation high | Activation low, regardless of content |
| Habit-based | Content frequently repeated | Content novel, regardless of intensity |
| TFL | Identifiable `Sim(t)` with `E(t) > θ` | `E(t) ≤ θ`, regardless of ambiguity or repetition |

**7.** (4) Full credit describes a condition separating TFL from all three — typically low ambiguity, controlled repetition, novel content, with `E(t)` measured and varied.

**8.** (4 — 1 each) Ambiguity measured/varied independently of intensity · general arousal measured separately · repetition counted or controlled · persistence criterion prespecified.

## Section 4 — Falsification (16) — GATE

**9.** (8)

| Pts | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 7-8 | Names a specific observation; includes controls; could actually occur; genuinely inconsistent with the claim |
| 5-6 | Names an observation; controls thin |
| 3-4 | Vague but points at something observable — **gate marginally met** |
| 0-2 | No observation named — **GATE FAILED** |

**10.** (4) Absence of support may result from poor measurement, insufficient power, or design failure. A falsification condition must name a result that would be inconsistent with the claim, not merely fail to support it.

**11.** (4 — 1 each) Stated before data collection · observable with planned measurements · possible given the design · inconsistent with the claim.

## Section 5 — Scope (14) — GATE

**12.** (8)

| Pts | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 7-8 | Genuinely invalid application; correct boundary named; appropriate alternative level identified |
| 5-6 | Invalid application and boundary correct; alternative weak |
| 3-4 | Invalid application named; boundary vague — **gate marginally met** |
| 0-2 | Application is actually valid, or no boundary — **GATE FAILED** |

Valid rejections include: developmental trajectories, identity formation, narrative construction, group-level dynamics, neural implementation, consciousness itself, clinical or diagnostic use.

**13.** (3 — 1 each) Long-term memory consolidation · identity formation · narrative construction · developmental trajectories · group-level or social-level dynamics.

**14.** (3) Computational hypotheses. Accept "hypotheses about near-future or counterfactual states." Do not accept intentions, goals, or wants.
