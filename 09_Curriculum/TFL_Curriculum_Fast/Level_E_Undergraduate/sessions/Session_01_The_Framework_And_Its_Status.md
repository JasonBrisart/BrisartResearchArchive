# Level E — Session 1: The Framework and Its Status

**Duration:** 75 min

## Objective

Students understand what TFL claims, what its epistemic status is, and why that status structures the entire course.

---

## 1. Frame — The Uncomfortable Opening (12 min)

Write both relations on the permanent board before students arrive. Say nothing about them yet.

**Say:**
> "This course is about that. Before we start, you need to know its status, because it changes what we're doing here."

Deliver the required disclosure. Do not soften it.

> "TFL is an original framework. It is specified well enough to be tested and to be wrong. As far as the published literature goes, it has not been independently validated. We're going to study it the way you would study any serious proposal that hasn't been through that process."

Let the room react. Someone will ask why they're studying it.

**Say:**
> "Because that's where most research actually lives. Every framework you've been taught as settled was once exactly here. You've never been shown one at this stage before it was decided."

---

## 2. Build — What the Framework Claims (25 min)

Give the claim in plain language before any notation.

> "Experience updates in short repeating steps. In each step, what you're sensing, what you're remembering, and what you're predicting combine into your current state. That state becomes the starting condition for the next step."

Then the fifth variable:

> "There's one more. How much something matters — affective intensity. And here's the distinctive part: it doesn't help build the prediction. It only decides whether the prediction survives into the next step."

Now point at the board and name the five: `Input(t)`, `Minfo(t)`, `Sim(t)`, `E(t)`, `F(t)`.

**Ask:**
> "Look at the first relation. Which of the five is missing?"

`E(t)`.

**Say:**
> "Hold that. Session 7 is about that absence, and it's the most falsifiable thing in the framework."

---

## 3. Contest — First Objections (20 min)

Solicit objections immediately. Do not defer them.

Write every objection on the board without answering. Expect:
- "How would you measure any of this?"
- "Isn't this just predictive processing?"
- "Who says it's 100-300 milliseconds?"
- "If the author defines the terms, how can it be wrong?"

**Say:**
> "Every one of these is a session. Sessions 5, 11, 4, and 13 respectively. Keep your own objection and check at the end whether the course answered it."

Have each student write their strongest objection on an index card, name attached. Collect. Return these at Session 13.

---

## 4. Apply — The First Trace (13 min)

Individually, students reconstruct one moment from the past hour using the five components in plain language only. No notation yet.

    The moment:
    What I was sensing:
    What came back from memory:
    What I expected next:
    How much it mattered:  high / low
    The whole moment felt like:

**Circulate.** The expectation line is where errors appear. Push for specific content, not mood.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "You just described a system with parts, without the notation. Next session we make prediction visible by breaking one, and then we name what broke."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Deliver the disclosure yourself, early, unprompted. If a student discovers the unvalidated status independently later, the course loses credibility it cannot recover.
- Do not answer the objections in Session 1. Cataloguing them and assigning them to future sessions demonstrates the course was built around the problems rather than around them.
- The index cards are the single most effective device in Level E. Students are consistently struck at Session 13 by which objections survived.
