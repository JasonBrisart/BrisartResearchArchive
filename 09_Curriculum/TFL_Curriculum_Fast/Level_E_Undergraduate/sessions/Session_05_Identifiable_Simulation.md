# Level E — Session 5: Identifiable Simulation

**Duration:** 75 min

## Objective

Students apply the identifiability rule, and recognize prespecification as the answer to the confabulation objection.

---

## 1. Frame — Return to Session 2 (10 min)

Name the student who raised the confabulation objection.

**Say:**
> "The objection was: people construct the expectation after the outcome. If that's right, every self-report in this framework is worthless. Today is the answer, and the answer is not clever — it's just timing."

---

## 2. Build — The Identifiability Rule (22 min)

Write two statements:

    A: "I had a bad feeling about the meeting."
    B: "I expected the budget item to be deferred again."

**Ask:** which is a `Sim(t)`?

B only.

**Write the test:**

    Can you state what you expected, specifically enough
    that a third party could check whether it happened?

      Yes → it is Sim(t)
      No  → the framework does not apply here

**Say the important part:**
> "This is a boundary of the framework, not a failing of the person. TFL applies only where simulation content is identifiable. A vague feeling is outside coverage — not a weak instance of it."

**Then prespecification:**

> "And the content has to be recorded before the outcome. Collected afterward, it is contaminated by the outcome and you have measured memory reconstruction, not simulation."

Write: `Content + prespecification = measurable. Either missing = not a TFL claim.`

---

## 3. Contest — Is the Rule Too Strong? (20 min)

Assigned position:

- **Student A:** The rule excludes most of mental life. A model that only covers stateable expectations covers very little.
- **Student B:** The exclusion is the framework's best feature; unrestricted coverage would make it unfalsifiable.

**Say:**
> "Both are correct and they're the same observation with opposite valence. The framework trades reach for testability. Whether that trade is worth it is a judgment you're entitled to make either way — but make it knowing what's being traded."

---

## 4. Apply — The Sharpening Exercise (18 min)

**Part A (8 min):** Students write five deliberately vague expectation statements from their own recent experience.

**Part B (10 min):** Attempt to sharpen each into a checkable `Sim(t)`.

    Vague:
    Sharpened:
    Checkable by a third party?   Y / N
    If N, why not:

**Key instruction:**
> "Some will not sharpen. Mark those honestly. The unsharpenable ones are the useful result — they map the boundary."

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "You can now say when the framework applies. Next: the relation that combines three of the five variables, and a question about the one that's missing."

---

## Instructor Notes

- This session prevents the most common Level F failure: a study built around an unmeasurable simulation.
- Students resist discarding examples. Frame exclusion as precision rather than loss, repeatedly.
