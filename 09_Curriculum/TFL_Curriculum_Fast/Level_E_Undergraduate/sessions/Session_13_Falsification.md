# Level E — Session 13: Falsification

**Duration:** 75 min
**Materials:** Session 1 index cards, returned

## Objective

Students state falsification conditions meeting all four requirements, and evaluate their own initial objections.

---

## 1. Frame — Return the Cards (12 min)

Hand back the Session 1 objection cards.

**Say:**
> "Read what you wrote before you knew anything. Mark it: answered, partly answered, or still standing."

Collect a show of hands on each category. "Still standing" is usually substantial and should be.

**Say:**
> "The ones still standing are the real ones. Today you turn them into something checkable."

---

## 2. Build — What Falsification Requires (20 min)

**Open with the trap:**

> "Predictions persist when they matter, and when they don't persist, they didn't matter enough."

**Ask:** What result contradicts that?

None. It is circular.

**Say:**
> "The framework escapes this with three features: a threshold, independently measured intensity, and identifiable content. Remove any one and you're back in the circle."

    A FALSIFICATION CONDITION MUST:
      1. Be stated before data collection
      2. Be observable with the planned measurements
      3. Be possible — the design could actually produce it
      4. Be inconsistent with the framework's claim

Model one:

    CLAIM: Simulations persist only when E(t) > θ_persistence.

    FALSIFIED IF: Content-identifiable simulations persist at
    equivalent rates across measured high and low E(t)
    conditions, with ambiguity and repetition controlled.

**Say:**
> "Note the controls. Without them an alternative account explains the result and nothing has been falsified."

---

## 3. Contest — Strongest and Weakest (18 min)

Students draft one condition each. Put two on the board side by side — one specific, one vague.

**Ask:** Which could be checked next month with real measurements?

**Say:**
> "Specificity is the entire difference between a falsification condition and a hedge. 'If we found no support' is not a condition — absence of support can come from bad measurement, low power, or a broken design."

---

## 4. Apply — Four Conditions (20 min)

    CLAIM 1: E(t) governs simulation persistence.
    FALSIFIED IF:

    CLAIM 2: E(t) does not contribute to integration.
    FALSIFIED IF:

    CLAIM 3: Contradiction produces revision or suppression.
    FALSIFIED IF:

    CLAIM 4: Integration is conditionally deterministic.
    FALSIFIED IF:

Peer exchange. Each student audits a partner's four against the numbered requirements and returns written notes.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next meeting is the final. Two gates: a falsification condition naming a real observation, and a correctly rejected application. Everything else is ordinary points."

---

## Due

**Critique Memo** collected.

## Instructor Notes

- Returning the cards is the highest-impact five minutes in Level E. Students routinely find their own objection was serious and unresolved, which is the correct outcome.
- Reject "no support found" firmly every time it appears. It recurs until named explicitly.
