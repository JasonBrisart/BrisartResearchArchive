# Level E — Session 9: Contradiction and Recurrence

**Duration:** 75 min
**Materials:** Shape cards for multi-break trials

## Objective

Students distinguish revision, suppression, persistence, and recurrence, and recognize the measurement problem separating the last two.

---

## 1. Frame — What Being Wrong Does (8 min)

**Say:**
> "Intuition says contradiction deletes a prediction. The framework says something weaker and stranger. We'll test it in the room."

---

## 2. Build — Contradiction Defined (20 min)

Run a pattern trial with polling before each card. Record class percentages on the board.

    Cards 1-5:  circle × 5
    Card 6:     square
    Cards 7-9:  circle × 3

Typical result: circle predictions drop after the square, then climb back.

**Write the definition:**

    CONTRADICTION
    Mismatch between Sim(t) and Input(t) producing
    measurable update effects, including revision
    or suppression.

**Emphasize the verbs:**
> "Revision. Suppression. Not deletion."

Then recurrence:

    PERSISTENCE — the simulation is carried forward across windows,
                  governed by E(t) relative to threshold

    RECURRENCE  — previously identified simulation content reappears,
                  as a function of persistence and reentry conditions

Note the framework defines recurrence in terms of reappearance of identified content, not in terms of a proven gap. Whether the content was continuously active or genuinely absent in between is an empirical question your sampling rate must be able to answer.

---

## 3. Contest — Can You Tell Them Apart? (22 min)

Pose the problem rather than assigning positions:

> "Between card 6 and card 7, was the circle prediction running the whole time, or gone and back? What measurement distinguishes those?"

Let the room work. Both positions are defensible, which is the point.

**Say:**
> "You cannot distinguish persistence from recurrence with the sampling interval you have. This is not a flaw in your reasoning — it's a real measurement requirement, and any study claiming to observe recurrence must specify a sampling rate fast enough to detect absence."

**Then the harder question:**

> "Your board data shows recovery. Does that support TFL specifically, or would habit predict identical numbers?"

Habit predicts the same recovery. This is the third time this term consistency has failed to be evidence, and by now students anticipate it.

---

## 4. Apply — Multi-Break Trials (20 min)

In pairs, students run a trial with three breaks and record how many are needed for durable change.

    Break 1 → prediction changed?  Y / N
    Break 2 → prediction changed?  Y / N
    Break 3 → prediction changed?  Y / N

    Breaks before durable change: ____

Pool the class numbers. Answers cluster above one.

**Then written, individually:**

> Name two confounds in the trial you just ran that would prevent it from being a publishable test of contradiction-driven revision.

Target answers: no prespecified criterion for "durable," experimenter and scorer identical, no verification the contradiction was detected, tiny n, no control condition.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: why the framework refuses to add variables, and what that refusal costs it."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Recording percentages before each card converts an impression into board data students can argue about.
- If a class shows durable change after one break, report it honestly and note that it would require adjudication rather than dismissal.
