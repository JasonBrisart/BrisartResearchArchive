# Level E — Session 12: Scope and Invalid Application

**Duration:** 75 min

## Objective

Students identify invalid applications and state the boundary violated in each case.

---

## 1. Frame — The Over-Application Trap (10 min)

Read six proposals. Students vote valid or invalid before any discussion.

1. Tracking whether stated expectations persist across a 20-minute task with rated intensity
2. Explaining why a student's political views shifted across four years
3. Testing whether contradicting feedback revises a stated expectation within a session
4. Identifying which employees are prone to anxiety
5. Explaining why a crowd at an event reacts as a unit
6. Locating which brain region generates `Sim(t)`

Valid: 1 and 3. Invalid: 2, 4, 5, 6.

**Say:**
> "Four of six were traps and at least one of them looked attractive to most of you. Getting this right is a scored outcome, not a technicality."

---

## 2. Build — The Rejection Criteria (22 min)

    NOT DEFINED TO MODEL
      long-term memory consolidation
      identity formation
      narrative construction
      developmental trajectories
      group-level or social-level dynamics

    DOES NOT SPECIFY
      neural circuits, anatomy, neurotransmitters,
      oscillatory signatures

    DOES NOT CLAIM
      what consciousness is
      how subjective experience arises

    DOES NOT PROVIDE
      diagnostic criteria
      therapeutic mechanisms
      explanations of psychopathology

    DOES NOT ATTRIBUTE
      agency, volition, goals, or selfhood

**On the last:**
> "Simulations are computational hypotheses. Not intentions, not wishes, not choices. That matters the moment someone describes a prediction as something the system 'wants'."

**On the clinical exclusion, at length:**
> "Proposal 4 is not merely out of scope. It is a misuse. The framework supplies no diagnostic criteria and no therapeutic mechanism, and the inference from a persistence pattern to any individual's condition is unsupported. This is the application adults propose most confidently and it is always wrong."

---

## 3. Contest — The Borderline Case (20 min)

Present a genuinely hard proposal:

> A study of whether expectations about a single workday persist across the day.

**Ask:** Is one day short-timescale?

Let the room argue. Productive resolution: it depends on whether identifiable simulation content can be tracked across windows, or whether the claim is really about the day as a unit. The first is inside; the second is not.

**Say:**
> "Borderline cases are decided by the identifiability rule, not by the clock. That's a more useful test than a duration threshold."

---

## 4. Apply — Formal Rejection Writing (18 min)

Three invalid proposals, each rejected formally.

    PROPOSAL:
    REJECTED BECAUSE:
    BOUNDARY VIOLATED:
    APPROPRIATE LEVEL OF ANALYSIS INSTEAD:

The last line requires recognizing that exclusion points elsewhere rather than dismissing the question.

**Required form:**

    INVALID because the framework is not defined to model
    [specific exclusion], which operates at [different
    timescale / level of analysis].

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Last content session: what would make you abandon this. Bring your Session 1 index card back to mind — I'm returning them."

---

## Assessment

Administer **Problem Set 4**.

## Instructor Notes

- Capable students over-apply more than struggling ones. Watch the strongest students most closely here.
- The clinical case warrants extra time in any section with counseling, nursing, social work, or education students.
