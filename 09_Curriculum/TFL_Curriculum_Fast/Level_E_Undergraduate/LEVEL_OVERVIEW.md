# Level E Overview — Undergraduate

**Course title:** Prediction, Persistence, and the Limits of a Model
**Meetings:** 14
**Length:** 75 minutes
**Typical placement:** 200-level; suitable as an elective in psychology, cognitive science, philosophy, education, or interdisciplinary methods

---

## What Students Learn

Level E moves a student from no exposure to competent first critique. By the end, a student can state the formal system, operationalize its variables at a basic level, articulate three competing accounts, and name a condition under which they would abandon the framework.

The organizing claim, as at every level:

> `E(t)` governs whether a simulation carries forward. It says nothing about whether the simulation was correct.

The organizing question, which is new at this level:

> Is this framework worth believing, and how would we find out?

## Session Arc

| # | Title | Core move |
|---|---|---|

| 4 | The Temporal Window | Update rate versus content duration |
| 5 | Identifiable Simulation | The rule that invalidates most applications |
| 6 | The Integration Function | Reading and using the first relation |
| 7 | The Persistence Gate | Why `E(t)` is structurally separate — pivot |
| 8 | Persistence Is Not Accuracy | The four-box study at adult scale |
| 9 | Contradiction and Recurrence | What mismatch actually does |
| 10 | Variable Closure and Its Cost | Explanatory reach traded for falsifiability |

Sessions 7 and 8 are the pivot pair. Do not compress them. If the term runs short, cut Session 10 before either.

## Prerequisites

None. Any major. No statistics assumed.

Students arriving from K-12 Band D may place into Session 4 or Session 6 using the instrument in `00_START_HERE/PREREQUISITES_AND_PLACEMENT.md`.

## Assessment Summary

| Instrument | Weight | When |
|---|---|---|
| Problem sets (4) | 24% | After Sessions 4, 7, 10, 12 |
| Midterm examination | 20% | After Session 7 |
| Critique memo | 16% | After Session 11 |
| Final examination | 40% | Session 14 |

**Gate requirement:** The final does not pass without a stated falsification condition naming an observation that could actually occur.

## Standing Rules

1. Prediction accuracy is never graded.
2. The student's conclusion about TFL is never graded — only the argument.
3. No case in favor of the framework is accepted before a falsification condition is on record.
4. Over-application is a scored error.
5. TFL is not a clinical model and no assessment may frame it as one.

## Required Disclosure

Delivered Session 1 and restated at the midterm and final. See `00_START_HERE/EPISTEMIC_STATUS_AND_DISCLOSURE.md`.

## What Carries Into Level F

Students finishing Level E should be able to:

- State and explain both core relations
- Explain the exclusive gating role of `E(t)`
- Apply the identifiability rule to reject inadequate examples
- Name and distinguish the three alternative accounts
- State a falsification condition naming a possible observation
- Correctly reject an invalid application and name the boundary

Level F begins from these and adds measurement at research scale.
