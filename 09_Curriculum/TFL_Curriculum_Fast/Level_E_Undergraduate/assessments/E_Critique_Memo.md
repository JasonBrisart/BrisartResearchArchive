# Level E — Critique Memo

**Assigned:** Session 11 · **Due:** Session 13 · **Points:** 40
**Weight:** 16% of course grade
**Length:** 1200-1800 words

---

## Task

Write a memo evaluating one specific claim made by TFL. Not the framework as a whole — one claim.

Acceptable claims include:

- `E(t)` functions exclusively as a persistence gate
- Integration is conditionally deterministic
- Contradiction produces revision or suppression rather than deletion
- Variable closure is methodologically justified
- The temporal window is an appropriate unit of analysis
- Persistence and accuracy are structurally independent

---

## Required Structure

**1. The claim, stated fairly (200-300 words)**
State the claim in its strongest form, as the framework's author would. If a reader cannot tell from this section which side you will take, you have done it correctly.

**2. What would have to be true (200-300 words)**
Identify the conditions under which the claim holds. Name the assumptions it depends on.

**3. The strongest objection (300-400 words)**
The best available argument against. Draw on at least one of the three alternative accounts, or on literature from your own discipline.

**4. What would settle it (300-400 words)**
A specific observation, with controls, that would distinguish the claim from its strongest rival. This section must meet all four falsification requirements.

**5. Your judgment (200-400 words)**
Where you land and why. Any conclusion is acceptable. Uncertainty is acceptable if it is specific about what would resolve it.

---

## Rules

- **Falsification before advocacy.** Section 4 must be complete before Section 5 argues anything. A memo that advocates without a stated falsification condition cannot pass.
- **No clinical claims.** Any proposal to apply the framework diagnostically is a scored error regardless of quality elsewhere.
- **Your conclusion is not graded.** Only the argument.

---

## Rubric (40 points)

### Claim statement, fairly rendered (8)

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 7-8 | Strongest form; reader cannot infer the author's position |
| 5-6 | Accurate but slightly weakened |
| 3-4 | Strawmanned, or conflates several claims |
| 0-2 | Misstates the claim |

### Assumptions identified (6)

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5-6 | Names the conditions the claim depends on, including at least one the framework does not state explicitly |
| 3-4 | Names stated assumptions only |
| 0-2 | Assumptions absent or incorrect |

### Objection quality (10)

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Genuinely threatening; engages an alternative account or outside literature precisely |
| 6-8 | Real objection, thinly developed |
| 3-5 | Generic skepticism not specific to the claim |
| 0-2 | Absent, or attacks a claim the framework doesn't make |

### Falsification condition (10) — **gate**

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Names a specific observation; includes controls; could actually occur; inconsistent with the claim |
| 6-8 | Names an observation; controls thin |
| 3-5 | Vague but points at something observable — **gate marginally met** |
| 0-2 | No observation named, or "no support found" — **GATE FAILED** |

### Judgment (6)

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5-6 | Position follows from the preceding sections; uncertainty, if present, is specific |
| 3-4 | Position stated but weakly connected to the analysis |
| 0-2 | Assertion independent of the argument |

---

## Gate

A memo scoring 0-2 on the falsification condition does not pass regardless of total. One revision permitted.

## Never Scored

Which conclusion is reached · length beyond the range · writing polish · topic selection among the acceptable claims
