## GATE CHECK — Score First

| Gate | Requirement | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Q11 states a falsification condition naming a possible observation | Y / N |
| **B** | Q15 identifies a genuinely invalid application AND names the boundary | Y / N |

**If either is N:** the final does not pass. Record the numeric score, mark the gate failure, assign remediation, permit one retake.

**Gate A fails if:** the answer says only "if we find no support," restates the claim, or names nothing observable.
**Gate B fails if:** the named application is actually valid, or no boundary is identified.

*Propagation (7)* — the weighted portion

| Pts | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 6-7 | Cycles 2-3 each show specific inherited content; persistence answers consistent with stated `E(t)` |
| 4-5 | Inheritance present but generic in one cycle |

Accept the continuous-vs-after-absence framing if the student notes it is a resolution-dependent distinction.

**7.** (3) No (1). Conditions (1 each, any two of three): congruence between `Sim(t)` and `Input(t)`; contradiction-driven updating or suppression; decay of `E(t)` below threshold.

## Section 3 — Adjudication (20)

**8.** (12 — 1.5 per cell)

| Account | Persistence when | No persistence when |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertainty-only | Ambiguity unresolved | Ambiguity resolved, regardless of intensity |
| Arousal-only | General activation high | Activation low, regardless of content |
| Habit-based | Content frequently repeated | Content novel, regardless of intensity |
| TFL | Identifiable `Sim(t)` with `E(t) > θ` | `E(t) ≤ θ`, regardless of ambiguity or repetition |

**9.** (4) Full credit describes a condition separating TFL from all three — typically low ambiguity, controlled repetition, novel content, with `E(t)` measured and varied.

**10.** (4 — 1 each) Ambiguity measured and varied independently of intensity · general arousal measured separately · repetition counted or controlled · persistence criterion prespecified.

## Section 4 — Falsification (22) — GATE

**11.** (10)

| Pts | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Names a specific observation; includes controls; could actually occur; genuinely inconsistent with the stated claim |
| 6-8 | Names an observation; controls thin |
| 3-5 | Vague but points at something observable — **gate marginally met** |
| 0-2 | No observation named — **GATE FAILED** |

**12.** (4) Absence of support may result from poor measurement, insufficient power, or design failure. A falsification condition must name a result inconsistent with the claim, not merely one that fails to support it.

**13.** (4 — 1 each) Stated before data collection · observable with planned measurements · possible given the design · inconsistent with the claim.

**14.** (4) Any three of: a defined threshold · independently measured affective intensity · identifiable simulation content · variable closure preventing post-hoc addition. Award 4 for three correct features.

## Section 5 — Scope and Status (18) — GATE

**15.** (8)

| Pts | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 7-8 | Genuinely invalid application; correct boundary named; appropriate alternative level identified |
| 5-6 | Application and boundary correct; alternative weak |
| 3-4 | Invalid application named; boundary vague — **gate marginally met** |
| 0-2 | Application actually valid, or no boundary — **GATE FAILED** |

Valid rejections: developmental trajectories · identity formation · narrative construction · group-level dynamics · neural implementation · consciousness · clinical or diagnostic use.

**16.** (3 — 1 each) Long-term memory consolidation · identity formation · narrative construction · developmental trajectories · group-level or social-level dynamics.

**17.** (3) Computational hypotheses. Accept "hypotheses about near-future or counterfactual states." Do not accept intentions, goals, or wants.

**18.** (4) Full credit states all three: original framework, formally specified and falsifiable in principle, not independently validated. Deduct for inflating either direction — "widely accepted" or "unscientific" both lose points.
