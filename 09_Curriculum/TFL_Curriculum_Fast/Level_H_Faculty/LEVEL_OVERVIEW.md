# Level H Overview — Postdoctoral and Faculty

**Workshop title:** Supervising, Teaching, and Reviewing Work on an Unvalidated Framework
**Meetings:** 8 modules
**Length:** 3-4 hours each
**Format:** One-week intensive, or monthly across an academic year
**Participants:** 6-20

---

## What This Level Is For

Levels E through G produce people who can evaluate and test the framework. Level H produces people responsible for **others** doing so — supervisors, instructors, reviewers, and program builders.

The shift is from competence to stewardship. A Level G graduate can conduct an honest adjudication. A Level H graduate can ensure that a lab, a course, or a review process produces honest adjudication when they are not in the room.

## Prerequisites

Doctoral training in a relevant field. Level G strongly recommended. Prior teaching or supervision experience assumed — Module 2 is not useful to someone who has never supervised.

Participants who have not completed Level G should read the full specification and complete the Level G specification audit before Module 1.

## Assessment

| Artifact | Weight |
|---|---|
| Disclosure protocol | 10% |
| Supervision plan | 20% |
| Scope-enforcement procedure | 15% |
| Syllabus | 20% |
| Review protocol | 15% |
| Program memo | 10% |
| Portfolio defense | 10% |

**Gate requirement:** The portfolio does not pass without a disclosure protocol and a scope-enforcement procedure, both executable by someone other than the author.

## The Level H Premise

Stated in Module 1 and returned to throughout:

> The framework's unvalidated status creates obligations that fall on you rather than on students. A student who overclaims has made an error. A supervisor who permitted it has made a different and larger one.

Scope discipline is weighted most heavily at this level because faculty are the last checkpoint before misuse reaches publication, classrooms, or practice.

## Standing Rules

1. Every artifact must be executable by someone who did not write it.
2. Disclosure is non-negotiable and is assessed.
3. Clinical, diagnostic, and individual-assessment applications are rejected, and participants must be able to enforce that rejection on others.
4. Participants are not asked to endorse the framework, and a participant who considers it unpromising can complete every artifact honestly.
