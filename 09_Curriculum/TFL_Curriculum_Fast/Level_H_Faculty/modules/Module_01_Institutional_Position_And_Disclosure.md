# Level H — Module 1: Institutional Position and Disclosure

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Artifact:** Disclosure protocol

## Objective

Participants determine and document how the framework's status will be represented in every context they control.

---

## 1. Frame — The Obligation Falls on You (25 min)

**Say:**
> "Everything below this level is about whether an individual can reason well. This level is about whether an institution behaves well when you aren't watching. Those are different problems and the second one is solved with documents, not with judgment."

State the premise:

> "A student who overclaims made an error. A supervisor who permitted it made a larger one. The asymmetry is the reason this level exists."

---

## 2. Build — Where Disclosure Has to Live (50 min)

Work through each context participants actually control:

    SYLLABUS              Status stated in writing, not only verbally
    FIRST CLASS MEETING   Spoken, before content
    STUDENT WORK          Required section in every submitted artifact
    LAB ONBOARDING        Stated to every new member, documented
    CONFERENCE ABSTRACTS  Status named, not implied
    MANUSCRIPTS           Framework introduced accurately in the text
    PUBLIC COMMUNICATION  The hardest case — no technical audience to self-correct
    GRANT LANGUAGE        Accuracy under incentive to overstate

**The two failure directions, both assessed:**

    OVERCLAIM    Presenting as established, accepted, or validated
    UNDERCLAIM   Presenting as unserious, which makes rigorous
                 engagement look like a waste and is equally inaccurate

**Say:**
> "Underclaiming is the one this room will get wrong. Academics hedge defensively, and 'some guy's model' is as inaccurate as 'an established account.' The correct statement is specific: original, formally specified, falsifiable in principle, not independently validated."

---

## 3. Contest — Does Disclosure Cost Credibility? (45 min)

- **Participant A:** Prominent disclosure invites dismissal. Reviewers and chairs stop reading at "unvalidated."
- **Participant B:** Non-disclosure that surfaces later is fatal in a way disclosure never is, and pre-validation work is legitimate when labeled.

**Say:**
> "A is describing a real cost and it should not be waved away. The question is which risk you can survive. Disclosed and dismissed is recoverable. Undisclosed and discovered is not."

---

## 4. Apply — Draft the Protocol (50 min)

    DISCLOSURE PROTOCOL

    Standard statement (verbatim, 2-4 sentences):

    Contexts and delivery:
      Syllabus            — placement and wording
      First meeting       — spoken, when
      Student artifacts   — required section, where
      Lab onboarding      — who delivers, documented how
      External writing    — where it appears in the text
      Public communication — adapted wording

    Who is responsible in each context:
    What happens if it is omitted:
    Review interval:

**Standard:** a colleague must be able to execute this without asking the author anything.

Peer exchange and audit.

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: supervision. The disclosure protocol is the easy half — the hard half is a student whose dissertation depends on a framework that may not survive."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The underclaiming direction genuinely surprises this audience. Spend real time on it.
- Require verbatim wording. A protocol that says "disclose appropriately" has not been written.
