# Level H — Module 3: Scope Enforcement as a Standing Duty

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Artifact:** Scope-enforcement procedure

## Objective

Participants build a procedure that stops invalid applications reliably, including when they are not present.

---

## 1. Frame — It Will Be Proposed Constantly (20 min)

**Say:**
> "The clinical application is the most intuitive thing anyone does with this framework, and it is always invalid. You will hear it from students, collaborators, journalists, and clinicians, and you will hear it after you have explained why it doesn't work."

**Say:**
> "Explaining does not scale. Procedures do."

---

## 2. Build — The Enforcement Hierarchy (50 min)

**Tier 1 — Structural prevention.** Scope rejection is a required section in every artifact your lab or course produces. The invalid proposal never gets written because the form demands its own boundary check.

**Tier 2 — Review interception.** A named checkpoint where a person other than the author checks scope specifically, with authority to stop.

**Tier 3 — Correction.** What happens when something invalid gets out — retraction, correction, and who decides.

**The five rejection categories, for the procedure itself:**

    TIMESCALE       Outcomes measured over months or years
    LEVEL           Groups, institutions, cultures as the unit
    IMPLEMENTATION  Locating variables in neural tissue
    CLINICAL        Classifying individuals, screening, intervening
    IDENTIFIABILITY Sim(t) that cannot be prespecified and checked

**Say:**
> "Clinical is the category that carries real-world harm. The others produce bad papers. This one can produce a screening instrument someone applies to a person."

---

## 3. Contest — Is Blanket Rejection Too Rigid? (45 min)

- **Participant A:** A total clinical prohibition prevents legitimate exploratory work that might eventually establish a valid application.
- **Participant B:** The framework supplies no diagnostic criteria and no therapeutic mechanism. There is nothing to explore from within the framework; establishing such content would be building a different framework.

**Say:**
> "B is correct on the specification, and A's concern is answered by building the diagnostic content first, elsewhere, and only then asking whether TFL relates to it. The prohibition is on inferring clinical content from a framework that has none — not on the underlying research question."

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: the syllabus. Same discipline, applied to a whole course."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Require the verbatim standing response. Participants who improvise clinical refusals in the moment soften them under social pressure, reliably.
- The Tier 2 authority question is where most procedures fail. A checker without authority to stop is a commenter.
