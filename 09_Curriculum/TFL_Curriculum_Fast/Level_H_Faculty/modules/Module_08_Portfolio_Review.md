# Level H — Module 8: Portfolio Review

**Duration:** 4 hours
**Artifact:** Defended portfolio

## Objective

Participants defend a complete, executable set of stewardship artifacts.

---

## 1. Frame — The Executability Test (20 min)

Restate the gate:

> "Two requirements. A disclosure protocol and a scope-enforcement procedure, both executable by someone other than you. If a colleague needs you in the room, the artifact has failed."

**Say:**
> "That standard applies to all six. You are building institutional capacity, and institutional capacity that depends on one person's judgment is just that person."

---

## 2. Blind Execution Test (75 min)

Before any defense, artifacts are tested rather than described.

Participants exchange portfolios with someone they have not worked with. Each then performs, using the received documents only, with no verbal support:

1. **Deliver the disclosure** from the protocol, verbatim, aloud
2. **Apply the scope procedure** to an instructor-supplied proposal and render a verdict
3. **Review a specimen manuscript** using the received brief and protocol
4. **Identify the stop conditions** in the program memo

Each performer reports where the documents failed them.

**Say:**
> "The failures you just heard are your grade on executability, and they are more useful than anything I could tell you."

---

## 3. Defenses (100 min)

Each participant: 8 minutes presenting the portfolio's logic, 8 minutes defending.

**Presentation covers:**

    1. My institutional position and how disclosure implements it
    2. How a student in my care is protected if the framework fails
    3. How an invalid application is stopped when I am absent
    4. What my course produces that survives refutation
    5. How work is judged, and by whom
    6. My program recommendation and its stop conditions
    7. My compliance position

**Challenge roles, assigned:**

- The **chair** — asks why this belongs in the curriculum at all
- The **student advocate** — asks what happens to a student when the framework fails
- The **skeptical reviewer** — asks why any of this is worth institutional resource
- The **compliance officer** — asks what rights the described activity requires

---

## 4. Close (25 min)

**Final discussion:**

> "Where does this group stand on institutional engagement with this framework? Not on whether it is true — on whether it warrants the resource."

Record the distribution, including declines.

**Say:**
> "Several of you have produced portfolios that recommend limited engagement or none. Those are complete and correct artifacts. The level asked you to become capable of stewarding this work responsibly, and deciding responsibly not to is inside that capability."

**Say:**
> "The framework is still unvalidated. What has changed is that there are now people who could run its examination properly, teach it honestly, supervise students through it safely, and review it without reviewing its reputation. That is what this level was for."

---

## End of Level H

Participants completing Level H should be able to:

- State and implement an accurate disclosure position across every context they control
- Supervise students on unvalidated work without transferring career risk to them
- Enforce scope boundaries through procedure rather than presence
- Design and defend a course whose outcomes survive refutation
- Build a review process that examines inference rather than reputation
- Design or decline a research program with explicit stop conditions
- Determine and document the rights their work requires

---

## End of the Higher Education Package

The four levels complete the sequence begun in the K-12 package. A student who entered at Band A learned that they are always predicting. A participant leaving Level H can decide, on behalf of an institution, whether that claim deserves examination — and make sure the examination is honest.
