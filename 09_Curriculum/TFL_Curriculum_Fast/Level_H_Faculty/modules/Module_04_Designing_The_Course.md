# Level H — Module 4: Designing the Course

**Duration:** 4 hours
**Artifact:** Syllabus for a chosen level

## Objective

Participants produce a defensible syllabus for a level they intend to teach, appropriate to their own institution.

---

## 1. Frame — Defensible to Whom (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Your syllabus has three audiences: your students, your chair, and a hypothetical critic who thinks teaching this is irresponsible. Write for the third and the first two are covered."

---

## 2. Build — What Makes It Defensible (55 min)

**Element 1 — Disclosure in writing.** Not a verbal convention. On the document.

**Element 2 — Learning outcomes that survive refutation.** Outcomes phrased as evaluation, measurement, and adjudication competences — not as framework knowledge. "Students will be able to state the persistence condition" is a weak outcome. "Students will be able to state a falsification condition for a formal claim" is a strong one and remains true if TFL collapses.

**Element 3 — Assigned opposition, scheduled.** Visible on the calendar, so the course's structure demonstrates its own even-handedness.

**Element 4 — Falsification before advocacy, as policy.** Written into the assignment sequence.

**Element 5 — Gate requirements, stated.** Students know before the final what cannot be omitted.

**Element 6 — Scope rejection assessed.** Appears in the grading scheme, not only in lectures.

**Element 7 — Standards alignment.** How this maps to existing program requirements, in your institution's language.

**Say:**
> "Element 2 is what answers the chair. If your outcomes are methodological, the course is a methods course that uses a live example, and that framing is both defensible and accurate."

---

## 3. Contest — Approval Simulation (60 min)

Participants pair. Each presents their syllabus for ten minutes to a partner playing curriculum committee, who must raise:

- What standard or requirement does this satisfy?
- Why teach an unvalidated framework rather than an established one?
- What protects students from being taught something false as true?
- What happens if this framework is refuted mid-term?

Swap roles.

**Say:**
> "The last question is the best one and most of you will not have an answer prepared. The honest one is that refutation mid-term is a teaching gift — the course's outcomes are about evaluating the claim, and watching one resolve is the ideal case."

---

## 4. Apply — Revise (55 min)

Participants revise against committee feedback in session, focusing on outcomes phrasing and standards alignment.

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: review processes. You've built how it's taught; now build how work on it gets judged."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The approval simulation is the module's core. Participants who skip it produce syllabi that read well and do not survive their actual institution.
- Outcomes phrasing is the highest-leverage revision. Most first drafts phrase outcomes as framework knowledge and fail the chair question for that reason alone.
