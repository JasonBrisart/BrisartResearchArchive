# Scope and Boundaries for Instructors

Read this before teaching any band. It defines what belongs in a TFL classroom and what does not.

## What TFL Models

TFL is a local, short-timescale model. It describes how sensory evidence, retrieved memory, and predictive simulation are integrated within brief temporal windows, and how affective intensity governs whether a prediction is carried into the next window.

Valid classroom territory:

- Moment-to-moment prediction
- Expectation and surprise
- Whether a prediction persists or fades
- What happens when a prediction meets contradicting information
- How the current moment becomes the starting point for the next

## What TFL Does Not Model

TFL is not defined to model:

- Long-term memory consolidation
- Identity formation
- Narrative construction
- Developmental trajectories
- Group-level or social-level dynamics

These require separate frameworks and are outside this curriculum.

## Not a Theory of Consciousness

TFL does not claim to explain what consciousness is, how subjective experience arises, or why awareness exists. It specifies a functional integration model.

If a student asks "so is this what consciousness is?", the accurate answer is:

> "No. This model describes how predictions are built, carried, and updated in short time steps. It doesn't try to explain what awareness is."

That answer is correct and is worth giving directly.

## Not a Neural Implementation

TFL does not specify exact neural circuits, anatomical substrates, neurotransmitter systems, or oscillatory signatures. Any brain references in classroom discussion should stay illustrative.

Avoid statements like "this happens in the hippocampus." Prefer "this model describes the process, not the specific brain wiring."

## Not a Clinical or Behavioral Model

TFL does not provide diagnostic criteria, therapeutic mechanisms, or explanations of psychopathology.

This has direct classroom consequences:

- Do not use TFL to explain any student's behavior.
- Do not use TFL to describe anxiety, trauma, intrusive thoughts, or any clinical condition.
- Do not invite students to analyze themselves or each other.
- Do not let persistent predictions be framed as symptoms.

## Affect Handling Rules

`E(t)` is a simple intensity signal, not a full emotional model. It captures only how strongly something sticks.

Approved example domains:

- Games and sports outcomes
- Weather predictions
- Schedule and routine changes
- Story endings
- Test or quiz expectations
- Everyday surprises

Off-limits in activities:

- Personal loss, grief, injury
- Family conflict
- Anything a student would not want read aloud

If a student volunteers something distressing: acknowledge briefly, do not analyze it using TFL, redirect to the activity, and follow your school's standard support procedure afterward.

## The Assumption Boundary

TFL is valid only when its assumptions hold: processing occurs in discrete windows, simulation content is identifiable, affective intensity is measurable, and inputs are stable enough within a window.

For classroom purposes, this reduces to one rule:

> If students cannot say what the specific prediction was, TFL does not apply to that example.

A vague feeling is not a simulation. Ask for the content. If there is none, choose a different example.

## Student-Facing Scope Question

Teach students this question and use it all unit:

> "Is that something this model is for?"

Students who can answer it correctly have understood the framework's edges, which is a genuine learning outcome and is assessed in Bands C and D.
