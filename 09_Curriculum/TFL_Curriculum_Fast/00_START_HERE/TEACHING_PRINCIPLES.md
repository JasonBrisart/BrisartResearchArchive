# Teaching Principles

Ten operating rules for TFL instruction. These apply across all four bands.

## Principle 1 — The loop is the content

Students should be able to draw a cycle before they can name its parts. If instruction ever becomes a list of five definitions, return to the loop.

## Principle 2 — Prediction is continuous, not occasional

The most common student model is that prediction happens when you deliberately guess. TFL describes prediction as running constantly, in every short window, whether or not anyone notices.

Teaching move: ask students to predict something they were not trying to predict. "Before I opened this door, what did you expect the room to sound like?" Almost all students will have an answer, which proves the prediction was already running.

## Principle 3 — Mismatch makes the invisible visible

Design every activity around a break. Correct predictions teach nothing observable. Broken ones expose the machinery.

## Principle 4 — Persistence is not accuracy

Strong feeling decides whether a prediction is carried forward. It does not decide whether the prediction was right. Reinforce this in every band at the appropriate level of formality.

## Principle 5 — Carry-forward is the hard idea

The genuinely difficult concept is not that we predict. It is that each moment becomes the starting condition for the next one, so nothing begins from zero.

Budget more time here than feels necessary. Band B spends two full sessions on it.

## Principle 6 — Student language first, framework language second

Let students describe what happened in their own words before supplying the term. The term should feel like a name for something they already reported.

## Principle 7 — Wrong predictions are ungraded

Nothing in this package awards points for predicting correctly. Points are awarded for recording, reasoning, and completeness. State this to students explicitly and repeatedly.

## Principle 8 — Keep affect low-stakes

Affect is taught as intensity that governs persistence. Use games, weather, schedules, sports, and surprises. Do not solicit personal emotional history. Do not present TFL as diagnostic or therapeutic.

## Principle 9 — Scope is content, not a disclaimer

Knowing what TFL does not cover is assessed. A student who over-applies the framework has misunderstood it.

## Principle 10 — End every session by propagating forward

Close with one sentence that becomes the opening of the next session. The instructional structure should mirror the framework being taught.
