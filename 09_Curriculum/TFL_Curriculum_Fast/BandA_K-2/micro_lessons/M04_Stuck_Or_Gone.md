# Micro-Lesson A4: Stuck or Gone

**Duration:** 5 minutes
**Band:** A (K-2)
**Materials:** None
**Use as:** End-of-day reflection or next-day follow-up

---

## Purpose

Separate what stuck from what faded, without connecting it to correctness.

## Script

**Ask:**
> "Think about this morning. What's one thing you still remember?"

Take three or four answers.

**Then ask:**
> "Now — what's something that happened this morning that you completely forgot about until right now?"

This is harder. Give wait time. Prompt: "What did you do right after you came in?"

**Say:**
> "Both of those things happened to you. One stuck. One didn't."

**Ask:**
> "Why do you think that one stuck?"

Accept: it was funny, it was loud, it was important to me.

**Close:**
> "It stuck because it mattered more. Not because it was right or wrong — just because it mattered."

## The Key Boundary

If a student offers something upsetting, acknowledge briefly and redirect to a school-based example. Do not explore it. Follow your school's standard support procedure afterward.

## Watch For

Students often think "stuck" means "important" in an evaluative sense. Keep it mechanical: stuck just means it came back on its own.
