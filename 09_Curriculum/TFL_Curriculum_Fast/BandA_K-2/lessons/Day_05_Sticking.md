# Band A — Day 5: Sticking

**Grades:** K-2
**Duration:** 20-25 minutes
**Materials:** Chart paper, drawing paper, crayons

---

## Objective

Students will observe that some surprises stay with them and some do not.

## Success Looks Like

Each student can name one thing that stuck with them and one thing that did not.

---

## 1. Hook — What Do You Still Remember? (5 min)

Do not review Day 4 first. Ask cold.

**Say:**
> "Yesterday I broke some things on purpose. Without me reminding you — what do you still remember?"

Collect answers. Tally them on chart paper.

Typically the song stop and the movement break dominate. The counting skip is often forgotten.

**Say:**
> "Look at this. Some of them stuck. Some of them are gone."

---

## 2. Name — Sticking (5 min)

Write **STUCK** on the chart.

**Say:**
> "When something stays with you and keeps coming back, we say it **stuck**."

**Say:**
> "And here's something surprising. Whether it stuck has nothing to do with whether you guessed right."

Give the example:
> "The counting one — lots of you were surprised by that, right? And most of you forgot it by today. It surprised you and it still didn't stick."

---

## 3. Work — The Sticking Sort (10 min)

Students sort their own experience into two groups.

**Drawing task.** Fold paper in half.

```
Left side:  Something that STUCK with me
Right side: Something I FORGOT about
```

Students draw one thing on each side. Any recent event counts — not just Day 4.

For grades 1-2, add a label or one sentence under each drawing.

**Circulate and ask:** "Why do you think that one stuck?"

**Expected answers:**
- "Because it was funny."
- "Because it was loud."
- "Because I kept thinking about it."

All three are usable. Reflect them back:
> "So it mattered more to you. That's why it hung around."

---

## 4. Compare — Different Things Stuck (4 min)

Hold up two or three drawings (with permission).

**Ask:**
> "Did the same thing stick for everybody?"

No.

**Say:**
> "Same classroom. Same day. Different things stuck. It depends on how much it mattered to you."

---

## 5. Carry — One Sentence Forward (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Next time is our last one. We're going to put all our words together and see how they go in a circle."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Keep "mattered" as the word. Do not say "emotion," "feeling," or "important."
- If a student says something distressing stuck with them, acknowledge it warmly and briefly, do not explore it, and redirect: "That makes sense. Can you draw something from school on this side?" Follow your school's standard procedure afterward.
- The counting-skip example is reliable for showing surprise without sticking, but substitute whatever your class actually forgot.

## Variations

**Oral version:** Partner interview instead of drawing. One asks, one answers, then swap.

**Tally version:** Build a class bar graph of what stuck. The visual spread makes the point efficiently.

**Extension:** Ask: "Is there something from a long time ago that still sticks?" Do not analyze the answers — just note that sticking can last.
