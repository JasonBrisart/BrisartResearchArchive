# Band A — Day 2: Remembering

**Grades:** K-2
**Duration:** 20-25 minutes
**Materials:** Chart paper from Day 1, markers

---

## Objective

Students will connect what they remember to what they expect, discovering that guesses come from before.

## Success Looks Like

Each student can name one thing they remember and say what it makes them expect.

---

## 1. Hook — The Missing Middle (4 min)

**Say, without introduction:**
> "Ready, set..."

Stop. Wait. Students will call out "GO!"

**Say:**
> "How did you know?"

Students will say they just knew, or they've heard it before.

**Say:**
> "You've heard it before. That's the whole answer. Let's do another."

Try two more:
- "Happy birthday to..."
- "One, two, three, four..."

Each time ask: "Where did that come from?"

---

## 2. Name — Remembering (5 min)

Write **REMEMBER** on the chart next to NOTICE.

**Say:**
> "Remembering is what comes back from before. You didn't go looking for it. It just showed up."

**Say:**
> "When I said 'ready, set' — your remembering handed you 'go' before you decided anything."

---

## 3. Work — Remember and Expect (10 min)

Introduce the paired structure. This is the day's core work.

**Model it first:**
> "I remember that we line up after this. So I expect the door will open soon."

Write the frame on chart paper:

```
I remember ________.
So I expect ________.
```

Students complete the frame about the school day. Grades 1-2 write or draw it. K does it orally in pairs, then shares.

**Good student answers look like:**
- "I remember we have music on Tuesday. So I expect music today."
- "I remember it was raining. So I expect the field is wet."
- "I remember my lunch is in my bag. So I expect a sandwich."

**Circulate and ask:** "How do you know that? Where did that come from?"

---

## 4. Compare — Where Guesses Come From (5 min)

Collect three or four student pairs and write them on the chart.

**Key question:**
> "Did anybody expect something they had never seen before?"

Almost always, no.

**Say:**
> "So expecting comes from remembering. You can't expect something out of nowhere."

This is the day's load-bearing idea.

---

## 5. Carry — One Sentence Forward (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Remembering gives you your guesses. Next time, we're going to say the guesses out loud *before* we find out."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Some students will fill the frame with wishes ("I remember pizza, so I expect pizza today"). That's fine. Ask: "Is that a guess or a hope?" Most can tell the difference.
- If a student cannot produce a memory, prompt with routine: "What happened right before you came in here?"
- Keep memories school-based and recent. Do not solicit memories from home life.

## Variations

**Non-verbal option:** Students draw two boxes — one for the memory, one for the expectation — and connect them with an arrow.

**Extension:** Ask a student to make a prediction about tomorrow and record it. Check it on Day 3. This creates a real, personally owned prediction.
