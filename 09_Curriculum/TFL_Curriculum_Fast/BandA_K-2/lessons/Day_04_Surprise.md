# Band A — Day 4: Surprise

**Grades:** K-2
**Duration:** 20-25 minutes
**Materials:** Audio source or noisemaker, chart paper

---

## Objective

Students will experience an engineered mismatch and recognize that surprise reveals a prediction they already had.

## Success Looks Like

Each student can complete: "I was surprised because I expected ______."

**This is the pivot session of Band A.** The whole unit turns here.

---

## 1. Hook — The Sound That Stops (5 min)

Play a familiar sound or song. Let it run long enough that students settle into it — about fifteen seconds.

**Stop it abruptly mid-phrase.**

Students will react. Some will vocalize. Some will laugh. Some will finish it themselves.

**Say immediately:**
> "What just happened in your head?"

Take reactions. Students will say it felt weird, unfinished, wrong.

**Say:**
> "Something in you was already playing the next part. When it didn't come, you noticed."

---

## 2. Name — Surprise (5 min)

Write **SURPRISE** on the chart, under the other three.

**Say:**
> "Surprise means you expected something and got something else."

**Then the key line:**
> "Here's the trick. When your guess is right, you don't notice it at all. You only find out you were guessing when it breaks."

Repeat it. This is the idea that makes the whole unit work.

**Say:**
> "So surprise is how we catch our guesses."

---

## 3. Work — Three Breaks (10 min)

Run three more engineered mismatches. After each one, students complete the sentence frame out loud or on paper:

```
I was surprised because I expected ________.
```

**Break 1 — The counting skip.**
Count aloud: "One, two, three, five..." Stop.

**Break 2 — The wrong ending.**
Tell a two-sentence familiar story with a wrong ending. "The three little pigs built houses. Then they all went swimming."

**Break 3 — The routine change.**
Change something physical about the room routine — clap a pattern three times, then do something different the fourth time.

After each: collect two or three completions of the frame.

**Watch for:** Students who say "I was surprised because it was weird." Push once:
> "Weird compared to what? What did you think was coming?"

That question converts a reaction into a reported prediction.

---

## 4. Compare — Nobody Was Surprised By Everything (3 min)

**Ask:**
> "Was anybody surprised by all three?"

Some yes, some no.

**Ask:**
> "Was anybody not surprised at all by one of them?"

**Say:**
> "That means your guess was right that time. And you didn't even notice you made it."

---

## 5. Carry — One Sentence Forward (2 min)

**Say:**
> "Some of these you'll have forgotten by tomorrow. And some of them — I bet somebody is still thinking about the song. We'll find out next time."

---

## Instructor Notes

- **Do not warn the class** before the first break. The surprise is the instrument.
- **Do warn individual students privately** if you know that unexpected sound is difficult for them. Tell them beforehand: "Something is going to stop suddenly today. It's on purpose." They still predict; they just aren't startled.
- Never make a break that singles out or embarrasses a student.
- Keep the energy light. Surprise should be funny, not alarming.

## Variations

**Visual break:** Show a repeating pattern of colored cards and change one.

**Tactile break:** Pass around three smooth objects and then one rough one.

**Movement break:** Lead three identical arm motions, then a different one. Students copying will do the expected motion before catching themselves — which is prediction made physically visible. This version is the most powerful for many students.
