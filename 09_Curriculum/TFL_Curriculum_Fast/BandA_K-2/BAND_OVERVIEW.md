# Band A Overview — Grades K-2

**Unit title:** Guessing What Comes Next

**Sessions:** 6
**Session length:** 20-25 minutes
**Suggested pacing:** Two weeks, three sessions per week

---

## What Students Learn

Band A teaches one idea: **your brain is always guessing what happens next, and what happens changes the next guess.**

That is the whole unit. Everything else is practice.

## Four Words Only

| Word | Meaning for students |
|---|---|
| **Notice** | What I see, hear, or feel right now |
| **Remember** | What comes back from before |
| **Expect** | What I think happens next |
| **Surprise** | When it was not what I expected |

Do not introduce any other terms. No notation. No variable names. No "temporal window."

## Session Arc

| Day | Title | Core move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Noticing | Prediction exists and you can catch it |
| 2 | Remembering | Expectations come from before |
| 3 | Expecting | Say the guess out loud before checking |
| 4 | Surprise | Breaking a guess makes it visible |
| 5 | Sticking | Some surprises stay with you |
| 6 | The Loop | Put it together and show it repeats |

Days 1 through 3 build the parts. Day 4 is the pivot — the engineered mismatch that makes prediction observable. Days 5 and 6 assemble the loop.

## Classroom Norms to Establish Day 1

State both of these out loud on the first day and repeat them every session:

> **Being wrong is the interesting part.** A wrong guess tells us more than a right one.

> **Every guess counts.** We never score whether you guessed right.

Band A depends on students guessing out loud without fear. Establish this before anything else.

## What Carries Into Band B

Students finishing Band A should be able to:

- Name something they noticed, remembered, and expected
- Report a surprise and say what they expected instead
- Indicate, with a gesture or drawing, that the cycle repeats

Band B starts from these and adds **right now** and **carry forward**.

## Boundaries for This Band

- Keep every example concrete and immediate: sounds, snacks, routines, weather, stories.
- Never ask students how something made them feel about themselves.
- Do not use the word "emotion." Use "it stuck with me" instead.
- Do not discuss the brain, consciousness, or any medical topic.

## Materials for the Whole Unit

- Chart paper and markers
- One noisemaker or audio source
- A familiar picture book
- Drawing paper and crayons
- One small object to pass (Day 6)

Total prep: about five minutes per session.
