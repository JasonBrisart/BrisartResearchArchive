# Level F — Session 3: Measuring E(t)

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students measure affective intensity in a way that separates it from general arousal and from the persistence it is supposed to predict.

---

## 1. Frame — The Circularity Trap, Operationalized (12 min)

**Say:**
> "Level E showed you the circular version: it persisted because it mattered, and if it didn't persist it didn't matter. Today you'll discover how easy it is to build that circle into a measurement without noticing."

---

## 2. Build — Three Requirements (30 min)

**Requirement 1 — Independent of the outcome variable.**
If intensity is rated at the same time as persistence, or by the same judgment, the correlation is guaranteed.

> "Rate intensity at the moment of prediction, before persistence can be known. Timestamp both."

**Requirement 2 — Distinguishable from general arousal.**
This is the arousal-only rival account, appearing as a measurement problem.

> "You need a separate index of general activation. If content-specific intensity and general arousal are not measured separately, an arousal-only account explains any result you get."

**Requirement 3 — Bounded and calibrated.**
The framework specifies `E(t)` as bounded. An unbounded scale permits the runaway fitting the framework explicitly disallows.

---

## 3. Contest — Can Intensity Be Measured Independently at All? (25 min)

- **Student A:** Content-specific intensity and general arousal are not separable in practice; any instrument will bleed.
- **Student B:** They are separable by design — vary content while holding activation constant, and vice versa.

**Say:**
> "B describes what the design has to accomplish. A is correct that most instruments fail at it. This is precisely where the arousal-only account wins by default in a sloppy study, and it is the most common way a result that looks like support for TFL is actually support for nothing."

---

## 4. Apply — The Intensity Protocol and Its Self-Audit (28 min)

Students draft the protocol, then audit their own against three questions:

1. Could intensity here be a proxy for general activation?
2. Could the rater's knowledge of the outcome influence the rating?
3. If persistence and intensity correlate, name one reason other than the framework's claim.

Question 3 is the graded item. A student who cannot answer it has built the circle.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: persistence itself, and the sampling problem that decides whether you can even tell persistence from recurrence."

---

## Assessment

Administer **Problem Set 1**.

## Instructor Notes

- This session determines whether the term's designs are worth anything. Do not let it run short.
- The self-audit in Apply catches more circularity than any amount of exposition.
