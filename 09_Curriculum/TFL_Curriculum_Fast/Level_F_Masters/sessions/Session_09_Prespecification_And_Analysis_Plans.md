# Level F — Session 9: Prespecification and Analysis Plans

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students produce a written analysis plan that fixes every consequential decision before data exist.

---

## 1. Frame — Flexibility Is the Enemy (12 min)

**Say:**
> "Every undecided detail is a place where the result can be chosen rather than observed. Today you remove them. This is also what converts your falsification condition from a sentence into something that can actually fire."

---

## 2. Build — What Must Be Locked (30 min)

    PRESPECIFIED BEFORE DATA:

      Hypotheses, stated separately for TFL and each rival
      Persistence criterion, including partial matches
      Sampling interval and handling of missed samples
      Inclusion and exclusion rules for participants
      Inclusion and exclusion rules for individual predictions
      Handling of unscoreable Sim(t) responses
      Primary outcome, distinguished from secondary
      Analysis method
      Stopping rule or target sample size
      The falsification condition

**Say:**
> "The unscoreable-response rule is the one that matters most here and the one everyone omits. If you decide after the fact which vague responses count, you have chosen your data."

Connect explicitly to the framework:

> "The framework requires that a falsification condition be stated before data collection. Your analysis plan is where that requirement becomes operational rather than rhetorical."

---

## 3. Contest — Does Prespecification Cost Too Much? (25 min)

- **Student A:** Rigid prespecification prevents discovery. Unanticipated patterns are where real findings come from.
- **Student B:** Exploration is legitimate and must be labeled. The problem is not looking; it is presenting exploration as confirmation.

**Say:**
> "B is the standard resolution and it's correct: prespecify the confirmatory test, label everything else exploratory, report both. A's concern is answered by disclosure, not by flexibility."

---

## 4. Apply — Write the Plan (28 min)

Students complete a full analysis plan against the checklist, then peer-audit with three questions:

1. Name a decision not fixed by this plan that could change the result.
2. Could the falsification condition actually fire given this analysis?
3. Is the unscoreable-response rule stated?

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: rejecting your own best idea. Scope enforcement applied to designs rather than examples."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Question 2 in the peer audit is the highest-value item in the session. A falsification condition that cannot fire under the stated analysis is decorative, and students find this in each other's plans reliably.
