# Level F — Session 2: Measuring Sim(t)

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students design prespecified, contamination-resistant instruments for capturing simulation content.

---

## 1. Frame — The Hardest Measurement (12 min)

**Say:**
> "Simulation content is the variable the framework most depends on and the one hardest to get honestly. Everything you'll be tempted to do contaminates it."

---

## 2. Build — Four Failure Modes (30 min)

**Failure 1 — Retrospective collection.** Asking after the outcome measures reconstruction, not simulation.

**Failure 2 — Leading elicitation.** Offering options supplies the content you intended to measure.

**Failure 3 — Unfalsifiable content.** "I expected it to go badly" cannot be scored against any outcome.

**Failure 4 — Scoring by the experimenter who elicited.** Expectancy contaminates the coding.

**Then the remedies, one per failure:**

    1. Prespecify — capture content before outcome, timestamped
    2. Open elicitation — free response before any options appear
    3. Checkability rule — content must be scoreable against a defined outcome
    4. Blind coding — scorers who do not know condition or hypothesis

**Say:**
> "None of these are exotic. All four are routinely violated in work claiming to test prediction, which is why the framework states the identifiability rule as a constraint rather than a suggestion."

---

## 3. Contest — Is Self-Report Adequate at All? (25 min)

- **Student A:** Verbal report cannot access a process operating at 100-300 ms. Any self-report instrument measures something else entirely.
- **Student B:** Report of content is not report of the mechanism. We are asking what was expected, not how the expectation was computed.

**Say:**
> "B's distinction is the framework's position and it is defensible, but A has identified a real limit: report happens slowly, so any design relying solely on it is measuring the content of a simulation, not its timing. If your hypothesis is about timing, self-report cannot carry it."

**Then:** "What measures could complement report where timing matters?" Collect: reaction time, anticipatory eye movement, prediction-error-sensitive behavioral measures. Note that none are required by the framework and all bring their own assumptions.

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: intensity. Same problem, different trap — you'll measure general arousal and call it `E(t)`."

---

## Instructor Notes

- The unscoreable worked example is the one students omit. Require it; it forces them to confront how much of their data will be unusable.
- Students with strong methods backgrounds finish early. Give them the timing objection from Contest to develop further.
