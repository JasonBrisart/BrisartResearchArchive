# Level F — Session 1: The Testability Constraint

**Duration:** 100 min

## Objective

Students state the testability constraint and apply it to reject inadequate proposals, including their own.

---

## 1. Frame — Most Uses Are Not Tests (15 min)

Restate the disclosure. Then:

**Say:**
> "Level E asked whether the framework is worth believing. This level asks what it would take to find out. The answer is more demanding than you expect, and by the end of today most of you will have abandoned your first study idea."

Present a flawed proposal:

> "We'll measure whether participants' simulations persist by asking them at the end of the week whether their predictions stuck with them."

**Ask:** What's wrong?

Collect: no specified `Sim(t)` content, `E(t)` never measured, no defined window, no prespecified persistence criterion, retrospective collection.

**Say:**
> "This is not a weak TFL study. It is not a TFL study. That distinction is the whole course."

---

## 2. Build — The Constraint and Its Consequences (30 min)

    TESTABILITY CONSTRAINT

    TFL applies only where all core variables can be
    operationalized and measured.

    Any use without observable mapping of Input(t),
    Minfo(t), Sim(t), E(t), and F(t) does not
    constitute a valid application or test of TFL.

**Say:**
> "The framework imposes this on itself. By its own standard, most casual uses are invalid. That's unusual and worth noticing — most frameworks do not supply the criterion that disqualifies their own popular applications."

Add the parameter requirement:

> "Control parameters must be linked to observable quantities in valid implementations. A weight you cannot connect to data is decoration."

Then the five-plus-two map students will build all term:

    Input(t)      → measured by:
    Minfo(t)      → measured by:
    Sim(t)        → measured by:   (stateable and checkable)
    E(t)          → measured by:   (measured, not assumed)
    F(t)          → measured by:
    θ_persistence → defined as:
    Window        → defined as:

**Standard to enforce from day one:**
> "Could another person run this without asking you what you meant?"

---

## 3. Contest — Is the Constraint Too Demanding? (25 min)

- **Student A:** The constraint is so strict that no feasible study satisfies it, making the framework untestable in practice even if testable in principle.
- **Student B:** The constraint is ordinary measurement rigor and A is describing the normal difficulty of doing research properly.

**Say after both:**
> "A raises something real. In-principle falsifiability that no achievable design can deliver is a weak virtue. Your job this term is to find out which of them is right by attempting it."

Note this genuinely is an open question about the framework, not a rhetorical setup.

---

## 4. Apply — First Draft Measurement Map (25 min)

Students draft a map for a study they would like to run. No constraints on topic yet.

Then swap with a partner. The partner's only task:

> Mark every line you could not execute without asking a question.

Most maps come back heavily marked. That is the intended result.

---

## 5. Propagate (5 min)

**Say:**
> "Next three sessions are the three hardest measurements: simulation content, affective intensity, and persistence. Everything after depends on them."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Let students propose ambitious designs today. Constraining them before they feel the difficulty produces compliance rather than understanding.
- Keep the first drafts. Returning them at Session 11 makes the term's progress visible.
