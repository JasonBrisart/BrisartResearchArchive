## Section 1 — The Constraint (14)

**1.** (4) TFL applies only where all core variables can be operationalized and measured. Any use without observable mapping of `Input(t)`, `Minfo(t)`, `E(t)`, `Sim(t)`, and `F(t)` does not constitute a valid application or test.

**2.** (5) Not a valid application as stated. Full credit requires: no operationalized variables, no measured intensity, no defined window, no prespecified criterion — and notes that it could *become* valid with those additions. Award 2 for "no" without the constructive half.

**3.** (5) Valid implementations require parameters linked to observable quantities; an unlinked parameter cannot be estimated, cannot constrain predictions, and permits post-hoc fitting. Example: a weight asserted from theory with no measurement pathway.

## Section 2 — Measuring Sim(t) (18)

**4.** (8 — 1 per failure, 1 per remedy)

| Failure | Remedy |
|---|---|
| Retrospective collection | Prespecify; timestamp before outcome |
| Leading elicitation | Open response before options appear |
| Unfalsifiable content | Checkability rule; scoreable against defined outcome |
| Experimenter scores own elicitation | Blind coding; report reliability |

**5.** (4) The outcome reshapes the report; what is measured is memory reconstruction under outcome knowledge, not the simulation that was active.

**6.** (6) Full credit requires a genuinely verbatim prompt (not a description of one) plus explicit timing relative to outcome. Award 3 if timing is stated but the prompt is paraphrased.

---

## Section 3 — Measuring E(t) (18)

**7.** (6 — 2 each) Independent of the outcome variable in time and judgment · distinguishable from general arousal via a separate index · bounded and calibrated.

**8.** (6) Intensity and persistence rated in the same judgment at the same time guarantees correlation by construction. Consequence: the correlation is uninterpretable — it cannot distinguish the framework's claim from a rating artifact.

**9.** (6) Without a separate activation index, the arousal-only account explains any persistence result equally well (4). Names arousal-only specifically (2).

---

## Section 4 — Persistence and Resolution (18)

**10.** (4 — 2 each) The persistence criterion, including partial matches · the sampling interval.

**11.** (5) Recurrence requires observing absence. Daily sampling cannot detect gaps shorter than a day, so absence is undetectable at that resolution and the recurrence claim is unsupported by construction.

**12.** (9 — 3 each)

    a. Requires window-scale instrumentation; not supportable by report-based designs
    b. Supportable at coarser resolution; must be stated as aggregate across windows
    c. Supportable by sampling; must define the minimum detectable gap

---

## Section 5 — Weights (12)

**13.** (4 — 1 each) Common or convertible scales for the three inputs · `F(t)` measured independently of its components · sufficient variation to identify three coefficients · non-collinear inputs.

**14.** (4) The prediction is generated from retrieved memory, so `Minfo(t)` and `Sim(t)` covary by construction in most tasks (2). Collinearity makes the individual coefficients unidentifiable even with good measurement (2).

**15.** (4 — 2 each, any two) The exclusive gating claim · contradiction produces revision or suppression · persistence/accuracy independence · conditional determinism · bounded affective persistence.
