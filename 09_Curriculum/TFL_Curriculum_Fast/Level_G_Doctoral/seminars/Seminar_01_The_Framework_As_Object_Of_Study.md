# Level G — Seminar 1: The Framework as Object of Study

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants establish the framework's status, the seminar's stance toward it, and what a seminar can and cannot settle.

---

## 1. Frame — Stating the Position (25 min)

Deliver the disclosure without hedging.

> "TFL is an original framework developed outside the conventional review process. It is formally specified, internally consistent, and falsifiable in principle. It has not been independently validated. Nothing in this seminar assumes it is correct."

**Then the seminar's stance:**

> "I am not its advocate. If the framework is defended here, one of you will be assigned to do it, and the assignment will rotate. My job is to make sure both positions get their strongest form."

**Then the licensing notice**, in full, before any work begins.

**Then the honest limit:**

> "Ten meetings will not settle whether TFL is true. What they can settle is what would settle it, and whether the framework is specified well enough for that question to have an answer. That is a smaller claim and an achievable one."

---

## 2. Build — The Specification, Read Closely (60 min)

Work through the full specification as a text, not as content to absorb.

Distribute and read together:

- The five declared variables and `Belief(t)`
- Both relations
- Variable closure
- The guarantee set and the convergence limit
- The testability constraint
- The exclusion set

**The reading instruction:**

> "Mark every place the specification makes a claim you could check, and every place it makes a claim you could not. We are building the second list."

Collect the second list on the board. It typically includes: the derivation of the window interval, what sets `θ_persistence`, how `Belief(t)` is updated, whether weights are stable within an individual, what counts as a partial match, the functional form of the integration.

**Say:**
> "That list is the seminar's raw material. Seminar 2 turns it into a document."

---

## 3. Contest — Is This Worth a Semester? (45 min)

Assign in advance. Genuinely open question; do not resolve it.

- **Participant A:** Studying an unvalidated single-author framework at doctoral level is a misallocation. The same effort spent on a validated literature produces more.
- **Participant B:** Pre-validation specification work is exactly what doctoral training neglects, and the absence of a defending literature makes the critique honest rather than ceremonial.

Open the floor. Instructor does not take a side.

**Say at the close:**
> "If A convinces you, say so in your critique paper. A well-argued case that the framework is not worth the field's attention is a passing submission, and it is harder to write than it sounds because it has to engage the specification rather than dismiss it."

---

## 4. Apply — The Standing Objection (35 min)

Each participant writes, and reads aloud, their single strongest objection to the framework in its current form.

Recorded and posted. Returned at Seminar 10.

**Rule:** the objection must be specific enough that someone could address it. "It's unvalidated" is a status, not an objection.

---

## 5. Propagate (15 min)

Assign Seminar 2 preparation: each participant brings three documented specification gaps with textual evidence.

**Say:**
> "Not complaints. Gaps — places the specification is silent where it needs not to be."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Do not defend the framework in Seminar 1 even if the room turns hostile. Assigning the defense from Seminar 2 onward is what keeps the term honest.
- The "what a seminar can settle" framing prevents the most common Level G failure: a term spent circling the validation question with no artifact produced.
