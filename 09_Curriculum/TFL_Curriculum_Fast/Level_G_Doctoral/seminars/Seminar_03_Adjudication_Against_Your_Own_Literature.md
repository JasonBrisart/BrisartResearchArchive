# Level G — Seminar 3: Adjudication Against Your Own Literature

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants replace the package's three generic rivals with the actual competing accounts in their own fields.

---

## 1. Frame — Three Rivals Is Not Enough (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Uncertainty-only, arousal-only, and habit-based are useful teaching rivals. They are not the competition. Every one of you works in a field with a literature that already explains something in this territory, and that literature is the real adjudication target."

---

## 2. Build — Constructing the Comparison (60 min)

Each participant presents their two field-specific rivals in eight minutes:

    ACCOUNT:
    FIELD AND STANDING:            (established / contested / emerging)
    WHAT IT EXPLAINS IN THIS TERRITORY:
    WHERE IT AND TFL AGREE:
    WHERE THEY DIVERGE:
    OBSERVATION THAT WOULD SEPARATE THEM:

**Instructor role:** press only on the divergence line. Participants routinely present rivals that make no distinguishable prediction, which is itself a finding.

**Say:**
> "If your field's account and TFL never diverge, one of three things is true: they're the same theory in different vocabulary, one is a special case of the other, or at least one is underspecified. Determine which. That's a paper."

---

## 3. Contest — Is TFL Redundant? (50 min)

The serious challenge at doctoral level.

- **Participant A:** TFL restates existing predictive accounts with different notation and an idiosyncratic affect claim. It adds nothing a field-standard account doesn't already supply.
- **Participant B (assigned defender):** The exclusive gating claim is genuinely novel and genuinely at odds with mainstream affective accounts. Novelty in one load-bearing claim is sufficient to warrant testing.

**Say:**
> "A's redundancy charge is the one most likely to end this framework, and it will not be settled by assertion in either direction. It is settled by producing the differential prediction. Everyone in this room should be able to name one by Seminar 8."

---

## 4. Apply — Differential Prediction Against a Field Rival (40 min)

| | Predicts persistence when | Predicts no persistence when | Diverges from TFL at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field rival 1 | | | |
| Field rival 2 | | | |
| TFL | | | — |

Then: one observation that would favour TFL over both, and one that would favour both over TFL. Both directions required.

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: the gating claim, examined at the level of pressure it actually deserves. Bring your affective-science objections."

---

## Instructor Notes

- This seminar is where cross-disciplinary enrollment pays off. A room with psychology, philosophy, CS, and education produces four genuinely different rival sets.
- The "requires both directions" rule in Apply prevents participants from building only the test that would vindicate their prior.
