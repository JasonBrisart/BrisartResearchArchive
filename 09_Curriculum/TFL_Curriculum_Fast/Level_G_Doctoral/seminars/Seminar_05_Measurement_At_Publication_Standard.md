# Level G — Seminar 5: Measurement at Publication Standard

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants evaluate measurement against the standard an actual reviewer would apply, not a course standard.

---

## 1. Frame — The Reviewer's Question (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Level F asked whether a design is executable. A reviewer asks something harsher: does this measurement support the inference the author wants to draw? Most designs that pass the first fail the second."

---

## 2. Build — Five Reviewer Objections (55 min)

**Objection 1 — Construct validity of `Sim(t)`.**
"The authors measure stated expectations. They infer predictive simulations. What licenses that inference?"

**Objection 2 — `E(t)` as a nuisance variable in disguise.**
"Intensity ratings correlate with engagement, task difficulty, and social desirability. Which is being measured?"

**Objection 3 — Persistence criterion opportunism.**
"The criterion is defensible. Was it chosen before or after the distribution was visible?"

**Objection 4 — Resolution mismatch.**
"Window-scale language, minute-scale data. The conclusions belong to a different design."

**Objection 5 — The rivals were not given a fair test.**
"The alternative accounts are stated in weakened form and tested against the authors' preferred operationalization."

**Say:**
> "Objection 5 is the one that gets papers rejected and the one authors never anticipate. If you operationalize the rival, you will operationalize it to lose."

---

## 3. Contest — Live Review (55 min)

Participants submit a Level F design or a current draft. Three are reviewed live, assigned in advance, each for fifteen minutes.

Reviewers are assigned objections 1-5 and must apply them specifically.

Authors respond only after all reviewers have spoken.

---

## 4. Apply — Self-Application (35 min)

Each participant applies all five objections to their own work in writing.

    Objection:
    Does it land on my design?      Yes / Partly / No
    Evidence for my answer:
    What I would have to change:

An answer of "no" to all five is returned. It indicates the objections were not genuinely applied.

---

## 5. Propagate (15 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: the gap between what your design supports and what you will be tempted to claim."

---

## Instructor Notes

- Objection 5 warrants extra time. Fair operationalization of rivals is the single most common integrity failure in adjudication research and participants have rarely been taught it explicitly.
- Live review works best with volunteers whose designs are strong. Reviewing a weak design teaches less because everything is wrong.
