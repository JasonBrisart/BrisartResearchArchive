# Level G — Seminar 4: The Gating Claim Under Pressure

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants subject the exclusive gating claim to the strongest available critique and determine what would resolve it.

---

## 1. Frame — The Most Exposed Position (20 min)

Write the claim in its strongest form:

    E(t) functions EXCLUSIVELY as a persistence gate.
    It does not contribute to integration weighting.
    It does not participate in simulation construction.
    It does not indicate accuracy.

**Say:**
> "This is where the framework is most likely to be wrong, and it knows it. Narrow claims are falsifiable claims. Today we find out whether this one survives contact with the affective science literature."

---

## 2. Build — What the Claim Commits To (50 min)

Work out the entailments explicitly:

    IF exclusive gating holds, THEN:

      - Integration weights are invariant to measured E(t),
        inputs held constant
      - Manipulating affect should not alter what is perceived
        or predicted within a window, only what carries forward
      - Affective influence on interpretation, where observed,
        must be mediated by carried-forward content rather than
        by within-window weighting

**Say:**
> "That third entailment is the framework's escape hatch and you should examine whether it's legitimate or ad hoc. It can explain away within-window affective effects as propagation from prior windows. Is that principled, or is it unfalsifiable rescue?"

This is a genuinely hard question and the seminar should not resolve it cheaply.

---

## 3. Contest — Structured Attack (60 min)

Three assigned groups, twenty minutes each including response.

**Group 1 — The empirical case against.** Present field evidence that affect influences perception and interpretation directly. Argue the exclusive claim is inconsistent with it.

**Group 2 — The mediation defence.** Argue observed effects are consistent with propagation across windows rather than within-window weighting, and specify what would distinguish them.

**Group 3 — The unfalsifiability charge.** Argue that if any affective effect can be reassigned to propagation, the claim cannot be refuted and the framework's apparent narrowness is illusory.

**Say at the close:**
> "Group 3 has raised the question that decides whether this claim is scientific. The answer requires specifying, in advance, what within-window weighting evidence would look like and committing to it. If that can't be done, Group 3 wins."

---

## 4. Apply — The Deciding Design Sketch (40 min)

Individually:

    CLAIM UNDER TEST:  E(t) does not contribute to integration weighting
    MANIPULATION:
    HELD CONSTANT:
    WITHIN-WINDOW MEASURE:
    HOW I DISTINGUISH WITHIN-WINDOW EFFECT FROM PROPAGATION:
    RESULT THAT FALSIFIES:

The fourth line is the graded item and the hard part.

---

## 5. Propagate (10 min)

**Say:**
> "Next: measurement at the standard a reviewer would actually apply. Bring your Level F design if you have one."

---

## Instructor Notes

- This is the most intellectually demanding seminar in Level G. Schedule it when the group is at full strength, not adjacent to a conference week.
- Do not rescue the framework from Group 3. If the room cannot specify the distinguishing evidence, report that honestly as the seminar's finding.
