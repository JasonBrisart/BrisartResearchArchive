# Level G — Seminar 6: Inference and What the Design Supports

**Duration:** 3 hours

## Objective

Participants distinguish what a result licenses from what its author will want to claim, and calibrate their own overclaiming.

---

## 1. Frame — Overclaiming Is the Default (20 min)

**Say:**
> "Nobody sets out to overclaim. It happens because the discussion section is written by someone who has lived with the hypothesis for a year. Today we build the check."

---

## 2. Build — The Inference Ladder (50 min)

Write the ladder and work each rung against a hypothetical result.

    OBSERVED:        Participants who rated expectations as
                     high-intensity reported them more often at
                     24 hours, with ambiguity and repetition controlled.

    RUNG 1 (safe):   Rated intensity predicted reported persistence
                     in this sample under these controls.

    RUNG 2:          The association is not attributable to ambiguity,
                     repetition, or general arousal as measured here.

    RUNG 3:          Consistent with a threshold-gated persistence
                     mechanism.

    RUNG 4 (unsafe): E(t) gates persistence.

    RUNG 5 (false):  TFL is supported.

**Say:**
> "Rung 3 is the ceiling for a single well-run study. Rung 4 requires converging designs. Rung 5 is not a thing any single result does for any framework."

**Then the reverse ladder**, which participants find harder:

    A null result licenses: this design did not detect the
    predicted association under these conditions.

    It does not license: the framework is refuted.

> "Your falsification condition fires only if it was prespecified and its controls held. Otherwise a null is uninformative, and saying so is not special pleading — it is the same standard you'd apply to a positive."

---

## 3. Contest — Where Does the Ceiling Sit? (50 min)

- **Participant A:** Rung 3 is too conservative. Cumulative reasoning is how fields progress, and refusing to make mechanism claims makes research uninterpretable.
- **Participant B (assigned):** Rung 3 is generous. Given a single unreplicated study on an unvalidated framework, Rung 2 is the honest ceiling.

**Say:**
> "Note that B's position is harder on the framework than A's, and B is the assigned defender this week. Defending a framework properly includes refusing to overclaim on its behalf. Overclaiming is how frameworks get discredited."

---

## 4. Apply — Ladder Your Own Result (45 min)

Participants write the ladder for their own anticipated result, both directions.

    MY ANTICIPATED POSITIVE RESULT:
    Rungs 1-3, written out:
    The rung I will be tempted to claim:
    What would be required to reach it:

    MY ANTICIPATED NULL RESULT:
    What it licenses:
    What it does not:
    Whether my falsification condition fires:

---

## 5. Propagate (15 min)

Critique papers due at Seminar 7. Distribute the workshop protocol.

---

## Instructor Notes

- The reverse ladder is neglected in most methods training and participants consistently find it the more useful half.
- Having the assigned defender argue for the more conservative ceiling is deliberate and worth pointing out explicitly.
