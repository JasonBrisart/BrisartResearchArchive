## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — Logging Properly.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every student has two entries with intensity rated at writing
- **Lesson 2:** every student can read the relation aloud as a sentence
- **Lesson 3:** every student has the direction correct in both threshold rows
- **Lesson 4:** every student classifies at least six correctly
- **Lesson 5:** every student has six entries passing all four rules
- **Lesson 6:** every student has four timestamped, contemporaneously rated entries
- **Lesson 7:** every student ranks eight and justifies two
- **Lesson 8:** every student's log has at least ten surviving entries

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | fill in entries retrospectively later | Timestamp every entry in class weekly so back-filling is visible. |
| 2 | read the relation without decoding theta | Name it: theta. It is a letter, not a mystery. |
| 3 | reverse the direction under time pressure | Drill it verbally: threshold up, persistence down. |
| 4 | collapse all three into forgotten | Separate with cases: a replaced expectation is not a faded one. |
| 5 | write unfalsifiable entries | Apply the third-party test to each entry. |
| 6 | rate intensity after the outcome | Require the rating in the same pen stroke as the prediction. |
| 7 | rank by how interesting the prediction is | Ask what makes it checkable, not what makes it interesting. |
| 8 | have too few entries to work with | Set a weekly minimum from Lesson 1 and check it every week. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
