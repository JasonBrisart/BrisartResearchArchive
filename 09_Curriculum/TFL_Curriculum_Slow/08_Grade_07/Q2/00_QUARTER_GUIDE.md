## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — Pooling The Class.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** every student finds at least three of the four combinations
- **Lesson 10:** every student has all four cells populated
- **Lesson 11:** every student writes about at least two entries from that cell
- **Lesson 12:** every student compares E(t) across both diagonals
- **Lesson 13:** the class has a pooled matrix with all four cells populated
- **Lesson 14:** every student has three genuine disconfirming conditions
- **Lesson 15:** every student has ten entries rated on both scales
- **Lesson 16:** the class marks at least six correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | treat the definitions as two names for the same thing | Require all four combinations: if they were the same, two would be impossible. |
| 10 | sort from memory rather than the log | Require the log on the desk. Refuse unsourced entries. |
| 11 | dismiss those entries as unusual | Count them: that is a quarter of the class data. |
| 12 | find no accurate-faded entries | Offer categories: doors, timetables, routine outcomes. |
| 13 | assume the pooled data will match their own | Compare individual to pooled explicitly. They usually differ. |
| 14 | write a condition that could never occur | Test it: could that actually happen in your data? |
| 15 | rate both identically | Find a case where they diverge and put it on the board. |
| 16 | hedge the statement | Require the clean sentence, written, from every student. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
