## Exit outcomes

- Set two expectations side by side and state the difference
- Name the differing memory that caused a difference in expectation
- Support a claim with evidence that can be pointed at
- Frame a changed expectation as an update, not a mistake

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **compare** | put two things side by side and say how they differ |
| **evidence** | the thing you point at to show your answer is right |
| **change** | when an expectation is different the second time |

**Not used at this grade:** Right now as a combined state, carry forward, all notation.

## How this grade is taught

Two connected written sentences, or a short paragraph. A claim must be followed by the evidence for it.

## How students show they have learned it

Written comparisons, quarter quizzes and tests, four projects.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "One of the two expectations must be wrong"

**Why it happens.** School usually frames difference as error.

**How to fix it.** Use a case where both were reasonable and neither checkable.

**What to say.** "Different does not mean one of you was wrong."

### "Evidence means saying it louder"

**Why it happens.** Students conflate confidence with support.

**How to fix it.** Ask them to point at the thing.

**What to say.** "If you cannot point at it, it is not evidence yet."

### "If my expectation changed, my first one was a mistake"

**Why it happens.** Change reads as correction rather than update.

**How to fix it.** Show a case where the first was correct and the situation changed.

**What to say.** "Something new arrived, so it updated. That is the system working."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Give the comparison as a printed two-column table with the claim pre-started.

**Extension.** Ask for two of their own expectations that disagreed at the same time. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
