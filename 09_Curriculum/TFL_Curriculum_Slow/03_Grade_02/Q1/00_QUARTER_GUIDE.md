## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — Both Reasonable.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every pair states a difference, not just two expectations
- **Lesson 2:** every student names two different memories
- **Lesson 3:** every student names at least two differences
- **Lesson 4:** every student compares two pairs
- **Lesson 5:** students can state that difference is not error
- **Lesson 6:** every student sorts three cases correctly
- **Lesson 7:** every pair names a cause
- **Lesson 8:** the class completes at least four correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | list both and stop | Ask for the missing sentence. |
| 2 | name the same memory for both | Ask the harder question: then why did they expect differently? |
| 3 | try to compare all three at once | Show the pairwise method explicitly. |
| 4 | compare unrelated things | Require the same topic in both halves of a pair. |
| 5 | still pick a winner | Remove the option: you may not pick one. |
| 6 | treat falsified and different as the same | Use the checkability test: could we find out? |
| 7 | produce identical accounts | Ask what each could not see from where they were. |
| 8 | omit the cause | Refuse any comparison without a named cause. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
