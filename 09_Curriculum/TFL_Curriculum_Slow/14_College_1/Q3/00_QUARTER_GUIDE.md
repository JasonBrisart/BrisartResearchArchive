## The pivot session of this quarter

**Session 18 — The Identifiability Rule.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Session 15:** every rival statement carries a recorded objection from a checker
- **Session 16:** every student finds that most results discriminate nothing
- **Session 17:** every student states honestly whether their design enters it
- **Session 18:** every student marks at least one as unsharpenable
- **Session 19:** the claim statement passes the reader test
- **Session 20:** section four precedes any advocacy in section five
- **Session 21:** every rejection names a boundary and points elsewhere

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Session | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | state rivals in forms convenient to defeat | Require the external check and refuse unchecked statements. |
| 16 | argue the rivals are less plausible | Redirect: plausibility is not the test. Does it predict your result? |
| 17 | claim entry without meeting all four criteria | Check the four criteria one at a time. |
| 18 | argue the rule excludes most of mental life | Both positions are correct with opposite valence. Say so. |
| 19 | signal their position in the claim statement | Run the reader test: which side does this person take? |
| 20 | advocate before stating the condition | Return it unmarked and say why. |
| 21 | over-apply because the framework feels universal | Watch the strongest students most closely here. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
