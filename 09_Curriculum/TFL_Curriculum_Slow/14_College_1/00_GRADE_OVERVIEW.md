**Course:** The Framework and Its Status · **Stage:** Undergraduate
**28 sessions of 75 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

Epistemic status as content, and stating a claim in its strongest form before attacking it.

## Exit outcomes

- State the framework's epistemic status accurately for three audiences
- State a claim in a form its author would accept before criticising it
- Map a moment with a Sim(t) checkable by a third party
- Convert a surviving objection into a falsification condition

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **epistemic status** | what is currently known about whether a framework is correct |
| **independent validation** | confirmation by researchers unconnected to the author |
| **overclaim** | presenting a framework as more established than it is |
| **underclaim** | presenting it as less serious than its specification warrants |
| **standing objection** | an objection recorded before study and revisited after |

**Not used at this grade:** Publication-standard measurement critique, referee-quality review.

## How this grade is taught

University reading load. A claim is stated as its author would state it before it is criticised. The student's own position is never graded.

## How students show they have learned it

Problem sets, a standing objection, a critique memo, midterm and final.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Unvalidated means unserious"

**Why it happens.** Academic hedging defaults to dismissal and students imitate it.

**How to fix it.** Show the specification is falsifiable in principle and internally consistent.

**What to say.** "Dismissal is as inaccurate as endorsement. Both are assessed."

### "If the author defines the terms, it cannot be wrong"

**Why it happens.** Author-defined terms feel unfalsifiable by construction.

**How to fix it.** Point at variable closure and the testability constraint.

**What to say.** "It supplies the rule that invalidates its own popular applications."

### "I should decide now whether I believe it"

**Why it happens.** Students want a verdict to organise their study around.

**How to fix it.** State that a verdict is not the outcome.

**What to say.** "You are being asked to be able to evaluate it, not to believe it."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Provide the status statement as a three-sentence template.

**Extension.** Ask them to state the status of a framework in their own discipline. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
