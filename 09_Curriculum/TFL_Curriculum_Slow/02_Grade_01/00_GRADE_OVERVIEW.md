# Grade 1

**Course:** Where Guesses Come From · **Stage:** Primary
**32 lessons of 25 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

> A guess is built, and I can say what it was built from.

## Entry check — before session one

**Grade 1 assumes Kindergarten.** The child separates noticing from remembering without prompting.

- [ ] Name something noticed right now and something remembered
- [ ] Complete 'I remember ___ so I guess ___' with a connected pair
- [ ] Report a surprise and state the guess it revealed
- [ ] Say that something stuck because it mattered, not because it was right

If a box will not tick, return to `../01_Kindergarten/` and reteach that outcome before starting. Advancement follows demonstrated readiness, never the calendar.

## Exit outcomes

- Name notice, remember, and expect in order
- Write a complete expectation with a connected because
- Trace an expectation backwards to a plausible source
- Apply the break test to prove an unnoticed expectation was there

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **expect** | the proper word for a guess about what happens next |
| **because** | the word that joins a guess to where it came from |

**Not used at this grade:** Right now as a combined state, carry forward, all notation.

## How this grade is taught

Short written sentences. One sentence per idea, two joined with 'so' or 'because'. Drawing remains an acceptable substitute for any answer.

## How students show they have learned it

Oral explanation, short written work, quarter quizzes and tests.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Predicting means guessing on purpose"

**Why it happens.** Everyday use of the word implies deliberate effort.

**How to fix it.** Ask about an expectation they never chose. The chair. The door.

**What to say.** "Expecting runs all the time. Deciding to guess is one small piece."

### "'I just knew' is a complete answer"

**Why it happens.** The source is invisible, so the expectation feels self-generated.

**How to fix it.** Push once, every time: 'Knew from what?'

**What to say.** "Everything you expect came from somewhere."

### "A wrong expectation was a bad one"

**Why it happens.** Students carry the general school rule that wrong answers lose points.

**How to fix it.** Show the mark scheme. Points are for the trace, not the outcome.

**What to say.** "Being wrong costs you nothing. Leaving it blank costs you the points."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Provide the sentence frame on a desk card. Allow drawing for either half.

**Extension.** Ask the student to find an expectation whose source they cannot name. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
