## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Loop.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student has two complete unaided traces
- **Lesson 26:** every student finds at least one thing to repair
- **Lesson 27:** every student's second cycle starts from the first cycle's ending
- **Lesson 28:** every student has three connected cycles
- **Lesson 29:** every student records a question they could not answer
- **Lesson 30:** every student compares an early trace with a late one
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | ask for the example to be supplied | Give a category, not an example: something about lunch. |
| 26 | find nothing wrong | Require one repair. There always is one. |
| 27 | write an unconnected second cycle | Point at the join: what happened at the end? Start there. |
| 28 | produce three independent cycles | Read only the connecting lines and mark the first break. |
| 29 | report that no question was asked | Ask the listener to ask one. Brief them beforehand. |
| 30 | cannot find early work | Keep everything in one folder from Lesson 1. Say so on day one. |
| 31 | treat it as the real thing and panic | Say clearly that this one does not count and is for finding gaps. |
| 32 | run out of time on the last item | The second cycle is the Grade 2 prerequisite. Flag it as the priority. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
