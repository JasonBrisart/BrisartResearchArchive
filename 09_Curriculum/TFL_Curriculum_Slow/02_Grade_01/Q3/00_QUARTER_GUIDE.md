## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Interrupts All Day.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every student has three completed surprise frames
- **Lesson 18:** students can state the trace is what is graded
- **Lesson 19:** every student has a completed nine-card tally
- **Lesson 20:** every student has five interrupts written before acting
- **Lesson 21:** every student brings three dated interrupts
- **Lesson 22:** every student analyses at least one wrong interrupt
- **Lesson 23:** every student answers whether R or W decides the column
- **Lesson 24:** the class can state why breaks are engineered

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | write because it was weird | Push once: weird compared to what? |
| 18 | apologise for a wrong expectation | Respond the same way every time: wrong is useful. |
| 19 | change their answer after seeing the card | Collect written expectations face-down before each reveal. |
| 20 | write the interrupt after acting | Insist on the order physically: pen down before hands move. |
| 21 | reconstruct them afterwards | Ask what time each was written. Unmarked ones do not count. |
| 22 | hide the wrong ones | Ask for them first, publicly, and treat them as the interesting cases. |
| 23 | fill only the right-and-stuck column | Prompt for the missing cell. The chair, the door, the bell. |
| 24 | still treat wrong as failure | Point at the mark scheme. Repeat it as often as needed. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
