## Entry check — before session one

**Master's Year 2 assumes Master's Year 1.** The student produces an executable measurement map and declares resolution.

- [ ] Write a Sim(t) protocol with blind coding and reported reliability
- [ ] Build an E(t) instrument that cannot manufacture its own correlation
- [ ] Write a resolution declaration naming conclusions not licensed
- [ ] Produce a measurement map a stranger can execute without clarification

If a box will not tick, return to `../18_Masters_1/` and reteach that outcome before starting. Advancement follows demonstrated readiness, never the calendar.

## Exit outcomes

- Conduct a six-section design review with specific located findings
- Trace a falsification condition's firing pathway rather than accepting it
- Calibrate a review and classify divergence as fact or standard
- Defend a design that enters the discriminating condition

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **fatal flaw** | a defect that makes the result uninterpretable |
| **calibration** | comparing your review against others of the same work |
| **review protocol** | the fixed structure every review follows |
| **executability gate** | the check performed before any other scoring |
| **residual risk** | what remains uncontrolled after the control is applied |

**Not used at this grade:** Original specification critique, referee reports on published manuscripts.

## How this grade is taught

Peer review to journal structure. Fatal flaws must be named specifically; a review that misses one is a failed review.

## How students show they have learned it

Review reports, calibration exercises, a defended final design.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "A good review is a gentle review"

**Why it happens.** Social pressure in a small cohort.

**How to fix it.** Run the calibration exercise on two reviews of the same design.

**What to say.** "One reviewer would have let an uninterpretable study run."

### "If the methods are careful the design is sound"

**Why it happens.** Rigour is visible; relevance is not.

**How to fix it.** Show a meticulous design that never enters the discriminating condition.

**What to say.** "Careful and irrelevant is a real category, and the commonest one."

### "I should review whether the framework is plausible"

**Why it happens.** The framework's status is salient.

**How to fix it.** State the governing rule: review the inference, not the framework.

**What to say.** "Ask whether the design supports what it claims."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Provide the review protocol as a form with executability pre-worked.

**Extension.** Ask them to review the same design twice a week apart and account for drift. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.

## This grade is gated

The final does not pass unless both gate items are satisfied regardless of total
score: a falsification condition naming a possible observation, and a correctly
identified invalid application with its boundary named. Announce both in session
one; never apply them retroactively.
