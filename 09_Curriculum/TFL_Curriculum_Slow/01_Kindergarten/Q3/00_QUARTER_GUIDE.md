## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Guessing About Tomorrow.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** children volunteer guesses before the reveal
- **Lesson 18:** every child draws before each reveal
- **Lesson 19:** every child records a number or a drawing
- **Lesson 20:** every child checks their own and marks it
- **Lesson 21:** every child records a tomorrow guess
- **Lesson 22:** every child guesses before their partner chooses
- **Lesson 23:** the group sees that most guesses were checked
- **Lesson 24:** the group catches at least two after-the-fact claims

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | wait to see and then claim they knew | Hands up before the page turns. Only those go on the chart. |
| 18 | change their drawing after seeing | Collect drawings before revealing. Physically remove the option. |
| 19 | want to know immediately | The wait is the lesson. Show them where the jar lives. |
| 20 | feel bad about a wrong one | Celebrate the wrong ones first, visibly and warmly. |
| 21 | guess something unverifiable | Steer to checkable things: weather, activities, who is here. |
| 22 | ask their partner first | Separate the pair for the guessing half. |
| 23 | count only the right ones | Count all of them. Say explicitly that right and wrong both count. |
| 24 | accept an after-the-fact claim | Plant them deliberately and make catching them a game. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
