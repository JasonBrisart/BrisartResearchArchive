## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — Noticing Together.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every child names one thing noticed
- **Lesson 2:** every child has three items from three senses
- **Lesson 3:** every child names something noticeable only in the new place
- **Lesson 4:** the group finds at least two changes per round
- **Lesson 5:** every pair finds one difference
- **Lesson 6:** every child finds something genuinely new to them
- **Lesson 7:** the group's tally is larger than they predicted
- **Lesson 8:** the group can define noticing in their own words

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | offer something from earlier in the day | Name it accurately: that is remembering, and we do that next time. |
| 2 | use sight for all three | Cover the eyes for the hearing and feeling rounds. |
| 3 | repeat their classroom answers | Ask what is here that was not there. |
| 4 | guess randomly rather than compare | Ask what it was like before. That is the comparison. |
| 5 | say they noticed everything | Ask for one thing the partner had that they did not. |
| 6 | pick obvious large objects | Rule it out explicitly: nothing bigger than your hand. |
| 7 | think noticing only happens when asked | Run the tally during a normal activity, not a noticing activity. |
| 8 | confuse noticing with remembering | Use the timing test: is that happening right now? |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
