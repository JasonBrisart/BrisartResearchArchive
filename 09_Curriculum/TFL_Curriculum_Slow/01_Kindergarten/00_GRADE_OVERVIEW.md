# Kindergarten

**Course:** Noticing and Remembering · **Stage:** Early Childhood
**32 lessons of 20 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

Separating what is happening now from what is coming back from before.

> What I notice and what I remember together make my guess.

## Entry check — before session one

**Kindergarten assumes Pre-Kindergarten.** The child guesses freely without fear of being wrong.

- [ ] Say a guess out loud when asked
- [ ] Report a surprise and say what they expected instead
- [ ] Name one thing that stuck and one that did not
- [ ] Show by gesture or drawing that guessing happens again and again

If a box will not tick, return to `../00_PreK/` and reteach that outcome before starting. Advancement follows demonstrated readiness, never the calendar.

## Exit outcomes

- Name something noticed right now and something remembered
- Complete 'I remember ___ so I guess ___' with a connected pair
- Report a surprise and state the guess it revealed
- Say that something stuck because it mattered, not because it was right

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **notice** | what I see, hear, or feel right now |
| **remember** | what comes back from before |

**Not used at this grade:** Expect, prediction, right now, carry forward, any variable name.

## How this grade is taught

Oral and drawn. Single-word labels on drawings are allowed. Two ideas may be held together in one session and no more.

## How students show they have learned it

Observation checklist and one drawing task per quarter.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Noticing means anything I can think of"

**Why it happens.** Notice is not yet distinct from think about.

**How to fix it.** Redirect to the senses and the present moment.

**What to say.** "That is remembering. What do you notice right now, with your ears?"

### "Remembering means trying hard to remember"

**Why it happens.** Elsewhere memory is taught as deliberate recall.

**How to fix it.** Say 'ready, set' and let them shout GO. Nobody tried.

**What to say.** "It came back on its own. That is remembering here."

### "If nothing surprised me, I did not guess"

**Why it happens.** Surprise is the only visible evidence.

**How to fix it.** Ask what would happen if their chair broke. They react.

**What to say.** "When your guess is right you do not notice it at all."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Do everything orally in pairs before asking for a drawing.

**Extension.** Ask the child to notice one thing they usually never notice. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
