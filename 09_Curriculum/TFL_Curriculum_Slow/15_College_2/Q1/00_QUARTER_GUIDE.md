## The pivot session of this quarter

**Session 4 — Prespecification.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Session 1:** every student lists at least three unmeasured variables in their own proposal
- **Session 2:** every student names evidence that would change their position
- **Session 3:** every student states the objection without weakening it
- **Session 4:** every student quantifies how much of their old data survives
- **Session 5:** every protocol includes an unscoreable worked example
- **Session 6:** every protocol names who scores and what they know
- **Session 7:** every student identifies a hypothesis report cannot carry

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Session | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | conclude the constraint makes the framework untestable | Flag the real question: that is next session's argument and it is open. |
| 2 | treat the question as rhetorical | State plainly that the instructor does not know the answer either. |
| 3 | dismiss the objection because the reports felt genuine | Press: feeling genuine is exactly what confabulation produces. |
| 4 | treat prespecification as a formality | Show the survival rate: that number is the argument. |
| 5 | describe the prompt rather than writing it verbatim | Require the exact words: write what you will say. |
| 6 | omit inter-rater reliability | Require the number: two coders, what agreement, reported how? |
| 7 | propose a non-report measure without naming its assumptions | Require them: reaction time assumes what? |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
