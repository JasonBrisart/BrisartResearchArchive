## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Notation.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student uses persistence correctly in three sentences
- **Lesson 26:** every student has three rows with visible connection
- **Lesson 27:** every student has six rows with honest fading marked
- **Lesson 28:** every student finds at least two of three error types
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** every student explains a row without reverting to plain language
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | use persistence to mean was correct for a long time | Correct immediately: persistence is about lasting, not about being right. |
| 26 | produce three disconnected rows | Cover every column but the connection and ask what remains. |
| 27 | invent content for later rows | Score marked-faint rows at full credit, visibly. |
| 28 | mark only the obvious error | Tell them the count: there are three. Keep looking. |
| 29 | revert to plain language when explaining | Restart: say it again using the names. You have earned them. |
| 30 | revert to plain language under pressure | Restart the explanation each time it happens. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | lose marks on the gating constraint | The constraint is the Grade 7 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
