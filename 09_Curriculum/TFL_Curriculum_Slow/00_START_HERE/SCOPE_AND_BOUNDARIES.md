# Scope and Boundaries

## What TFL models

How sensory evidence, retrieved memory, and predictive simulation are integrated
within brief temporal windows, and how affective intensity governs whether a
prediction carries into the next window.

Valid classroom territory: moment-to-moment prediction; expectation and surprise;
whether a prediction persists or fades; what happens when a prediction meets
contradicting information; how the current moment becomes the starting point for
the next.

## What TFL does not model

Long-term memory consolidation · identity formation · narrative construction ·
developmental trajectories · group-level dynamics · neural implementation ·
consciousness · agency, volition, goals, intentions, or selfhood.

## Not a clinical model

No diagnostic criteria, no therapeutic mechanisms, no explanations of
psychopathology.

- Do not use it to explain any student's behaviour.
- Do not use it to describe anxiety, trauma, or any condition.
- Do not invite students to analyse themselves or each other.
- Do not let persistent predictions be framed as symptoms.

This exclusion is absolute at every grade. From Grade 7 upward a student who
proposes a clinical application has made a scored error.

## Affect handling

**Approved:** games and sport, weather, schedule changes, story endings, test
expectations, everyday surprises.

**Off limits:** personal loss, grief, injury, family conflict, anything a student
would not want read aloud.

If a student volunteers something distressing: acknowledge briefly, do not analyse
it using TFL, redirect to the activity, and follow your school's standard support
procedure afterwards.

## The rule that decides borderline cases

> If a student cannot say what the specific prediction was, the framework does not
> apply to that example.

## Epistemic status

TFL is original, formally specified, falsifiable in principle, and **not
independently validated**. Presenting it as established is an overclaim.
Presenting it as unserious is an underclaim. Both are errors, and the second is
the one most teachers make.
