## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 5 — Making A Condition Fire.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 1:** every student has recorded both gates
- **Lesson 2:** every condition names an observation rather than an absence
- **Lesson 3:** every student names all three escape features
- **Lesson 4:** every student distinguishes at least three correctly
- **Lesson 5:** every student demonstrates a pathway for at least three
- **Lesson 6:** every student writes three outside conditions
- **Lesson 7:** every student traces pathways rather than accepting assertions
- **Lesson 8:** the class judges at least six correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | assume the gates are a formality | Show the distribution: gates fail more often than the rest of the paper. |
| 2 | write if we find no support | Reject it by name every time: absence of support is not falsification. |
| 3 | name the features without explaining their function | Remove one at a time and ask what becomes unfalsifiable. |
| 4 | treat every null as disconfirming | Ask what else could produce it: measurement, power, or a broken design. |
| 5 | assert a condition could fire without showing how | Require the result: write the numbers that would trigger it. |
| 6 | assume every claim has one | Find one that does not and name why. |
| 7 | accept the author's claim | Require the trace: show me the result that triggers it. |
| 8 | write conditions naming absences | Reject them by name, every time. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
