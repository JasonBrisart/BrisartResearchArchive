## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — Borderline Cases.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** every student produces all five groups and six correct markings
- **Lesson 10:** every student removes agency from at least five
- **Lesson 11:** every rejection names a boundary and an alternative level
- **Lesson 12:** every student has a verbatim refusal and answers both challenges
- **Lesson 13:** every student resolves at least two by identifiability
- **Lesson 14:** every student compares three outside models
- **Lesson 15:** every student answers both challenges specifically
- **Lesson 16:** the class judges at least eight correctly

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | treat the exclusions as legal hedging | Run the argument: a model that claims everything predicts nothing. |
| 10 | substitute one agency word for another | List the banned set: wants, tries, decides, chooses, hopes, intends. |
| 11 | reject without naming an alternative level | Require the fourth line: where does that question belong? |
| 12 | soften the refusal under social pressure | Practise saying it aloud. Improvised refusals soften. |
| 13 | decide by duration alone | Supply a counter-case with long duration and stateable content. |
| 14 | assume all models state limits | Find one that does not and ask what that costs it. |
| 15 | defend by restating the rejection | Require engagement with the specific challenge. |
| 16 | reject without the boundary | Require the boundary word every time. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
