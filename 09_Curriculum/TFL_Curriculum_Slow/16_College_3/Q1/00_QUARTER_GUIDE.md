## The pivot session of this quarter

**Session 4 — The Discriminating Condition.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Session 1:** every student finds most results discriminate nothing
- **Session 2:** every rival carries a recorded objection from its assigned defender
- **Session 3:** every student has four distinct rows or an honestly marked coincidence
- **Session 4:** every student addresses the generalisation limit
- **Session 5:** every student attempts the distinguishing line
- **Session 6:** every student presents two field rivals with divergence lines attempted
- **Session 7:** every student produces a genuine differential prediction

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Session | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | argue the rivals are less plausible | Redirect: does the rival predict your result? Then it does not separate them. |
| 2 | select a sympathetic checker | Assign checkers rather than letting students choose. |
| 3 | adjust a rival's prediction to force distinctness | Check against the fair statement: would the proponent accept that? |
| 4 | claim the cell is common in natural settings | Press: how often do people care intensely about something settled and novel? |
| 5 | resolve the charge in the framework's favour | Refuse to rescue it: can you specify the evidence or not? |
| 6 | present a rival with no distinguishable prediction | Name the three possibilities and assign the determination as work. |
| 7 | assert novelty without a differential prediction | Require it: name the observation where they differ. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
