## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — Guarantees Versus Predictions.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** every student recalls at least six
- **Lesson 10:** every student articulates the falsifiability argument
- **Lesson 11:** every student produces four genuine violating observations
- **Lesson 12:** every student ranks and justifies two
- **Lesson 13:** every student sorts at least six correctly
- **Lesson 14:** every student lists three absent guarantees with reasons
- **Lesson 15:** every student defends two specifically
- **Lesson 16:** the class recalls at least six and produces four violations

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | recall the guarantees as a list without meaning | Ask for one consequence per guarantee rather than the wording. |
| 10 | read the absence as a weakness | Run the argument: what would it take to defend universal resolution? |
| 11 | write a violation that could never be observed | Apply the test: could somebody check that with real measurements? |
| 12 | rank by importance rather than exposure | Ask which would be easiest to catch out. |
| 13 | treat every guarantee as an empirical prediction | Ask what kind of evidence would bear on each. |
| 14 | list guarantees that are actually present | Check each against the list before accepting it. |
| 15 | defend by restating the guarantee | Require engagement with the challenge. |
| 16 | recall guarantees without violations | Require the violation for every guarantee named. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
