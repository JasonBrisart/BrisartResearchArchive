## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 29 — Explaining The Guarantees.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 25:** every student produces two genuine alternative explanations
- **Lesson 26:** every student applies alternatives to at least two of their own results
- **Lesson 27:** every student names a separator for at least two
- **Lesson 28:** every student's redesign produces separating evidence
- **Lesson 29:** every student records the hardest question
- **Lesson 30:** every student compares an early and a late design
- **Lesson 31:** every student identifies one area to review
- **Lesson 32:** all four outcomes are demonstrated

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | produce strawman alternatives | Have a partner judge whether each is genuinely plausible. |
| 26 | argue the alternatives are less plausible | Redirect to prediction: does the alternative predict your result? |
| 27 | name an observation both explanations predict | Test it aloud: if you saw that, which explanation is in trouble? |
| 28 | add measurements without adding separation | Ask which explanation each new measurement would embarrass. |
| 29 | present only the promises | Require the refusals: and what does it not promise? |
| 30 | cannot find early work | One folder from Lesson 1. |
| 31 | panic | Say clearly this one does not count. |
| 32 | state gating without exclusivity | Exclusivity is the Grade 11 prerequisite. Flag it first. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
