# Pre-Kindergarten

**Course:** Guessing Games · **Stage:** Early Childhood
**32 lessons of 15 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

That a guess exists at all, and that surprise is what a wrong guess feels like.

> Your brain is already guessing what happens next.

## Exit outcomes

- Say a guess out loud when asked
- Report a surprise and say what they expected instead
- Name one thing that stuck and one that did not
- Show by gesture or drawing that guessing happens again and again

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **guess** | saying what you think happens next |
| **surprise** | when something different happened than you guessed |
| **again** | it happens over and over and does not stop |
| **stuck** | it stayed in your head |

**Not used at this grade:** Notice, remember, expect, prediction, memory, brain, emotion. None of these yet.

## How this grade is taught

Entirely oral and physical. No reading, no writing, no counting. A child answers by speaking, pointing, moving, or drawing. One idea per session.

## How students show they have learned it

Observation during play, and one drawing task per quarter.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Guessing means saying something silly"

**Why it happens.** Nobody has told them a guess is real, so they treat it as a joke answer.

**How to fix it.** Ask about something they will certainly get right. 'What happens if I let go of this?'

**What to say.** "See? That was a guess too. A good one."

### "Being wrong means I did something bad"

**Why it happens.** At this age a wrong answer reads as a rule broken.

**How to fix it.** Model it. Guess aloud, be wrong on purpose, be delighted.

**What to say.** "I was wrong! That is my favourite part."

### "I only guess when you ask me to"

**Why it happens.** A correct guess is invisible, so guessing feels like it happens on request.

**How to fix it.** Stop mid-activity and ask what is behind them. They answer without looking.

**What to say.** "Nobody looked. Everybody knew. It was already in there."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Offer a choice of two rather than an open question.

**Extension.** Ask the child to set up the guessing game for a partner. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.

## What you need

- A cloth or bag to hide objects
- Familiar picture books
- A noisemaker or audio source
- Drawing paper and crayons
- One small object to pass around

Preparation: about two minutes per lesson.
