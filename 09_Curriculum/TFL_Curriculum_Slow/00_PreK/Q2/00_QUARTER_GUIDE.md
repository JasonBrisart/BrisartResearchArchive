## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — The Counting Skip.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** children predict before being prompted
- **Lesson 10:** every child can say what they thought was coming instead
- **Lesson 11:** every child has completed the surprise frame
- **Lesson 12:** children stop or react when the pattern breaks
- **Lesson 13:** children react to the skip without being told
- **Lesson 14:** every child connects the face to an expectation
- **Lesson 15:** every child names the expectation, not only the surprise
- **Lesson 16:** the group names the expectation for at least four surprises

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | shout the ending and spoil the stops | Hands up quietly, then point at one child. |
| 10 | say only that is wrong | Ask once more: wrong compared to what? |
| 11 | say only it was weird | Push once: weird compared to what? What was supposed to be there? |
| 12 | keep clapping the old pattern | Name it. Their hands kept going, which means the guess was still running. |
| 13 | not notice the subtler skips | Start with obvious skips and build. Do not begin with the hardest. |
| 14 | draw only the face and not the cause | Ask for the second drawing explicitly: and what happened? |
| 15 | describe the event without the expectation | Ask the one question every time: what did you think was going to happen? |
| 16 | list surprises without expectations | Do not accept an entry until the expectation is named. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
