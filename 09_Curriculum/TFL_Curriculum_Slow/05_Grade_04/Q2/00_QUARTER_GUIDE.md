## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 13 — Checking A Chain.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 9:** every student has six rows with honest marking
- **Lesson 10:** every student extends a trace backwards by two rows
- **Lesson 11:** every student marks at least one interaction point
- **Lesson 12:** every student names inheritance across at least three points
- **Lesson 13:** every student receives an audited trace with a specific first break
- **Lesson 14:** every student makes a repair and classifies it
- **Lesson 15:** every student classifies at least four correctly
- **Lesson 16:** the class identifies invented inheritance in at least two traces

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | invent inheritance for later rows | Praise the marked ones: a marked faint row is honest work. |
| 10 | insist the chain started at their first row | Ask what they were doing ten seconds earlier. |
| 11 | keep the chains entirely separate | Ask for the moment one changed what they noticed in the other. |
| 12 | claim nothing carried across the week | Offer anchors: an argument, a result, a change of routine. |
| 13 | mark every imperfect row | Narrow the job: one break, the first one. |
| 14 | rewrite the whole trace | Require the original to stay visible. Repair on top of it. |
| 15 | classify every break as an error | Show one where inserting a row fixes it completely. |
| 16 | reward length over honesty | Score the marked-faint rows as full credit, visibly. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
