# Grade 5

**Course:** Invisible and Broken · **Stage:** Intermediate
**32 lessons of 40 minutes** · **Four quarters**

## What this year owns

That contradiction revises rather than deletes, and that alternatives must be stated.

> A correct expectation is invisible. Breaking one is the only way to see it.

## Exit outcomes

- Explain why correct expectations are invisible
- Apply the break test to prove an unnoticed expectation existed
- State that contradiction revises or replaces rather than deletes
- Offer a genuine alternative explanation for their own result

## Vocabulary released this year

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **invisible** | a correct expectation that leaves no trace |
| **contradiction** | when what happened does not match what was expected |
| **revise** | change an expectation a little rather than delete it |
| **alternative** | another explanation that would produce the same result |

**Not used at this grade:** All variable names, persistence threshold, suppression as a technical term.

## How this grade is taught

Structured written work with a claim, evidence, and a stated alternative. Students say what would have counted against their answer.

## How students show they have learned it

Written investigations, quarter quizzes and tests, four projects.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "If nothing surprised me, no expectation happened"

**Why it happens.** Surprise is the only visible evidence.

**How to fix it.** The chair demonstration and the break test.

**What to say.** "When prediction works you do not notice it."

### "Being wrong deletes the expectation immediately"

**Why it happens.** Students assume contradiction acts like an eraser.

**How to fix it.** The multi-break trial. The old expectation returns.

**What to say.** "Contradiction changes or pushes aside. It does not erase."

### "One trial is enough to know"

**Why it happens.** A single vivid result feels conclusive.

**How to fix it.** Pool class results and show the spread.

**What to say.** "One trial is a story. Twenty is evidence."


## Support and extension

**More support.** Run trials in pairs with a printed tally sheet.

**Extension.** Ask them to design a break that would be hard to recover from. Do not use extension to release later vocabulary.
