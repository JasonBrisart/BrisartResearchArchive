## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Choosing Between Explanations.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every student runs a break satisfying all four rules
- **Lesson 18:** every student names a cause for a failed break
- **Lesson 19:** every student re-runs and compares
- **Lesson 20:** every student has three genuine alternatives, not strawmen
- **Lesson 21:** every student names a separator for at least two of three
- **Lesson 22:** every student produces three with separators
- **Lesson 23:** every student has a six-part write-up with a genuine alternative
- **Lesson 24:** the class identifies the missing part in at least four

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | establish the pattern only once | Three repetitions minimum. Send it back otherwise. |
| 18 | hide the failed break | Ask for them publicly and treat them as the interesting cases. |
| 19 | change everything at once | Require one change at a time, named before the re-run. |
| 20 | write a deliberately weak alternative | Raise the standard: give me the one that would actually worry you. |
| 21 | name an observation both explanations predict | Test it aloud: if you saw that, which one is in trouble? |
| 22 | think it only applies here | Do the first one from another subject, aloud. |
| 23 | omit the separator as too hard | Accept a weak one over none: a weak separator is work, a blank is not. |
| 24 | skip the alternative | Return any investigation without one, unmarked. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
