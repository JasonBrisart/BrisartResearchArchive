## Entry check — before session one

**Field Stewardship assumes Faculty.** The participant produces institutional artifacts that pass blind execution.

- [ ] Produce a disclosure protocol executable by somebody else
- [ ] Supervise without transferring career risk to the student
- [ ] Enforce scope through procedure rather than presence
- [ ] Pass blind execution on disclosure and scope enforcement

If a box will not tick, return to `../25_Faculty/` and reteach that outcome before starting. Advancement follows demonstrated readiness, never the calendar.

## Exit outcomes

- Identify which field-level functions currently have no owner
- Establish version authority without claiming authority over the specification
- Specify a registry making prespecification verifiable and nulls recoverable
- Pass blind adoption on version authority and retirement

## How this grade is taught

Every instrument must be adoptable by a body that did not write it, tested by blind adoption rather than by reading.

## How students show they have learned it

Five governance instruments, blind adoption tests, a defended charter.

## Predicted misconceptions

### "Governance means control of the framework"

**Why it happens.** The instruments look like authority.

**How to fix it.** The body governs the examination, not the specification.

**What to say.** "This confers capability, not ownership."

### "Superseding a version means removing it"

**Why it happens.** Supersession sounds like replacement.

**How to fix it.** Erasing superseded versions destroys the citing literature's interpretability.

**What to say.** "Supersession is not deletion."

### "Standardising implementation improves comparability"

**Why it happens.** Uniformity looks like rigour.

**How to fix it.** A protocol dictating local implementation will not be adopted.

**What to say.** "A protocol nobody runs produces nothing."
