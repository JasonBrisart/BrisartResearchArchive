## The pivot lesson of this quarter

**Lesson 21 — Assumptions In Other Subjects.** If you set homework only once this
quarter, set it after this one. If you are short of time, cut anything before
cutting this.

## Do not move on until

- **Lesson 17:** every claim has two genuine assumptions
- **Lesson 18:** every student justifies a weakest assumption for three claims
- **Lesson 19:** every student receives an audit naming at least one per claim
- **Lesson 20:** every student names at least three method assumptions
- **Lesson 21:** every student audits three outside claims
- **Lesson 22:** every student states what survives for three claims
- **Lesson 23:** every student has a five-part claim, audited and repaired
- **Lesson 24:** the class finds at least four hidden assumptions

## Errors to expect this quarter

| Lesson | Students will | Correct it by |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | list restatements of the claim as assumptions | Test each: if that were false, would the claim fall? |
| 18 | rank by how obvious the assumption is | Ask what happens to the claim if each one fails. |
| 19 | return an audit saying the claims were fine | Require a minimum: every claim leans on something unstated. |
| 20 | treat method choices as neutral | Ask what breaks: if gaps were shorter than your interval, then what? |
| 21 | think it only applies here | Do the first one from another subject, aloud. |
| 22 | assume the whole claim always collapses | Show one where the claim narrows rather than falls. |
| 23 | submit a claim with no stated limits | Return it: what could your method not have seen? |
| 24 | list restatements as assumptions | Re-run the falsity test on every listed assumption. |

## Honest data rule

If a classroom activity does not produce the expected pattern, preserve the
result. Do not re-run it to get a tidier outcome, and do not change any student's
classification. Ask instead whether the method could have detected the pattern at
all, and record the answer. A result that does not fit is a result.
